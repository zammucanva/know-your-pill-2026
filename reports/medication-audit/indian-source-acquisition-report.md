## What the importer accepts and refuses

- Only a product's own package insert, prescribing information or patient-information leaflet (documentType). Safety alerts, news and advertisements are refused.
- Provenance is required: who supplied the document, or where and when it was obtained.
- Product, holder, formulation and the document's own revision date are required; the URL host must already be in the allowed list.
- Every passage must be found in the PDF's text (two reading orders tried); a scan without a text layer is refused.
- The stated medicine must be named in the document, and every strength in the formulation must appear in it.
- Numbers in a wording must be in its passage; the wording may not drop a negation or a condition from its passage, may not equal the passage, and may not claim a review.
- The same document cannot be stored under different metadata, and two different documents cannot claim the same product, holder, formulation and revision.
- An accepted import stores short passages and the document's SHA-256 only, marks every claim not-reviewed, and appends an audit-log entry (hashes and counts, no document text). A rejected attempt is logged too.

## How a document goes in

```
bun scripts/evidence/indian-import.ts spec.json
```

`spec.json` fields are listed at the top of `scripts/evidence/indian-import.ts` (slug, documentType, provenance, pdf, url, product, holder, revised, formulation, claims with exact passages and plain wording). Then run `bun test` and `bun run audit:four-mode`. Nothing imported is reviewed, and clinical review is a separate step (`docs/patient-explanation-content-workflow.md`).
