# Stimulant use disorders — amphetamine and cocaine

## 1. In one line + overview

**Tagline:** Stimulants borrow tomorrow's energy at crushing interest (days of borrowed drive followed by the **"crash" of flatness, sleep and depression**) and their treatment, unlike opioids, has **no magic maintenance medicine**: the treatment is structure, safety, psychiatric care of the crash-and-psychosis states, and rebuilding days that no longer need to be borrowed from.

**Overview, in plain words:** Amphetamine-type stimulants (ATS, including methamphetamine, "speed", diverted ADHD medicines, and party-time pills) and cocaine (powder, crack) push the brain's dopamine and noradrenaline systems to full volume: wakefulness, confidence, talkativeness, appetite gone, sex drive up, fatigue abolished. The catch is arithmetic: they spend the brain's stored currency, so the high is followed by a **rebound crash** (hypersomnia, hunger, flat mood, anhedonia and craving) that can last days to weeks, with chronic use thinning into a genuine paranoid-hallucinatory psychosis, weight collapse, dental decay (methamphetamine), nasal septum destruction (cocaine snorting) and cardiovascular catastrophes at any age. India's picture is a quiet ATS-and-cocaine growth layer in metros (nightlife, corporate and student worlds, and long-haul drivers on "stay-awake" tablets) over the older structural base; diverted prescription stimulants are the under-recognised stream. Management is honest about its limits: **no approved agonist maintenance** (the trial world of substitute stimulants, disulfiram-for-cocaine and agonist-like agents has shown only modest signal); the evidence lives in **contingency structures, behavioural activation, treating the comorbidity, and psychiatric emergency care** of stimulant psychosis, hyperthermia and chest pain.

## 2. Learning objectives

1. Describe the stimulant cycle: run–crash–craving, with the crash as the relapse engine.
2. Recognise and manage **stimulant psychosis** (paranoid-hallucinatory, formication) and distinguish it from schizophrenia.
3. Manage the acute medical emergencies: hyperthermia, agitation, chest pain, seizure, stroke.
4. Identify the routes and their local damages: snorting (nose), smoking (lungs, crack), injecting, rectal/vaginal.
5. Build treatment without a maintenance drug: contingency structures, behavioural activation, cue work, exercise, and comorbidity treatment.
6. Screen specifically for diverted ADHD medicines and "stay-awake" tablets among students, corporate workers and drivers.
7. Counsel the crash honestly ("depression that lifts by itself") to prevent antidepressant misuse and relapse-by-self-treatment.

## 4. Epidemiology

### Global
- Amphetamine-type stimulants are the **second-most used illicit drug class** worldwide after cannabis, with tens of millions of past-year users; methamphetamine dominates East Asia/Oceania and North America; cocaine remains a ~20-million-user belt centred in the Americas and Western Europe.
- Stimulants account for a growing share of drug-related Emergency presentations, psychiatric and cardiovascular at once.

### India
- Recorded ATS and cocaine use in India is a **small but growing metropolitan layer** (nightlife economics, corporate performance culture, nightlife-linked peer networks) plus the hidden functional stream: **students, software and corporate workers, and long-haul truck drivers using "stay-awake" tablets** (unregulated amphetamine-type and ephedrine-adjacent compounds); ask about it, because nobody volunteers it.
- Diversion of prescribed stimulants (methylphenidate-class) from ADHD treatment into performance use is a predictable growth area as paediatric ADHD diagnosis expands, prescription discipline protects both patients.
- Seizure data (the Narcotics Control Bureau lineage) shows rising methamphetamine trafficking routes through the north-east and metros, the leading indicator of street availability.

## 5. Causes & risk factors

- **Biology:** dopamine-transporter pharmacology that delivers effect-size reward beyond natural rewards (the overview note's thermostat logic applies, faster, higher, shorter half-life than cocaine for methamphetamine); performance-demand contexts creating "functional" initial use (drivers, students).
- **Psychological:** performance and confidence expectancies; body-image drive (appetite suppression); self-treatment of ADHD, social anxiety and atypical depressive-energy states; trauma-linked hypervigilance self-medicated by noradrenergic blunting.
- **Social:** nightlife and party economies; peer availability; long-haul shift economics; sexual-risk subcultures (chemsex-type patterns in metropolitan pockets; ask respectfully, it changes the care plan); weight-loss cultures for young women.
- **Course:** weekend-use → crash management with sedatives/alcohol (the polysubstance trap) → binge pattern → psychosis or cardiovascular event.

## 6. What happens in the body/brain

**Story 1: the overdraft account.** Stimulants force the brain to release its stored dopamine and noradrenaline *all at once*, spending next week's pay tonight. The run feels like the best version of oneself; the account is overdrawn by morning. The crash is the overdraft notice: sleep for a day and a half, eat everything, feel nothing, want more. Each cycle deepens the debt (receptor adaptation), and the person who "just needs to finish this project" ends up needing the drug to feel normal.

**Story 2: the alarm that never turns off.** Noradrenaline is the brain's alarm system. Stimulants leave it jammed on: pupils wide, heart racing, sweat, vigilance, and a mind that starts to find meaning in that alarm. With heavy chronic use, vigilance graduates into **paranoia** ("I am being followed"), then perception breaks: voices outside the window, shadow figures, and the classic **formication**, insects crawling under the skin (leading to skin-picking and the visible excoriation clue). This psychosis is dose-and-duration-driven and usually clears in **days to a week** of abstinence, but methamphetamine's persistence in the brain can stretch it to weeks, and the post-psychosis "flat grey period" is where schizophrenia gets misdiagnosed.

**Story 3: the sodium-channel heart.** Cocaine in particular blocks cardiac sodium channels on top of its vasospasm: the coronary artery clamps and the rhythm destabilises **at any age, with any dose**; a 28-year-old's chest pain in a party context is a myocardial infarction until proven otherwise. Add hyperthermia and agitation, and the medical and psychiatric emergencies arrive in the same patient.

## 7. Symptoms

**Intoxication ("the run"):** euphoria or blunted-affect-plus-drive, grandiosity, talkativeness, insomnia for days in binge patterns, tachycardia, hypertension, sweating, dilated pupils, hyperthermia, repetitive stereotyped activities ("punding", dismantling gadgets, sorting), bruxism, anorexia, hypersexuality, high-risk driving and sex.

**Withdrawal ("the crash"):** profound hypersomnia (or disturbed sleep-reversal), ravenous hunger, flat mood and anhedonia, irritability, vivid unpleasant dreams, strong craving, psychomotor slowing; anxiety can coexist with the flatness. Duration: several days to a few weeks; the anhedonia tail is the relapse engine.

**Stimulant psychosis:** paranoid delusions (persecutory, reference), visual and auditory hallucinations, formication, agitation; **clear consciousness** (unlike delirium) is a key discriminator; risk of harm to self or others during persecution-perception is real.

**Chronic harm inventory:** weight loss and malnutrition; dental decay (methamphetamine: dry mouth + bruxism + neglect); septal perforation and chronic sinusitis (snorting); "crack lung"/respiratory syndromes; skin excoriations and abscesses (injecting); HIV/HCV (injecting, chemsex routes); cardiovascular disease, stroke; movement abnormalities; and a cognition thinning (attention, working memory) that recovers slowly and partially.

## 8. Diagnosis

- The **severity-graded single-disorder logic** (see the overview note) applied to stimulants; binge pattern and crash pattern are the history anchors.
- **The direct questions nobody volunteers:** "stay-awake tablets for driving?", "study or performance pills?", "party drugs, pills, crystals, powder?"
- **Examination clues:** pupils, dental state, nasal septum, skin excoriations/picking, injection marks, cardiac rhythm, temperature, weight/BMI.
- **Psychosis differential (the viva favourite):** stimulant psychosis, onset with use, persecutory-dominant, formication, clears within days–weeks of abstinence, insight returns with the crash's resolution; schizophrenia: insidious onset, negative-symptom background, first-rank symptom pattern, no dose-timeline. **Do not diagnose schizophrenia in the grey weeks after stimulant abstinence, re-assess at 4–6 weeks** unless danger demands treatment now.
- **Cardiovascular workup** for chest pain/palpitations: ECG and troponin, vitals, temperature; the psychiatric unit must not hold a methamphetamine chest pain without medical clearance.
- **Urine screens:** amphetamine-class immunoassays detect ATS (with prescribed-medicine disclosure needed to interpret positives); cocaine metabolite detection is short-windowed (hours to ~1–3 days).

## 9. Management

### (a) Emergencies first
- **Agitation and psychosis:** low-stimulation room (the over-stimulated brain must not be restrained into more noradrenaline), benzodiazepines first-line (lorazepam-type, calm the alarm without adding dopamine blockade), antipsychotic (e.g. haloperidol/olanzapine-class) where benzodiazepines are insufficient; **with cardiac monitoring** (QT and the arrhythmia-prone heart), and treat hyperthermia actively (cooling, not just antipyretics). Physical restraint as a last-ditch safety measure only; it can worsen hyperthermia and rhabdomyolysis.
- **Chest pain and cardiovascular events:** standard ACS protocols, benzodiazepines for the sympathetically-driven component; avoid unopposed beta-blockade in cocaine chest pain (the classic exam pearl, theoretical coronary clamp-worsening; mixed guidelines, benzodiazepines first).
- **Hyperthermia, seizures, rhabdomyolysis:** active cooling, fluids, monitoring, medical co-management from the first hour.

### (b) Withdrawal: honest supportive care
No medical detoxification protocol exists as such: sleep, food, hydration, benzodiazepines briefly for agitation and sleep-reversal, and **watchful waiting through the crash**, its end (days to weeks) must be explained at intake: *"The depression that follows is the debt of the high: it lifts by itself; our job is to keep you safe while it does."* Antidepressants are not automatic: reserve for syndromal depression persisting beyond the grey period, or antecedent mood disorder.

### (c) The treatment core without a maintenance medicine
- **Contingency structures (the strongest evidence):** concrete rewards for verified abstinence; clinic privileges, family-administered incentives, employment-linked verification where occupations allow; the prize is attendance itself in resource-poor Indian adaptations.
- **Behavioural activation and exercise:** the crash's flatness is best combated by scheduled activation, physical exercise (natural dopamine), sunlight and duty; structure replaces the borrowed drive.
- **Cue work and stimulus control:** party networks, phone contacts, nightlife geographies, the exam-season panic, mapped and replaced like the alcohol cue-map.
- **Comorbidity treatment:** genuine ADHD (diagnose properly; treat with proper stimulant-adjacent prescriptions under contract, not punishment), social anxiety, atypical depression, trauma; treating the self-medicated condition is relapse prevention.
- **Community reinforcement + family contract:** as in the alcohol note; medicines held, money rules, relapse plan, protected relationship activity.
- **Pharmacotherapy honest table (exam-level knowledge):** no approved maintenance; modest signals for substitute-stimulant strategies, disulfiram and topiramate-class agents for cocaine, naltrexone-combination signals for amphetamine-methamphetamine co-use; mirtazapine-type agents for methamphetamine in some trials, all "modest, not established", in contrast to opioid agonist treatment. Do not prescribe loyalty to any of them.

*India note: the treatment budget is almost entirely human-time and family structure; the pharmacology bill is near zero; the scarce resource is the supervised, drug-free day structure; the best Indian approximations are employer-anchored contracts and family-run daily routines with telephonic follow-up (NASHA 14446/tele-MANAS linkage).*

### (d) Harm reduction (while use continues)
Never use alone; never mix with alcohol/sedatives for the "come-down" (the polysubstance trap and overdose mechanism); test the reaction dose; cardiac symptoms = hospital immediately; for injecting users, needle-syringe linkage; for nightlife users: hydration, avoid hyperthermic environments, and carry the "friend-of-a-friend" rule: someone unresponsive means ambulance now.

## 10. Indian practice

- **Screening where it hides:** the tired student with "study pills", the driver with "route tablets", the corporate employee with weekend binges, the gym-goer with "fat-burners" (ephedrine-adjacent), the nightlife-affiliated young adult, direct, non-judgmental questions behind fatigue, weight loss, dental decay and insomnia presentations.
- **Prescription discipline:** ADHD stimulant prescribing with one pharmacy, family-held medicines and quantity documentation, the diversion-prevention package.
- **The chemsex pocket:** in metropolitan sexual networks, stimulant-plus-sedative use with high-risk sex; ask respectfully in private; it changes HIV-prevention (PrEP linkage) and the whole relapse plan.
- **Psychosis pathway reality:** most stimulant psychosis in India reaches casualty, gets short haloperidol courses and discharge without addiction follow-up; the missed week is the treatment opportunity; every discharge should carry the crash-map and a 4–6 week re-assessment appointment (the misdiagnosis-prevention step).
- **Family teaching points:** the crash is an illness phase, not laziness; the paranoid accusations during use are chemical, not the person; and the single most protective family act is filling the calendar; the empty evening is the relapse address.

## 11. Clinical cases

**Case 1: the driver who could not stop using "route tablets".** A 34-year-old long-haul driver presented with insomnia, weight loss and "nerves"; behind those sat six years of stay-awake tablets, escalating from twice-weekly to daily, plus weekend alcohol "to come down". The run-crash cycle explained his presentation: runs of 3 sleepless days, crashes of 24-hour sleep at home, and a marriage fraying on both. Management: employer-anchored contract (route restructuring with co-driver; documented leave for treatment), crash-phase supportive care, behavioural activation programme (morning walks, fixed meals), family contract with wife (money, medicines, calendar), and 4-month telephonic follow-up with stimulant-negative verification at the employer's medicals. Two lapses (festival period; a deadline week), both mapped, both survived in care.

*Teaching points: functional-initiation streams (drivers, students) need direct screening; the crash cycle presented as "nerves and insomnia"; the treatment's spine was structure (employer, family, calendar) not pharmacology.*

**Case 2: the "schizophrenia" that cleared in ten days.** A 26-year-old IT professional was brought by friends after a week of progressively believing colleagues were monitoring him through his phone, then seeing figures at the window and "insects coming out of the keyboard"; he had been picking at his forearms. Casualty labelled first-episode psychosis; urine screen told the rest: a three-month methamphetamine binge from "work-pressure pills". Management: low-stimulation room, lorazepam, brief antipsychotic with cardiac monitoring, cooling for mild hyperthermia; psychosis resolved over ten days of abstinence; the flat grey period was explained in advance; no long-term antipsychotic was started; re-assessment at six weeks showed a recovered, embarrassed, motivated young man who entered contingency-anchored outpatient care with family supervision and employer leave documentation.

*Teaching points: formication and the dose-timeline are stimulant signatures; clear consciousness separates it from delirium; the 4–6 week re-assessment rule prevents the schizophrenia mislabel; and the "pills" began as performance self-treatment.*

## 13. FAQ

**"Are these party drugs really addictive?"**
Yes: the crash-and-craving cycle is the addiction, and the confidence borrowed in the run is repaid with interest. Weekend use becomes binge use faster than families expect, because the fatigue of Monday looks like a problem the drug "fixed".

**"He sleeps for two days after a binge. Why?"**
That is the crash, the brain's energy account overdrawn. It is expected, it passes, and it is the phase where we keep him safe and fed without moralising the sleep.

**"He says insects are coming out of his skin."**
That is formication, a stimulant perception error, not a delusion of infestation from another illness. It resolves with abstinence; the skin picking needs wound care meanwhile.

**"Is it schizophrenia?"**
The timeline decides: with recent stimulant use, psychosis is chemical until proven otherwise. Most cases clear within days to a few weeks clean. We re-assess in a month before using that word, and treat the danger now, not the label.

**"Are there medicines to treat this addiction?"**
Honestly, no approved maintenance like methadone for opioids. The evidence lives in structure, incentives, exercise, cue-management and treating the conditions the drug was self-treating. Family-anchored programmes work; quick-fix detox camps do not.

**"Can he take the ADHD medicine he was originally prescribed?"**
If genuine ADHD exists, properly diagnosed treatment under a contract (one pharmacy, family-held, documented) is legitimate and reduces street diversion, but a stimulant-use history demands stricter structure than usual.

**"He only uses it to stay awake for routes/exams."**
The functional start is the trap: tolerance builds, the crash lengthens, and the "tool" becomes the need. Route restructuring, co-driver rules, study-habits rebuilding, the alternative tools, are part of the medical plan, not moral advice.

**"Can it damage the heart at his age?"**
Yes: cocaine-type stimulants can cause heart attack and stroke at any age and any dose through vasospasm and rhythm effects. Chest pain, palpitations or collapse during use means hospital now.

## 14. Exam & student lens

- **Mnemonics:** the cycle as **"Run–Crash–Crave"**; the psychosis clues as **"Tin-foil paranoia, insects, clear sensorium"**; the emergency order as **"Room–Benzos–Cool–Watch QT"**.
- **Highest-yield facts:** formication; days-to-weeks psychosis clearance and the 4–6 week re-assessment rule; no unopposed beta-blockade in cocaine chest pain; hyperthermia management; methamphetamine's dental and neurotoxic signatures; the second-most-used class after cannabis globally.
- **Classic traps:** (1) diagnosing schizophrenia inside the grey weeks; (2) restraining the hyperthermic patient into more heat; (3) missing the "study pill/driver tablet" histories; (4) prescribing antidepressants reflexively for the crash; (5) forgetting the cardiovascular workup in psychiatric arrivals.
- **Viva favourites:** stimulant vs schizophreniform psychosis; punding/stereotypy; cocaine's sodium-channel story; contingency management as the evidence-based core; chemsex-linked dual risk; ADHD-diversion prescribing ethics.
- **Indian exam corner:** NCB seizure trends as leading indicators; long-haul driver tablet culture; nightlife metropolitan layer; prescription-stimulant diversion discipline; the "no detox camp" argument as a service-design answer.
- **Rehearse one line:** "In stimulant use, the run is borrowed and the crash is the bill; the treatment must make the days affordable without the loan."

## 16. Self-test MCQs

**1. Formication in stimulant psychosis refers to:**
A. Formal thought disorder
B. A sensation of insects crawling under/below the skin
C. Fear of open spaces
D. Retrograde amnesia

**Answer: B.** It drives skin-picking: a physical-exam clue to stimulant use.

**2. The best initial drug class for the agitated, hyperthermic stimulant patient is:**
A. Antipsychotics first, always
B. Benzodiazepines (plus cooling and cardiac monitoring)
C. Beta-blockers
D. Antihistamines

**Answer: B.** Calm the noradrenergic alarm first; antipsychotics are added when needed, under QT/vitals watch; unopposed beta-blockade is the classic cocaine-chest-pain trap.

**3. Stimulant psychosis, compared with schizophrenia, characteristically:**
A. Never involves hallucinations
B. Clears within days to weeks of abstinence, with persecutory content and formication
C. Begins insidiously over years
D. Requires lifelong antipsychotics from day one

**Answer: B.** Onset tied to use, clear consciousness, rapid clearance with abstinence, hence the 4–6 week re-assessment rule before the schizophrenia label.

**4. The crash phase of stimulant withdrawal:**
A. Is dangerous to life like alcohol withdrawal
B. Features hypersomnia, hunger, flat mood and craving; supportive care with watchful waiting
C. Requires naloxone
D. Lasts only minutes

**Answer: B.** Miserable but not lethal; its anhedonia tail is the relapse engine; antidepressants are not automatic.

**5. The strongest current evidence base for treating stimulant use disorder lies in:**
A. An approved maintenance agonist
B. Contingency-management and structured behavioural programmes
C. A vaccine
D. Long-term benzodiazepines

**Answer: B.** No approved maintenance exists; contingency structures plus activation, cue work and comorbidity treatment carry the evidence.

**6. A 28-year-old with chest pain after cocaine use:**
A. Should be reassured it is anxiety
B. Needs ECG, troponin and medical evaluation now, with benzodiazepines for the sympathetic component
C. Should receive propranolol alone immediately
D. Needs only an antacid

**Answer: B.** Cocaine chest pain is ACS until proven otherwise at any age; benzodiazepines first for the sympathetic load, avoid unopposed beta-blockade.
