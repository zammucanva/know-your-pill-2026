# Memory & Emotion — The Psychological Science

## 1. In one line + overview

**Memory is not one function but several dissociable systems, declarative (episodic and semantic) versus implicit (procedural, priming, conditioning) with different brain bases, whose cellular engine is LTP/LTD, and whose emotional gateways (the amygdala's fear conditioning and extinction circuitry) map directly onto PTSD, dementia and the amnesias.**

The Oxford chapters (2.5.3-2.5.4) deliver psychiatry's memory-emotion science. **The memory chapter (2.5.3)**: the systems taxonomy, by duration (**ultrashort-term** (sensory/echoic-iconic, milliseconds-seconds), **short-term** (seconds-minutes, capacity-limited (~seven items), rehearsal-fed; the working-memory workspace variant) and **long-term** (minutes-to-lifespan, not capacity-limited, structurally dependent)); by content (**explicit/declarative**: episodic (events with temporal-spatial-emotional context; familiarity versus recollection; déjà vu; false memories) and semantic (facts, context-independent, slowly stored in neocortex) versus **implicit/nondeclarative**, procedural skills, priming, conditioning and extinction); each with its neural system (medial temporal lobe-hippocampal formation for episodic; neocortex for semantic; striatum/cerebellum for procedural; neocortex for priming; **amygdala for fear conditioning**); the cellular mechanisms (**LTP**: high-frequency stimulation producing weeks-long synaptic enhancement through NMDA-calcium cascades, CREB and protein synthesis (early versus late phases, learning versus consolidation); **LTD**: the bidirectional counterpart; the Hebbian assembly logic); the declarative-memory anatomy (the H.M. lesion: complete anterograde amnesia with preserved working, procedural and priming memory; the Korsakov diencephalic system; the entorhinal-hippocampal pattern-separation-and-completion model; the recollection-hippocampus/familiarity-perirhinal division; left-prefrontal-encoding/right-prefrontal-retrieval asymmetry; neocortex as the engram store); the neuropsychology of assessment (the word-list batteries; the savings/forgetting-rate measures: Alzheimer's and Korsakov below 50% savings at minutes); and the implicit-memory clinical findings. **The emotion chapter (2.5.4)**: the amygdala's fear-conditioning role; extinction's active cingulate-amygdala circuitry (the depression-anxiety non-extinction connection).

## 4. The core ideas

### 4.1 The systems taxonomy

**By duration.** *Ultrashort-term* (sensoric; echoic/iconic): milliseconds-to-seconds modality-specific sensory retention (replaying the last second of conversation; the scene after closing the eyes). *Short-term*: seconds-to-minutes holding with rehearsal; **capacity-limited, about seven items** (the phone number from reading to dialling); the studied variant: **working memory**, holding task-relevant, environment-absent information in the mental workspace for manipulation and goal-directed behaviour (the psychiatric significance: the schizophrenia working-memory impairments; the prefrontal-parallel-link neurobiology). *Long-term*: minutes-to-lifespan, not clearly capacity-limited, dependent on enduring neuronal-structure changes, the engram question (where stored, how encoded, how retrieved).

**By content: the declarative/implicit divide.** *Explicit (declarative)*: consciously, intentionally retrievable; subdivided into **episodic** (events with temporal, spatial and emotional context, "what did you have for breakfast?"; the clinical features: familiarity-without-recollection (the déjà vu experience); **false memories** (people can be convinced to remember events that never happened, the laboratory paradigms and the recovered-memories note's territory); the recollection-familiarity double process) and **semantic** (facts and vocabulary, context-independent, slowly consolidated into neocortex over multiple exposures, retrievable without the MTL; the episodic system's rapid single-trial learning contrasting). *Implicit (nondeclarative)*: not consciously retrieved, **procedural memory** (sensorimotor, perceptual and cognitive skills by gradual repeated acquisition, striatum and cerebellum); **priming** (the facilitation of responses to previously encountered items, neocortical); **conditioning and extinction** (the amygdala's circuitry); the clinical warning: the declarative/implicit distinction concerns *conscious access*, not test-surface (a sentence-completion test can be priming-driven).

### 4.2 The cellular engine: LTP and LTD

**LTP (long-term potentiation)**: high-frequency presynaptic stimulation (the hippocampal CA1 model) producing a long-lasting postsynaptic responsiveness enhancement lasting weeks. The cascade: calcium influx through **NMDA** receptors (with AMPA and mGluR contributions) → cAMP and protein kinases (CaMKII) → gene-transcription-factor changes (**CREB**) → altered protein synthesis and phosphorylation, with the time-course distinction: **early-phase LTP** (seconds-minutes: the cellular signature of learning) and **late-phase LTP** (minutes-hours, protein-synthesis-dependent: the consolidation signature). **LTD**: the closely related opposite, lower presynaptic stimulation → less calcium influx → preferential calcineurin (phosphatase) activation → enduring synaptic depression, together giving **bidirectional modulation of synaptic strength** (formation and reversal of experience-dependent coupling). Hebb's assembly principle: changed synaptic efficacy across neural assemblies can, in principle, encode, store and retrieve information, the network-modelling bridge from molecules to mind.

### 4.3 The declarative architecture

**The hippocampal formation (HF)**: hippocampus proper + entorhinal cortex (the main cortical port of entry) + perirhinal and parahippocampal cortices (receiving from all neocortical areas, the bidirectional wiring with the whole brain); with the **prefrontal cortex** for encoding and retrieval organisation. **The H.M. lesson (1953)**: bilateral HF and amygdala resection for epilepsy → **complete enduring anterograde amnesia** (no new episodic memories) with partial retrograde amnesia (pre-surgery episodic memories unretrievable) (but **working memory, procedural memory and priming unimpaired**) the dissociation establishing the HF's episodic role and its non-involvement in implicit systems. The same pattern in **Wernicke-Korsakov syndrome** and diencephalic lesions (medial thalamus, mamillary body, fornix), the distributed episodic-encoding system. The rodent complement: the HF as a **neural map of the environment** (controversially extended to humans). Neuroimaging confirmation: hippocampal activation during successful encoding and retrieval.

**The computational model** (the pattern-separation/completion architecture): two segregated cortical streams (spatial and item information) converging in entorhinal cortex (integrating co-occurrence); the hippocampus proper rapidly coding the conjunction and **separating overlapping entorhinal patterns** (pattern separation, ensuring unambiguous retrieval and enormous storage capacity); retrieval by partial cues activating entorhinal fragments → associated hippocampal nodes → **pattern completion** reinstating the full representation with its features. **The schizophrenia simulation**: reduced inter-module connectivity → compromised item-context cross-association plus mild pattern-separation loss → patterns irretrievable (recognition mildly affected) and single-cue searching failing (free recall disproportionately impaired), "disproportionate retrieval failures due to compromised encoding", the information-processing reading of schizophrenia's memory deficit. The **recollection/familiarity division**: recollection predominantly hippocampal, familiarity linked to **perirhinal cortex**, supported by neuroimaging, with the lesion-evidence qualification (circumscribed hippocampal lesions also impair familiarity, the MTL as an integrated network). The **hemispheric asymmetry**: left prefrontal differentially encoding, right differentially retrieving (the hemisphere framework); the **engram store**: neocortex (the category-specific anomias (people, tools, living things) from circumscribed cortical lesions; the distributed-synaptic-connections assumption).

**The neurotransmitters**: glutamatergic transmission as the LTP-LTD substrate; **acetylcholine** modulating declarative memory (muscarinic blockade impairing episodic memory; the Meynert basal-nucleus degeneration of Alzheimer's: the cholinergic-mTL modulation story).

### 4.4 Assessment and the implicit systems

**Clinical assessment**: the episodic batteries (the Wechsler Memory Scale-III with its verbal-visual immediate and delayed indexes; the California and Hopkins Verbal Learning Tests; the Selective Reminding Test: with alternate forms reducing practice effects); the comprehensive evaluation principles: multiple word-list trials (the learning slope); immediate and delayed recall (30 minutes); **recognition testing** (minimising effortful retrieval); visual and verbal tests (the material-specific MTL laterality). The processing findings: **depth of encoding** (semantic judgements beating orthographic, the levels-of-processing effect); **familiarity versus recollection** as retrieval routes; **rate of forgetting** (savings at 80-90% for hours in normals; **below 50% after minutes in Alzheimer's and Korsakov's**, the accelerated-forgetting signature; increased forgetting also in a frontotemporal dementia variant). **The implicit systems clinically**: procedural memory, spared in deep MTL amnesia; **impaired in Huntington's and other basal-ganglia disease** (the double dissociation with episodic memory; the striatum's parallel loops integrating sensorimotor, cognitive and emotional information; the cerebellum's closed-loop and error-control role); **priming**: neocortical (reduced activation to primed stimuli, the "pruned" optimised assemblies); the prediction: **semantic priming impaired in Alzheimer's disease**; **fear conditioning**: the amygdala's subnuclei establishing and storing the CS-US association (of high psychiatric relevance across phobias, GAD and depression); **extinction** (now clearly an *active* process depending on **amygdala-cingulate interactions**, not passive fading) with the direct clinical reading: dysphoric mood and affect abnormally maintained (not extinguished) in depression and anxiety, the circuit-level account of persistent negative affect.

### 4.5 Emotion and memory: the integration

**Emotional memories**: hippocampal-amygdalar interactions (especially for fearful or aversive material), the amygdala modulating hippocampal consolidation (the catecholamine and stress-hormone mechanisms of the neuroendocrinology note); **extreme stress** producing simultaneously enhanced fear conditioning and impaired autobiographical memory (the PTSD pattern, and the recovered-memories note's amnesia speculations); the memory-psychiatry links: PTSD's intrusive emotional memories (the emotional-memory system overactive, the contextual system impaired, trauma without contextual binding); the amnesias and dementias (the episodic-encoding system failing); schizophrenia (the encoding-retrieval simulation; working memory); psychotherapy (remembered episodes as material; new habits and response patterns as procedural learning, declarative insight converting to implicit skill over time).

## 5. Why it matters clinically

- **The systems map is differential diagnosis**: episodic failure (MTL-diencephalic, Alzheimer's, Korsakov) versus procedural failure (striatal, Huntington's) versus working-memory failure (prefrontal, schizophrenia), the same "memory complaint" localising differently by system.
- **The forgetting-rate test is cheap and powerful**: savings below 50% at minutes separates the amnesic and dementing from the depressed and normal, a bedside-trackable metric.
- **The extinction insight reframes therapy**: exposure treatment is not forgetting but new cingulate-mediated learning overriding the amygdala's fear; explaining relapse patterns and the practice requirements.
- **The H.M. lesson protects clinical expectation-setting**: procedural learning survives pure episodic failure; the amnesic patient who cannot remember the therapy session can still learn the skill practised in it.
- **The false-memory science is forensic psychiatry's foundation**: laboratory implantation, suggestibility and source-confusion (the recovered-memories note), memory's reconstructive nature as expert-witness knowledge.
- **India**: the memory-clinic discipline (word lists, delay, recognition, deliverable without batteries), the Korsakov alcohol burden (thiamine-first, the amnesic note), and the dementia epidemic's assessment infrastructure all run on this note's science.

## 6. India lens

- **The bedside memory examination India can run**: three-word learning with distraction, 30-minute delayed recall, and a yes/no recognition trial, the system-level examination (episodic encoding, retention, retrieval discrimination) without any proprietary battery; the savings concept (how much was retained relative to immediate recall) as the quantitative layer.
- **The Korsakov pathway**: the thiamine-deficiency amnesia (the amnesic-syndromes note's clinical programme) remains a common Indian memory-clinic presentation, this note supplies its systems-level explanation (the diencephalic episodic-encoding failure with spared procedural learning, hence the value of routine-based rehabilitation).
- **The dementia expansion**: India's ageing demographics make the episodic/semantic/implicit dissociations daily clinical tools, the Alzheimer's pattern (episodic first, priming failing with disease progression, procedural relatively spared; the bath and prayer routines persisting after the names have gone); the family-education value of that knowledge.
- **The trauma-memory framing**: disaster, conflict and displacement populations present with the PTSD memory signature (intrusive affect-laden fragments without contextual binding); the systems-level explanation of why the traumatic memory is simultaneously unforgettable and fragmented; the exposure-therapy rationale (new extinction learning) expressed in plain Indian clinical language.
- **The seven-item and workspace teaching**: working-memory limits explain everyday clinic phenomena (instructions exceeding capacity; the two-step instruction rule for cognitive impairment; the schizophrenia conversation-tracking difficulty): practical cognitive communication.
- **The interpreter of complaints**: "memory weak" in an Indian OPD spans the systems, depression's retrieval failure (recognition intact), ageing's slowed encoding (savings preserved), MCI's beyond-normal forgetting (the MCI note), the dementias' consolidation failure (savings collapsed), this note's taxonomy plus the cognitive-assessment note's discipline as the interpreter.

**FAQ**
*"Is memory one thing or several?"*: Several: the systems differ by duration (seconds to lifelong), content (events and facts versus skills, priming and conditioning) and brain basis (hippocampal for events; striatal for habits; amygdalar for fears), which is why one disease can destroy memory for events while leaving skills and emotional learning intact.
*"What is working memory?"*: The mental workspace holding information for the task at hand when the information is not in front of you, limited to about seven items, the system whose failure makes schizophrenia's conversation-tracking and planning difficult.
*"Why can't she learn anything new but still plays the harmonium?"*: Because the systems are separate: H.M. could not form new episodic memories but acquired new skills, procedural learning (striatum-cerebellum) survives hippocampal failure; therapy for the amnesic patient builds on the same spared system.
*"What actually changes in the brain when we remember?"*: Synaptic strength: high-frequency use strengthens connections (LTP) and disuse-patterns weaken them (LTD); protein-synthesis-dependent changes that make some assemblies easier to reactivate than others; the memory is the changed ease of reactivation.
*"Why do traumatic memories behave so strangely?"*: Because the emotional and contextual systems can dissociate under extreme stress: the amygdala's fear learning strengthens while the hippocampal contextual binding weakens, leaving an unforgettable fragment without a context (the PTSD signature).
*"How does exposure therapy work if the fear memory is permanent?"*: By new learning, not erasure: extinction is an active cingulate-amygdala process that inhibits the fear response, which is why practice matters and relapse is possible: the original trace remains, overridden by new circuitry.
*"Is 'memory weak' always a memory disease?"*: No: depression impairs retrieval (recognition preserved), ageing slows encoding, attention failures masquerade as memory loss, the system examination (learning slope, delay, recognition, savings) sorts the mimics from the true amnesias.

**Self-test MCQs**

**Q1.** Patient H.M., after bilateral hippocampal and amygdala resection, demonstrated which pattern?
- (a) Global memory loss including skills
- (b) Complete anterograde episodic amnesia with preserved working memory, procedural memory and priming
- (c) Retrograde-only amnesia
- (d) No memory impairment

**Answer: (b).** The dissociation that established the systems: episodic encoding (hippocampal-diencephalic) lost; implicit systems and the workspace spared.

**Q2.** The synaptic mechanism of long-term potentiation begins with:
- (a) Calcium influx through NMDA receptors
- (b) Dopamine D2 blockade
- (c) Myelin thickening
- (d) Cell division

**Answer: (a).** The NMDA-calcium cascade → kinases → CREB → protein synthesis; late-phase LTP being the consolidation signature (minutes-to-hours, protein-synthesis-dependent).

**Q3.** In the entorhinal-hippocampal memory model, "pattern separation" refers to:
- (a) Spatial navigation only
- (b) The hippocampus's separating of overlapping entorhinal representations, ensuring unambiguous retrieval and expanding storage capacity
- (c) Sleep architecture
- (d) Emotional regulation

**Answer: (b).** With pattern completion (partial cues reinstating full representations) as the retrieval counterpart, and the reduced-connectivity schizophrenia simulation deriving its "retrieval failures from encoding failure" conclusion from the same architecture.

**Q4.** Which double dissociation links procedural memory to its neural system?
- (a) Procedural memory spared in hippocampal amnesia; impaired in Huntington's/basal-ganglia disease
- (b) Procedural memory impaired by amygdala lesions
- (c) Procedural memory is cerebellar-only
- (d) No dissociation exists

**Answer: (a).** The striatum-cerebellum system for skills versus the MTL-diencephalic system for episodes, the mirror-image patient profiles.

**Q5.** Extinction of conditioned fear, as currently understood:
- (a) Is passive decay of the fear memory
- (b) Is an active process involving amygdala-cingulate interactions, new learning overriding rather than erasing the original trace
- (c) Requires hippocampal damage
- (d) Is permanent once achieved

**Answer: (b).** The clinical corollaries: exposure therapy as active new learning (practice, relapse possibility); the depression-anxiety non-extinguishing dysphoria as circuit-level persistence.

**Q6.** Savings (retention relative to initial learning) in normal individuals versus Alzheimer's and Korsakov's patients:
- (a) Identical in all groups
- (b) 80-90% for hours in normals; below 50% after minutes in Alzheimer's and Korsakov's
- (c) 100% in all
- (d) Zero in normals

**Answer: (b).** The accelerated-forgetting signature: the cheap, powerful discriminator the bedside examination can track.
