# Prion Diseases (CJD and Relatives)

## 1. In one line + overview

**Prion disease is the fastest-moving dementia psychiatry meets, a rare, always-fatal brain illness in which a misfolded protein recruits the mind's own proteins into its wrong shape, typically killing within about a year: the classic picture is a person in their sixties declining from weeks to months, with jerks (myoclonus), a peculiar EEG, and a brain MRI that lights up like a ribbon, but the practical psychiatry of prion disease is mostly the discipline of *ruling out the treatable mimics* before accepting this untreatable diagnosis.**

Three facts organise everything else. First, prions are infectious proteins with no DNA or RNA (a shape, not a creature) which is why they resist the sterilisation and immune logic of ordinary germs. Second, almost every case is either spontaneous (sporadic CJD, roughly 85 per cent), inherited (a known gene mutation in about 10–15 per cent), or, rarely, acquired through tissue exposure (historical growth-hormone and dura-mater graft cases, beef-borne variant CJD, and the extinct cannibalism-transmitted kuru). Third, there is no disease-slowing treatment; management is accurate diagnosis, comfort, honest counsel, and care of the family. The psychiatric relevance is double: variant CJD *presents* to psychiatry (young patients with depression, anxiety and personality change first), and every "rapidly progressive dementia" arriving at a psychiatric clinic obliges a workup for the autoimmune, infective and metabolic illnesses that imitate CJD and are treatable; a lookalike rescued is worth more than a prion diagnosis made.

## 2. Learning objectives

After this note you can:
- State the prion concept in three plain sentences (shape change, template seeding, spongiform damage) and the three transmission routes (sporadic, genetic, acquired)
- Recognise the sporadic CJD picture: subacute cognitive collapse over weeks-to-months plus myoclonus plus neurological signs, and its three investigations (MRI ribbon, EEG periodic complexes, CSF markers including RT-QuIC)
- Distinguish variant CJD from sporadic: younger age, psychiatric-first onset, sensory complaints, ataxia before dementia, the MRI pulvinar sign, and the tonsil-biopsy history
- Name the rarer phenotypes in one line each: fatal familial insomnia (the thalamus-and-sleep disease), Gerstmann–Sträussler–Scheinker (ataxia-first), and kuru (the historical laughter-and-tremor epidemic that won a Nobel prize)
- Run the mimic workup of any rapid dementia, the treatable list (autoimmune/anti-NMDA and other antibody encephalitides, Hashimoto's encephalopathy, paraneoplastic, neurosyphilis, HIV, metabolic and toxic, subdural, NPH), and know the rule: treat the mimic before settling the prion
- Counsel families honestly: trajectory, inheritance testing for blood relatives, infection-control realities (what is and is not contagious), and end-of-life care
- Describe Indian realities: under-recognition, where RT-QuIC and specialist MRI access actually exist, costs (approx 2026), and the referral pathway

## 4. Epidemiology

**Global.** Sporadic CJD occurs worldwide at roughly 1–1.5 cases per million people per year, peaking between 60 and 70 years of age, with no robust sex difference and no convincingly demonstrated environmental cause. Familial forms cluster by mutation and ancestry (the E200K mutation famous among Libyan Jews and Slovak clusters; D178N for fatal familial insomnia; P102L for GSS). Iatrogenic CJD is a closed historical ledger, cadaveric growth-hormone treatment and dura-mater grafts before the 1990s accounting for most recorded transmissions, with a handful from corneal grafts and reused neurosurgical instruments. Variant CJD struck mainly the United Kingdom, about 230 cases in total across the world, beginning 1996, in people exposed to BSE-contaminated beef in the late 1980s; the epidemic has essentially burned out, with the late lesson that incubation periods can be decades and a few blood-transmission cases in the tail.

**India.** No systematic national surveillance (no Indian CJD registry equivalent to the EU's networks), so figures are estimates built from hospital series: the incidence is almost certainly under-recognised rather than truly lower, since a rapidly declining elder is frequently labelled "stroke," "senility," or "advanced Alzheimer's" and dies without MRI or CSF study. Case reports and series accumulate from the national institutes and medical colleges (NIMHANS, AIIMS, PGI lines); RT-QuIC is available at a small number of centres (approx 2026). For the clinician the working assumption is: it exists in your district, you will meet it rarely, and you will meet its *mimics* far more often, which is the practical epidemiology.

## 5. Causes & risk factors

- **Sporadic** (sporadic CJD, ~85%): a chance misfolding event or a spontaneous somatic PRNP conversion, no behaviour, no diet, no contact is known to raise the risk (the usual family questions, "was it the mutton, was it the vaccine?": have, for sporadic disease, the answer no)
- **Genetic / familial** (~10–15%): inherited PRNP mutations, usually autosomal dominant with variable penetrance, familial CJD, GSS (P102L and neighbours), fatal familial insomnia (D178N with methionine at codon 129, in the classic double-mutant logic), and the codon-129 polymorphism (M/V) shaping phenotype in both sporadic and variant disease
- **Acquired** (rare): iatrogenic tissue exposure, historical cadaveric growth hormone, dura-mater grafts (the Japan series notable), corneal and instrument-linked transmission; variant CJD via BSE-infected beef exposure (UK-era); kuru via endocannibalism (fore people of Papua New Guinea, extinct)
- **Age** (sporadic form peaks 60–70; variant struck the young: median early twenties at the UK peak)
- **Indian context**: no identified local risk factor of any kind; the genuine risks here are *diagnostic*; the missing MRI, the unexamined mimic, the label of "rapid Alzheimer's" given without workup

## 6. What happens in the brain: two short stories

**Story one: the shape that recruits.** A normal brain protein (the prion protein, sitting on cell membranes, day-job still not fully understood) flips into a wrong shape. The wrong shape is sticky and stable, and it is a *template*: wherever it meets a normally-shaped prion protein, it presses it into its own image. One becomes two, two become four, and the conversion runs exponentially like a crystal seeding a supersaturated solution, the "ice-nine" analogy. Because the misfolded form resists the enzymes that clear normal proteins, it accumulates; the neurons hosting it sicken and die, leaving spongy holes (spongiform change) and dense scarring (gliosis). No virus, no bacterium, no immune war, a shape, spreading. This is why prions resist standard autoclaving and why no vaccine has ever been possible: there is nothing foreign to teach the immune system to see, the enemy wears the body's own protein.

**Story two, where it lands decides the script.** The misfolding avalanche lands differently by region, and the region writes the phenotype. Cortex-heavy spread presents as the fastest dementias. Basal ganglia and thalamus add movement chaos and myoclonus. A thalamus-confined familial mutation (the D178N/FFI lineage) attacks the sleep-relay hub first, producing the terrifying phenotype of fatal insomnia: months of dream-consciousness intruding into waking, autonomic storms, and exhaustion without sleep, in families where the same death has visited before. Cerebellum-first (GSS, some sporadic variants) presents as ataxia years before dementia. The lesson for the clinician: prion disease is not one disease but a family of scripts, with the same ending.

## 7. Symptoms

**Sporadic CJD, the classic script:**
- Cognitive collapse over weeks to a few months: forgetfulness on Monday, disorientation by the month, mute and bed-bound by the season; this SPEED is the signature (a dementia that declares itself fully between two consecutive OPD visits)
- Myoclonus: sudden lightning jerks of limbs or face, worsened by startle, present in most, at times the family's first alarming sign
- Neurological quicksand under the dementia: cerebellar ataxia, stiff-rigid pyramidal and extrapyramidal signs, visual field distortion and cortical blindness variants, aphasia-as-first-sign variants (the Heidenhain-style visual phenotypes a specialty of exams)
- Behavioural and psychiatric shading: irritability, withdrawal, depression and sleep inversion early on; the reason psychiatry sees these patients before neurology does
- Terminal phase: mutism, rigidity, swallowing failure, unresponsiveness, death typically within a year of onset (median survival months)

**Variant CJD: the different script (the psychiatrist's version):**
- Young patients (teens–forties at the UK peak), presenting first with psychiatric symptoms (anxiety, depression, withdrawal, behavioural change) typically months BEFORE any clear neurological sign, generating repeated psychiatric contacts before the diagnosis declares itself
- Prominent painful sensory symptoms (persistent limb and face pain that finds no anatomy)
- Then progressive ataxia, dance-like movements (chorea/dystonia), cognitive decline; later the same mutism and myoclonus

**The rarer phenotypes, one line each:**
- **Fatal familial insomnia**: adult-onset untreatable insomnia with dream-filled waking, autonomic storms (sweats, fever, pulse swings), and attention collapse over roughly a year-plus, familial, dominant, and the sleep-medicine nightmare
- **Gerstmann–Sträussler–Scheinker**: ataxia-first family disease running years, dementia late
- **Kuru**: the historical tremor-and-laughing disease of the fore people, spread by funeral cannibalism, studied by Gajdusek (Nobel 1976), the proof that human-to-human transmission of a "slow virus" (later prion, Prusiner's Nobel 1997) could cause a brain disease decades after exposure

## 8. Diagnosis

**The logic in plain words.** No single bedside feature proves prion disease; the diagnosis is a converging triangle of (1) the tempo, genuinely rapid progressive dementia-plus, (2) the investigations below, and (3) the exclusion of mimics. The modern criteria weight MRI and the RT-QuIC assay heavily; the old EEG-only era is gone.

**Investigations:**
- **MRI brain (DWI/FLAIR), the workhorse**: bright ribbon-like cortical signal ("cortical ribboning") plus basal-ganglia brightness; in variant CJD the pulvinar sign (bright posterior thalamus, the "hockey-stick" pattern); a well-performed MRI carries the single greatest diagnostic weight in India, where CSF markers are thinly available
- **EEG**: periodic sharp-wave complexes; the classic exam answer, present in a majority of late sporadic cases but neither early nor specific
- **CSF**: the older 14-3-3 protein and total-tau markers (sensitive but non-specific, raised in any rapid neuronal death); the modern **RT-QuIC** (real-time quaking-induced conversion: the patient's CSF is used to seed a shaking tube of normal prion protein, and if it converts, light shows, highly specific, the closest thing to a living confirmation); availability in India limited to major centres (approx 2026)
- **Genetic testing** (PRNP sequencing) for the familial phenotypes and for counselling blood relatives, the FFI/GSS families in which a positive test rewrites the family's future
- **Tonsil biopsy** in suspected variant CJD (the misfolded protein appears in lymphoid tissue there, unlike sporadic), now largely historical as the epidemic ended

**The differential: the treatable mimics that matter more than the prion (a rapid-dementia workup table):**

| Mimic | The clue that breaks the disguise | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Autoimmune encephalitis (anti-NMDA and cousins) | Young, seizures/psychosis-first, movement disorder, ovarian teratoma (young women) | Steroids, IVIG, plasma exchange, tumour removal: recovery possible |
| Hashimoto's encephalopathy | Myoclonus + rapid cognitive decline with thyroid antibodies and steroid-responsiveness | Steroids: dramatic response |
| Paraneoplastic limbic encephalitis | Subacute amnesia/seizures, known or hidden cancer screen | Oncologic + immunotherapy |
| Neurosyphilis / HIV / rare viral encephalitides | Risk history, serology, CSF studies; HIV's own dementia is slower but the opportunistic mimics are fast | Antimicrobial/antiretroviral |
| Vascular (multi-infarct, CADASIL) | Stepwise not smooth, imaging | Secondary prevention |
| Chronic subdural / NPH | Gait-first NPH; head injury history for subdural; imaging | Surgical: reversible |
| Toxic/metabolic (B12, thyroid, heavy metal, drug) | Screenable bloodwork | Replace/withdraw |
| Rapid Alzheimer's / DLB decline | Slower on close inspection; parkinsonism for DLB | Supportive |

The governing rule, worth saying aloud in viva and ward: **a rapid dementia gets MRI + CSF + the mimic screen BEFORE it gets a CJD label**, because at least one in five real-world "CJD-suspects" in published series turns out to be something treatable, and the anti-NMDA and Hashimoto's rescues are the happiest endings this differential owns.

## 9. Management

**There is no disease-modifying treatment**; flortaucipir-style hope does not exist here; trials of antiprion agents (pentosan, quinacrine, PRN-100 / doxycycline lines) have failed or remain unproven. The honest management is:

- **Confirm what can be confirmed** (MRI ± CSF at the referral centre), and **close the mimic workup first**; the treatable differential must be actively eliminated, not assumed away
- **Symptom comfort**: myoclonus, clonazepam, valproate or levetiracetam; spasticity: gentle positioning and physiotherapy; insomnia and agitation: cautious benzodiazepine or carefully chosen sedation (the FFI families' sleep battle); pain and distress: morphine-class analgesia in the late phase is not controversial, it is the standard
- **Nutrition and skin care** as swallowing fails: speech-swallow guidance, cup-thick feeds, ultimately honest family decisions about feeding (prion services frame the late-phase feeding conversation early, without euphemism)
- **Family care, the core of the enterprise**: honest trajectory counselling (months, not years); the caregiver crash-course this rapid disease forces; psychological support for relatives; genetic counselling where a familial form is identified (the at-risk relative's decision about predictive PRNP testing is a full psychiatric consultation in itself; most at present decline testing, an existing family death having taught them more than a genotype could)
- **Infection control, precise, not panicked**: the patient is NOT contagious by ordinary contact (touch, sharing rooms, saliva, sexual contact); special handling applies only to CSF/brain/spinal-cord and lymphoid tissue; neurosurgery and autopsy instruments need prion-specific sterilisation; standard precautions plus tissue-discipline are the whole story; blood-donation deferral rules in India are variant-era policies (the UK-exposure-era deferrals)
- *India note*: care is usually home-based (the rapid trajectory makes prolonged institutional care pointless in most families' calculus, and honest families prefer the home); the district team's role is comfort logistics, morphine access and family support; the specialist centre's role is diagnostic closure (MRI/RT-QuIC, genetic counselling).

## 10. Indian practice

The practical pathway: a rapidly dementing patient reaches a physician or psychiatrist; the MRI (DWI sequences specifically: ask for them by name, because routine sequences miss the ribbon) plus the mimic screen happens at the nearest medical college; true suspects travel to the national centres (NIMHANS, AIIMS, PGI tier) where advanced MRI reading and RT-QuIC-class assays live. Costs (approx 2026): MRI brain ₹3,000–8,000 government to ₹8,000–15,000 private; CSF 14-3-3/RT-QuIC, where available, a few thousand rupees and often research-subsidised; EEG ₹1,000–3,000; genetic counselling and PRNP sequencing largely research-tier (priced ₹15,000–40,000 in private laboratories, if sourced). Certification of death is as organic brain disease; the family's questions about "was it contagious to the children" deserve the clear answer above (no). The home-care arc (a hospital bed rented in the hall, the morphine the family doctor agreed to start, the grandmother turned every two hours, the priest called a month before anyone expected) is the standard Indian prion-service, delivered by families, unsupported in most districts, and worth acknowledging in any lecture that calls itself Indian psychiatry.

## 11. Clinical cases

**Case 1: the decline called depression.** A 63-year-old retired railway clerk in Nagpur: four weeks of "he's become silent, sits facing the wall" after his brother's death. Treated as bereavement-depression; two weeks later he is found switching on the stove at 3 a.m., misrecognising his wife, and a startle at the door sends both arms flying. On examination: profound attention collapse, bilateral myoclonus on tap; MRI (DWI asked for by name) shows cortical ribboning and bright basal ganglia; CSF at the referral centre seals it. The family is counselled on a Thursday; he dies eleven weeks later at home; the relatives who asked "should we all get tested" receive the sporadic-form answer (no, nothing to inherit), and the two sons who spent nights on the floor-bed by his side are screened by the family doctor for their own emerging depression. Teaching points: grief and depression are the default first labels; the *tempo* is the tell; myoclonus plus rapid cognitive fall is a same-day MRI; "ask for DWI" is a clinical skill, not a radiologist's property.

**Case 2: the mimic that got rescued.** A 21-year-old engineering student in Ernakulam: six weeks of insomnia, panic-like spells, then disorientation and episodes where she "barks words that aren't hers"; an outside label of acute psychosis; a family already arranging a faith-healing ceremony. The admitting team holds the line: young rapid cognitive-plus-psychiatric decline is a medical workup, not a referral to the temple: MRI shows normal-to-minimal change, CSF and the autoimmune panel return anti-NMDA receptor antibody positive; pelvic imaging finds the ovarian teratoma. Steroids, tumour removal, rehabilitation: eight months later she re-enrols. Teaching points: this is the illness prion disease masquerades as at this age, and vice versa; the anti-NMDA rescue is why the CJD label must be *earned* by exclusion; the same discipline that protects the young protects the old, and "psychosis of new onset with any cognitive flag" is a lumbar-puncture-and-antibody situation.

## 13. FAQ

- *"Can we catch it from him, sharing his plate, holding his hand?"*: No. Ordinary contact (touch, plates, rooms, tears, kissing) transmits nothing. The special precautions apply only to brain, spinal and related tissues during procedures; your family's daily life is safe exactly as it is.
- *"Was it something he ate, the mutton, the fast food?"*: In the sporadic form, which is most cases: no food, no habit, nothing known. The beef link belongs to a different form (variant CJD) from a specific UK-era exposure; Indian beef-eating today carries no identified prion risk.
- *"Is it hereditary, should our children be tested?"*: In the sporadic form, no. In the rare familial forms, the question deserves a dedicated genetic-counselling session, because a positive test in an adult-without-symptoms currently offers no prevention; knowing what would help a relative decide is itself the counselling.
- *"Is there any tablet that slows it?"*: Honestly, none exists; anything claimed otherwise is a research story, not a prescription. What we can do, and will do, is keep him comfortable, treat the jerks and the distress, and stand with the family.
- *"The doctor said 'periodic complexes' on the EEG, is that the diagnosis?"*: It is strong supporting evidence, not a verdict by itself; the MRI and the special CSF tests carry more weight today, and the treatable lookalikes are still checked first.
- *"Why does he jump when the door slams?"*: The startle reflex has lost its gate; the jerks are the same disease seen from the muscle side; we quieten them with medicine, but they are not seizures of the epilepsy kind.
- *"How long do we have, doctor, honestly?"*: Most people with the common form are lost within about a year, often less, from the first clear decline; planning the home care and the goodbyes sooner rather than later is the kindness, and the morphine question can be answered before the suffering forces it.
- *"He died of it, can the autopsy be done for the family's answers?"*: It can, at centres equipped for it, and it confirms, with strict instrument rules; for most families the imaging-and-CSF diagnosis during life has already closed the question, and the funeral rites carry no prion risk at all.

## 14. Exam & student lens

- **High-yield triad**: rapidly progressive dementia + myoclonus + periodic EEG sharp-wave complexes, always completes the stem
- **Investigation ladder**: DWI/FLAIR MRI (ribbon, basal ganglia; pulvinar in variant) > RT-QuIC (specific) > 14-3-3/total tau (sensitive, non-specific) > EEG (classic, late)
- **Sporadic vs variant CJD table**, the perennial short-note: age (60s vs young), onset (cognitive vs psychiatric-first with painful sensory symptoms), duration (months vs 14-ish months), MRI (ribbon vs pulvinar/hockey-stick), tonsil biopsy (positive in variant), exposure (none vs BSE-era beef)
- **One-liners to bank**: FFI = D178N thalamus, insomnia plus autonomic storms; GSS = P102L, ataxia-first, years; kuru = fore tribe, funeral cannibalism, Gajdusek; iatrogenic = growth hormone, dura grafts; Prusiner = prion protein Nobel 1997; PrP has no nucleic acid
- **The trap questions**: "14-3-3 positivity equals CJD", false (any rapid neuronal death raises it); "autopsy refused, diagnosis impossible": false (RT-QuIC/MRI era); "myoclonus means CJD"; false (myoclonus in delirium, metabolic states and dementia syndromes is commoner than prion disease a hundred-fold); "the EEG finding is the earliest test", false
- **Indian exam corner**: viva favourite, "a 62-year-old with a 3-month decline and jerks, your steps, in order", answer: tempo recognition, DWI-MRI, mimic screen (the autoimmune/metabolic/infective panel), CSF markers, comfort care and family counselling, and note the anti-NMDA rescue as the system's moral.

## 16. Self-test MCQs

**1. The single most differentiating clinical feature of sporadic CJD among the dementias:**
A. Age above 70
B. Tempo: a dementia that completes its decline over weeks-to-months
C. Early urinary incontinence
D. Family history in 85%
**Answer: B.** Speed is the signature; Alzheimer's measures in years, prion disease between two appointments.

**2. A 25-year-old with three months of depression-like withdrawal, limb pains, then stumbling gait and cognitive slips. Among these, the diagnosis that MUST be actively excluded before any psychiatric label settles:**
A. Variant CJD (psychiatric-first, young, sensory pains) but via the full mimic workup, especially treatable encephalitides
B. Adjustment disorder
C. Adult ADHD
D. Simple schizophrenia
**Answer: A.** The script matches variant CJD's opening; but the workup discipline is the same: MRI plus CSF and the antibody panel (anti-NMDA lives at this age too).

**3. RT-QuIC earns its diagnostic status because it is:**
A. A rapid bedside EEG technique
B. A CSF seeding assay (the patient's misfolded protein converts a normal substrate in the tube) highly specific to prion disease
C. A specific MRI sequence
D. A gene test for PRNP
**Answer: B.** The test that reproduces the disease's own mechanism in glass; specificity is its selling-point against the non-specific 14-3-3.

**4. Family sharing meals, beds and tears with the patient at home. Your infection-control instruction:**
A. Isolation room, mask and separate utensils
B. Ordinary living is safe; special tissue-handling rules apply only to brain/spinal/CSF contact in procedures
C. No kissing, only hand-holding
D. The children should be tested monthly for a year
**Answer: B.** No social transmission route exists; the panic (and the utensil separation that Indian households sometimes self-impose) is the harm to prevent.

**5. In fatal familial insomnia the defining early features are:**
A. Seizures and stroke-like episodes
B. Untreatable insomnia with dream-like intrusions into waking plus autonomic storms, the D178N thalamic lineage
C. Myoclonus followed by blindness
D. Personality change and laughter fits
**Answer: B.** The sleep-relay hub is the target; the phenotype that taught sleep medicine what the thalamus is for; laughter belongs to kuru's description, not FFI.

**6. Among published rapid-progression "CJD suspect" series, roughly one in five proved to have:**
A. Lewy body dementia
B. A treatable mimic (autoimmune encephalitis, Hashimoto's, metabolic, infective), hence the exclusion-before-label rule
C. Normal pressure hydrocephalus only
D. Epilepsy only
**Answer: B.** The moral arithmetic of this whole note; the rescue rate justifies the entire mimic workup.
