# ADHD — Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder

## 1. In one line + overview

**ADHD is a neurodevelopmental condition where the brain's regulation of attention, impulse control and activity level runs immature, like a powerful engine with late-developing brakes, producing a child (or adult) who is bright, often wonderfully energetic, and yet loses notebooks, blurts answers, cannot sit through a period, makes careless mistakes, and reaps daily punishment for a brain state rather than a character flaw.**

The core trouble is not a lack of intelligence or effort. It is REGULATION, the brain's prefrontal traffic system matures slowly and noisily, so the child's attention locks onto what is NOVEL or rewarding and slides off what is boring-but-important; impulses fire before the consequences arrive; and the body fidgets when it is told to be still. Teachers call it naughtiness, relatives call it bad upbringing, and the child calls it "I always get scolded for the same thing even when I tried"; by age nine most have a settled self-image of being "bad at studies" or "the trouble child". ADHD shows in at least two settings (school AND home), is present before 12, and runs a course far beyond childhood: a majority of diagnosed children still meet criteria or carry meaningful symptoms into adulthood, where it appears as deadline chaos, careless errors, impulsive spending, driving scrapes, emotional volatility and fatigue from masking. It is one of psychiatry's most treatable conditions: behavioural structure plus, where appropriate, stimulant medication produces some of the largest short-term effect sizes in child mental health, while the untreated picture quietly costs years of education, self-esteem and accident risk.

## 2. Learning objectives

After this note you can:
- Define ADHD's three presentations and the "two-settings, before-12, impairment" logic
- Explain the brakes-and-engine story, the dopamine-noradrenaline signalling story, and the maturation-lag story in plain words
- Run a proper diagnostic workup: multi-source history (parent + teacher), rating scales (named), exclusion of mimics, sleep, hearing, absence seizures, anxiety, trauma, chaotic environments
- Understand stimulant pharmacology the way prescribers need it: methylphenidate's mechanism, dosing, monitoring, and the atomoxetine/lisdexamfetamine alternatives with their distinct logic
- Deliver parent training and school accommodations concretely, the behavioural half of treatment
- Manage adult ADHD: diagnosis inherited from the child literature, vocational/living structure, medication continuation rules
- Handle Indian realities: Schedule-X prescription discipline, hostel diversion, coaching-city misuse, the punishment-first culture, costs
- Separate evidence from myth: sugar, screens, parenting, and the honest answer on growth, cardiovascular risk and addiction
- Screen the FAMILY: ADHD is strongly inherited, and the child's diagnosis is often the parent's first clue about their own 40 years

## 4. Epidemiology

**Global.** Meta-analytic childhood prevalence around 5% (DSM-5 estimates ~5% children; ~2.5% adults); a substantial pool of "sub-threshold" impairment sits below the line. Boys are diagnosed 2–3:1 in childhood (combined presentation dominates), while adult identification approaches parity; girls' quieter inattentive presentations (dreamy, disorganised, "slow worker") are chronically missed at school age. Onset criteria require symptoms before 12. Course: roughly two-thirds of children retain some impairing symptoms into late adolescence; about one-third to half meet full criteria as adults; the modern framing is "symptoms mature, consequences adultify". Untreated ADHD raises accident rates, school dropout, substance-use initiation, teenage pregnancy and employment instability, the epidemiological case for treating it.

**India.** School-based studies report ADHD around 1–8% depending on instrument and cutoff, with the same detection gap for girls and inattentive presentations. The Indian story is shaped by structure: classrooms of 50 with zero tolerance for movement, tuition culture multiplying homework and stationary time (a mismatch engine for hyperactive children), corporal punishment shifting the narrative from "brain state" to "character defect", and parents presenting only after school ultimatums. Meanwhile India has evolved a distinctive 2020s problem: stimulant-seeking among NEET/JEE coaching students and young professionals in Kota and metros, both diversion (sharing of prescribed tablets) and symptom-feigning for prescriptions ("I can't focus" as entry ticket), which makes diagnostic rigour a public-health duty, not just good practice. Methylphenidate and atomoxetine are well-established; lisdexamfetamine has recently entered the Indian market.

## 5. Causes & risk factors

**Genetic (the strongest single layer)**
- Heritability ~70–80%, among the most heritable psychiatric conditions; adoptee studies converge: children resemble their biological, not adoptive, parents
- Sibling risk several-fold elevated; having a parent with ADHD is the most useful risk marker clinically, always screen the parents
- Dopamine-pathway gene variants (DRD4, DAT1 lineages) are the classic association candidates, small effects each, no clinical testing value

**Neurodevelopmental / biological**
- Prematurity, low birth weight, prenatal alcohol/tobacco exposure, lead exposure, reliable moderate risks
- Brain story: prefrontal–striatal–cerebellar maturation delay (~2–3 years on average in imaging studies); atypical dopamine/noradrenaline signalling reduces "boring-signal" amplification
- Executive functions (working memory, inhibition, planning) are the measured casualties; overall IQ is normal-distributed: ADHD exists at every IQ level

**Psychological / environmental (modulators, not origins)**
- Chaotic households, inconsistent discipline, and high screen-diets do not CAUSE ADHD but make the same wiring look catastrophically worse (and treating structure improves function without touching the wiring)
- Sleep loss (including sleep apnoea, late mobile gaming) manufactures an ADHD-identical state, always audit sleep before diagnosing
- Severe early adversity can produce dysregulation that mimics (and sometimes meets criteria for) ADHD, the "trauma ADHD" overlap

**Indian context**
- The punishment-first response: teachers and parents treat the symptom set as disrespect; by presentation time the secondary anxiety/ODD layer is often bigger than the core
- The "he was fine till Std 4" phenomenon: inattentive presentations survive till the year homework exceeds the attention span, then collapse visibly
- Coaching-pressured adolescents: sleep-starved, caffeine-loaded, performance-anxious; presenting with attention complaints that are mostly NOT ADHD

## 6. What happens in the brain

**Story 1: the brakes and the engine.** A child's prefrontal cortex is the braking-and-steering system over the engine of drives. In ADHD the engine is full-size and the brakes arrive late (maturation ~2–3 years behind) and work inconsistently. The child KNOWS the rule (knowledge is intact) but the rule does not arrive at the moment of impulse, which is why punishment after the fact teaches nothing: the system being punished was not the one that failed. Consequences work best immediate, predictable and small, the design logic of every behavioural programme for ADHD.

**Story 2: the interest dial.** Attention is not a quantity you have more or less of; it is a LOCK-ON mechanism with a dial. In ADHD the dial rotates to NOVEL, IMMEDIATE and REWARDING, and resists BORING-BUT-IMPORTANT. The same child plays three hours of cricket highlights but cannot copy ten lines, not laziness, dial mechanics. Dopamine and noradrenaline are the chemicals carrying the "this is worth it" signal; stimulants work by amplifying the boring-signal until it competes. This story is the one that most changes how teachers and parents see the child.

**Story 3: the maturation timeline.** Group imaging shows prefrontal cortical thickness peaking ~2–3 years later in ADHD. Many children genuinely "grow out" of visible hyperactivity (the bouncing becomes tapping, the running becomes restlessness) while the inattention and time-organisation problems persist in adult costume. This is why the honest longitudinal sentence is "symptoms usually soften; consequences can harden".

**Story 4: the overloaded classroom.** Take a regulation-challenged child, seat them in a 50-student room with 45-minute stationary stretches, add five subjects of nightly homework, subtract any movement from the day, and add a tuition hour at both ends; the Indian school system is an ADHD amplification machine. The same child in a farm household with physical work often looks half-diagnosed. Environment does not create the wiring; it decides how much of it becomes pathology.

## 7. Symptoms

**Inattention (the quiet half, the girls' half):**
- Careless mistakes in schoolwork (signs reversed in sums, missing whole questions); work done but not submitted
- Cannot hold attention on tasks not self-chosen; "he plays for hours, so his attention is fine" is the standard parental paradox, interest-locked attention, task-drifting attention
- Does not seem to listen when spoken to directly; instructions lost mid-route ("I forgot" as the family's most-heard word)
- Cannot finish chapter-wise homework without supervision; avoids sustained mental effort like plague
- Loses things as a profession: pencils, water bottles, notebooks, the fee receipt (objects that must travel through the day intact)
- Forgets the tuition bag, the exam date, the promised errand, and is punished for it as defiance
- Time blindness: two days until a test is not an actionable quantity

**Hyperactivity (the loud half, the boys' referral reason):**
- Fidgets, taps, shifts seat; cannot stay seated where expected (assembly lines, classrooms, dinner tables)
- Runs/climbs where inappropriate in the school years; internal restlessness as "I need to move" in adolescents/adults
- Cannot play quietly; excessive talking; motor-driven play
- Often "on the go" as if "powered by a motor", and shutdowns/irritable evenings from masking all day

**Impulsivity (the cost-driving half):**
- Answers blurted before questions end; cannot await turn in queues and games
- Interrupts and intrudes (conversations, games, queues)
- Impulsive decisions without preview: touching the exhibit, taking the friend's pen, spending, posting, and the adolescent version, risky driving and riding stunts
- Emotional impulsivity: quick flare-ups that resolve fast (distinguished from ODD's deliberate refusal pattern and from mood disorder's sustained states)

**The adult picture (what the child becomes):**
- Deadline and paperwork chaos; jobs lost for disorganisation, not incompetence
- Restlessness as boredom intolerance; serial projects, abandoned hobbies
- Relationship friction from interrupting, forgetting, impulse-spending, hot tempers
- Exhaustion from compensating (lists, alarms, over-apologising) and self-medication patterns (caffeine chains, nicotine early)

**Presentations:** predominantly inattentive (the missed girl), predominantly hyperactive-impulsive (mostly preschoolers), combined (the classic). Severity in DSM-5 is mild/moderate/severe by symptom count and impairment.

## 8. Diagnosis

**The logic (DSM-5-TR paraphrased):** at least 6 of 9 inattention and/or 6 of 9 hyperactivity-impulsivity items, present at least 6 months, to a degree inconsistent with developmental level; SEVERAL symptoms present before age 12; in TWO OR MORE settings; clear functional interference; not better explained by another condition. DSM-5-TR's criterion of "several" (rather than "one") symptoms before 12 tightened the childhood-onset gate to block late-onset mimics (sleep deprivation, substance, depression).

**The assessment sequence (the discipline that earns the label):**
1. **Multi-source history**: parent AND teacher accounts (rating scales: Conners and Vanderbilt lineages, SNAP-IV, named, not reproduced; Indian-adapted versions exist). Two-setting confirmation is the gate: the child who is symptomatic ONLY at school raises the school-problem flag; ONLY at home raises the home-problem flag
2. **Developmental and family history**: birth risk factors, speech/motor milestones, learning screen (a third have learning disorders); the parent ADHD screen (the ASRS for adults, named), the family's undiagnosed cases surface here
3. **The mimics audit (the differential in clinical clothing):** sleep (duration, timing, snoring/apnoea, sleep-starved children are ADHD-impersonators), hearing (any child with "not listening" gets ears checked, always), absence seizures (the vacant spells, a teacher's "staring blankly, unresponsive for seconds" needs EEG thinking, not a scolding), thyroid only where clinical clues, anxiety (attention stolen by worry), depression (concentration loss with mood change), trauma (adversity history), substance in adolescents (cannabis, nicotine, stimulant misuse), iron deficiency in severe picky eaters, and environmental chaos as the amplifier audit
4. **Mental state and classroom observation** where feasible (the observation hour outperforms all recollection)
5. **Testing tiers:** IQ/learning assessment where the school picture demands (learning disorders ride in a third of cases); continuous performance tests are adjuncts, not diagnoses (they measure a moment, not a life)
6. **Baseline before any medication:** height, weight, BP, pulse; cardiac history screen (family sudden-death under 40, exertional syncope, structural disease); enough to decide ECG/referral for the few who need it; sleep and appetite baseline documented
7. **Adults:** developmental retrofit (school report cards, parent interview about the child-you-were, the single most decisive document in adult ADHD assessment), current functional map (deadlines, driving, money, relationships), collateral from partner

### Differential table

| Looks like ADHD | The distinguishing feature |
|---|---|
| Insufficient sleep / late-night mobiles | Sleep history fixes it; symptoms weekend-structured; treat the sleep, re-look in 4 weeks |
| Hearing loss | Universal in "not listening" children; test ears before the label |
| Absence epilepsy | Stereotyped vacant spells, seconds long, interrupting activity mid-gesture; EEG |
| Anxiety disorders | Worry-content history; somatic complaints; symptoms situation-linked (exam months); attention is STOLEN, not unfixed |
| Depression | Concentration loss WITH mood change, sleep/appetite shift, anhedonia; onset datable |
| Learning disorder | Attention intact for non-academic demands; oral bright, written collapsing (see the Learning Disorders note) |
| Trauma/adversity dysregulation | Adversity history; hypervigilance/startle; symptoms cluster around triggers |
| Autism spectrum | Social-reciprocity history and sensory profile; ADHD comorbids with rather than mimics it |
| ODD | Deliberate refusal and defiance (calm, aimed) vs impulsive acts (fast, unaimed, regretted); comorbidity is the norm though |
| Thyroid/iron/lead states | The targeted labs where clinical clues point (risk-anchored, not blanket screening) |

## 9. Management

**The frame: treatment has two rails, structure and medicine, and the first needs the second less than most people think, but the second without the first underperforms.**

**1: Parent training and behaviour structure (first-line always; sole first-line under ~6 years).**
- The programme logic (the New Forest/Incredible Years/PCIT lineages): attention-first (praise the desired 5:1), commands short-positive-one-at-a-time, routines with visual timetables, star-charts with immediate small rewards, planned ignoring for whining, consistent consequences within seconds, time-out done technically
- The key reframe taught to parents: "catch him being good", because attention itself is the currency the child works for; scolding is still attention
- Homework architecture: 20-minute blocks with movement breaks, supervising ADJACENT not hovering; written planner as external prefrontal lobe
- *India note: structured parent-training programmes are metro-concentrated (psychologists/child-guidance clinics); a workable rural/semi-urban route is 3–4 clinic-based coaching sessions on the core rules with WhatsApp follow-up, evidence-adjacent, hugely better than nothing; behavioural paediatric guidance under NIMHANS-model district centres where available*

**2: School accommodations (the environmental half).**
- Seating front-and-centre away from window/door; permitted movement channels (errand jobs: "send him with the register" turns the engine into employment)
- Shortened written workload and homework batching; breaks between tests; oral testing where writing masks knowledge
- Daily report card between teacher and parent (the 3-target card: seat-work done, hands-up not blurting, submitted homework), the highest-yield school tool ever designed
- Indian realities: RPwD 2016 covers ADHD under the benchmark-disability category "specific learning disability AND others" only in severe cases with impairment certification; most ADHD accommodation in India is negotiated school-by-school with a doctor's letter (extra time, scribe only where specific learning disorder coexists); parent-teacher meetings with the child's therapist as bridge

**3: Pharmacotherapy (the big-effect-size tier, school-age and beyond).**

*Methylphenidate (the workhorse):* dopamine/noradrenaline reuptake inhibition in prefrontal cortex, sharpens boring-signal amplification. Short-acting (3–4 hours, twice/thrice daily, useful for titration and cost) and long-acting (8–12 hours, the adherence choice). Start low (5 mg base dose), titrate weekly to effect; effects visible day-one (dose-finding is days, not weeks). Side-effects: appetite fall (give breakfast before the dose; weight/height charted 6-monthly: the growth effect averages small and mostly catch-up-guardable), sleep delay (last dose timing), headache/stomach-ache initial days, BP/pulse small rises. Contraindication care: structural cardiac disease, active psychosis/mania; the family-sudden-death screen before starting. India: Schedule X, the prescription rules of rigor (locally: prescription with specific dispensing documentation, no refills without review; new prescriptions monthly-ish by state practice): methylphenidate ₹200–800/month by brand, long-acting costlier (₹1,000–2,500) (approx 2026).
*Lisdexamfetamine (new Indian arrival):* prodrug of dexamphetamine, long-acting, smoother; useful where methylphenidate response is partial or rebound prominent; same monitoring; Schedule X; higher cost. Doses in the 20–70 mg-day lineage, titrated.
*Atomoxetine (the non-stimulant):* noradrenaline reuptake inhibition (non-dopaminergic, non-controlled, Schedule H); the choice where abuse potential matters (hostel/boarded risk, diversion history, personal/substance misuse), comorbid anxiety (often dual-beneficial), or family refusal of "the stimulant label". SLOW: 2–6 weeks to effect, longer titration; appetite/weight and BP monitoring; rare liver-warning and suicidality-class warning awareness; ₹800–2,500/month by brand (approx 2026).
*Second-line/adjuncts:* clonidine (useful for sleep and tics comorbidity, sedation watch); guanfacine (limited Indian availability); bupropion and SSRIs are comorbidity tools, not ADHD core tools. Foods, fish-oil, "brain tonics", and elimination diets are not treatments (parent-friendly wording: "the evidence is thin-to-none, spend the money on the tutor with the patience instead").

**4: Comorbidity treatment (the plan-changers):** learning disorder remediation (else "treatment failure" is actually teaching mismatch), ODD/CD behavioural escalation protocols, anxiety/mood treatment, tics (see the Paediatric OCD–tics note), sleep treatment as its own target.

**5: Adolescents and adults (the continuity era):**
- Medication continuation with patient-controlled dosing days (exam months intensified, holidays lighter, the planned-break protocol some families choose), diversion counsellings explicit: the hostel roommate asking to "borrow one tablet" for an all-nighter is Schedule-X sharing, and both parties are at risk
- Vocational/living structure: external prefrontal prostheses, alarms, single notebook, calendar blocking, accountability partner; ADHD coaching where available
- Driving counselled specifically (accident-risk data), substance screening honest (treated ADHD is associated with LOWER substance risk than untreated, the counter-intuitive headline for the addiction-fearing parent)
- Adults: same molecule logic; long-acting formulations preferred; the "finally organised" functional gains are often transformative (the diagnosis-in-the-30s email the profession treasures)

*India note (approx 2026): child-guidance clinic routes: government medical colleges (low-cost, real waiting lists), NIMHANS/state institutes (excellence with travel), private developmental paediatricians/psychiatrists (₹800–2,500 per consult in metros); rating-scale use uneven: insist on the two-setting rule; teacher letters carry disproportionate weight in Indian schools. The biggest unmeasured cost is the treatment gap: an Indian classroom of 50 has, on average, 2–3 children with ADHD; almost all are being managed as discipline problems.*

## 10. Indian practice

- **The punishment-first culture is the presentation filter.** Indian ADHD patients arrive pre-roughened: years of beatings, seat-benches, humiliations, "useless/lazy" labels, so by consult time the child has secondary ODD behaviour, low academic self-image, and parents oscillating between guilt and defensiveness. The first clinical act is often reframing the CHILD to the PARENTS ("punish the brakes you didn't build" is the sentence families report remembering years later), before any prescription.
- **The tuition paradox.** The standard Indian response: add tuition hours; increases stationary seat-time for the child whose deficit is stationary seat-time, with a tutor who reads fidgeting as disrespect. The ADHD-correct architecture is shorter blocks, movement breaks, and homework BY TIME not BY VOLUME (finish in 40 minutes, done, this also restores parent sanity).
- **Schedule X reality discipline.** Methylphenidate and amphetamines are controlled in India: prescriptions need specific documentation formats, pharmacies log dispensing, and repeat prescriptions need reviews. Explain this to families as protection, not bureaucracy; it is what keeps the treatment climate from going the way of some prescribing cultures. Feigned presentation pressure exists (coaching cities, exam seasons): hold the diagnostic bar (childhood evidence, multi-source, impairment mapping), document, and reserve stimulants for the diagnosed.
- **The family screen is free and enormous.** Because heritability is ~75%, the child's assessment is the family's screening event: the father with the lifelong "list of unfinished businesses" and the mother with anxiety-driven over-compensation structure both need naming; treating the parent's ADHD often improves the child's environment more than another therapy hour.
- **Marriage-market and diagnosis-shyness.** Adults avoid the label fearing social/employment disclosure; India has no legal disclosure duty for ordinary employment; the practical frame: "this is a treatment decision, not an identity announcement; who you tell is your choice". For the IT/medical professionals who do present, the exam-month selective-dosing requests are best handled with a plan, not a standoff.
- **The school negotiation kit.** A doctor's letter specifying seating, movement breaks, homework caps and the report-card system works in most CBSE/ICSE/State schools; the leverage sentence is the classroom-average statistic (2–3 per class of 50; "this is a normal brain you will meet every year of your career").
- **The missed girl is India's biggest ADHD gap.** Dreamy, quiet, disorganised girls are daydreaming through Indian classrooms right now, reported as "can do better if she tried". The two-question screen for girls: "does homework take four times the promised time?" and "is her bag a permanent archaeological site?": positive answers earn the full assessment.

## 11. Clinical cases (de-identified)

**Case 1: the boy they call the class donkey.** A 9-year-old Lucknow boy was brought after a school ultimatum, with the father carrying a punishment log he had maintained since Std 1 ("caning at school, slapped at home; nothing changes"). Teacher's account: out of seat constantly, "does not listen", finishes work never, sharp mind in quizzes, brilliant at cricket, "but he writes like a two-year-old and loses his books weekly". Mother's account: identical at home; homework 3 hours for 30 minutes of work, "I have to sit with him", daily whining, quick flare-ups, bedtime 11 pm after mobile gaming until 10.30. He had been called "the class donkey" and begun "I am bad at studying" self-statements. Assessment: symptom counts exceeded thresholds in BOTH settings with onset before 7; sleep audit moved bedtime to a 21:30 (the later dose of the day, gaming till late, mild deprivation layer removed); hearing normal; learning screen identified writing-slow (not dyslexia); the teacher's Conners-type ratings and mother's agreed; no anxiety/depression mood architecture; cardiac screen clear. Diagnosis: ADHD, combined presentation, mild-to-moderate, with a learning-disorder-in-writing overlap. Management: three parent-coaching sessions (catch-him-good, 20-minute homework blocks, the 5:1 praise ratio with a star chart the father, re-trained from punisher to scorekeeper, ran with visible relish), teacher letter (front seat, errand jobs, he became the register boy, a daily status event; homework-by-time; daily report card on three targets), methylphenidate long-acting started at lowest paediatric dose with weekly titration to an effective dose, breakfast-before-dose, weight/height/BP monitoring, and writing fluency drills with the resource teacher for the handwriting channel. Six weeks: homework 45 minutes unaided, report card green 4 weeks of 6, "the donkey" title retired (his own words to the psychologist: "my brain works now"); growth and appetite stable with the breakfast strategy; sleep boundary held. Teaching points: (1) the father's punishment log was diagnosis data, not child management; (2) the sleep audit came before the prescription; the cheapest mimic removed; (3) environment-structure + medication outperformed either alone; (4) the errand job converted the engine's power to social standing; (5) the writing channel needed its own treatment; the ADHD tablet does not treat handwriting.

**Case 2: the medical student who finally opened her books.** A 21-year-old third-year MBBS student in a Bengaluru medical college self-referred, anxious, apologising: "I have the symptoms, I diagnosed myself on the internet, my friends think I just want the exam pill." History: Std 1 report cards (retrieved from home, photographed) contained teacher remarks that were verbatim ADHD ("very bright but careless and restless; work incomplete"); school career on 3–4-hour homework nights with a supervising mother; a brilliant-board-exam Std 10 (feared into focus), followed by college-level collapse: 47 missed assignment deadlines in the first two years, supplementary exams, notes from friends, chronic lateness to wards, the now-constant anxiety of the "lazy-fraud" self-label, three all-nighters per cycle, two coffees minimum, weekend crash-sleeps. Her father, the paediatrician confirmed from history, was a diagnostic prototype (lost keys, unfinished projects, library fines legend). Structured assessment: childhood-onset evidence from documents and parent interview, two-setting (college, home), impairment in multiple domains, ASRS strongly positive; screens negative for mood and anxiety disorders apart from secondary worry; sleep restructured first (the 2 a.m. phone issue), and a four-week trial of external scaffolding (calendar blocking, body-doubling study partner) gave partial gains, insufficient for professional stakes. After ECG/baseline checks, atomoxetine was chosen (the hostel-diversion issue was real; roommates had already "joked" about methylphenidate), titrated over five weeks; cognitive restructuring for the "fraud" narrative; a structured study-skills rebuild (Pomodoro-typed blocks, active recall, ward-log discipline). Twelve weeks: all assignments current, no supplementary, the study partner converted to permanent system; the anxiety unwound by itself as the deadline record cleaned. Her one-line verdict: "I did not get smarter: I finally stopped paying interest on a debt." Teaching points: (1) the school report card is the adult ADHD diagnosis's strongest document, retrieve it; (2) scaffolding-first trial established need and reduced the anxiety before medicine; (3) atomoxetine's slower onset requires warning, or students abandon it in week 2; (4) diversion risk is a campus reality that shapes molecule choice; (5) "exam-pill seeker" prejudice nearly cost this student three more years, the childhood evidence settles it.

## 13. FAQ: asked the way patients and parents ask it

**"Is it bad parenting? Did we spoil him?"** No. This is one of the most heritable brain conditions we know; roughly three-quarters of the variance is genetic. Parenting style can make it worse or better, but it did not create it, and neither beatings nor "being strict" will remove it; the punishments will, however, add a second layer of anger and anxiety on top of the first problem.

**"Sugar, chocolate, chips, should we cut these out?"** The sugar effect is essentially myth; blinded trials have repeatedly failed to find it. Some parents genuinely report behaviour differences with specific colouring agents or preservatives in individual children, remove these for a structured month if you suspect them, but don't build your whole plan on the diet aisle.

**"It's the mobile and TV, isn't it?"** Screens don't cause ADHD. What they do: eat sleep hours (which mimics it), displace homework practice, and drill the brain on instant rewards (which makes the boring tasks feel more boring). So we limit screens (for the same reason we limit them for every child) but removing the screen will not remove the condition.

**"Will he grow out of it?"** Partially, honestly. Hyperactivity visibly softens into restlessness in most; attention and organisation problems persist into adulthood in roughly half to two-thirds at some level. "Growing out" is really "growing in"; the brain matures, the systems improve, and with treatment the whole road is smoother.

**"Will the medicine make him addicted? It's a stimulant."** The paradox is the opposite: treated ADHD shows LOWER rates of substance misuse than untreated ADHD in follow-up studies. The medication is not a high at proper doses; it is signal amplification. The real addiction risks are in NOT treating: school failure and early substance initiation are the classic gateways.

**"His appetite fell after starting the tablet. Growth? Weight?"** Common and manageable: give a solid breakfast before the morning dose (the effect on appetite peaks mid-day), chart height and weight six-monthly, expect the average growth effect to be small (millimetres in a year on group data, mostly recoverable). If weight tracks down consistently, we adjust dose, timing or molecule; do not just stop.

**"Does he have heart risk? We heard a sudden-death story."** Routine cardiac disease is NOT a complication of ADHD medicines in healthy hearts, the scare stories concern children with pre-existing structural disease. Our job: ask the family history (sudden unexplained death under 40, fainting with exercise) and examine properly before starting; only the few with red flags get ECG/cardiology. Your child's heart being normal, the tablet is not a cardiac event.

**"Should we tell the school he is on medication?"** Tell the school the ACCOMMODATIONS (seat, breaks, report card) and that he has an attention condition; the medicine details are optional disclosure. The teacher needs the HOW-TO-TEACH information more than the pharmacology.

**"What are the non-medicine options actually worth the money?"** Parent-training (the real skills), the daily report card system, occupational support for handwriting, structured study-skills coaching for teens, and regular exercise (which measurably helps symptom control). Not worth the money: brain-training packages, fish-oil megadoses, gluten "cures", homoeopathic "attention formulas", the tonics aisle.

**"Can he focus for three hours of cricket? Then he can focus, he just doesn't want to study."** That is the interest dial, and it is the misunderstanding that most hurts these children. The ability to lock on to exciting things proves the engine; the fault is in the GEARBOX, shifting to boring-but-important demands. We treat the gearbox; the cricket focus is his strength, not his defence.

## 14. Exam & student lens

**The criteria skeleton for viva:** 6/9 + 6/9 domains, 6 months, before 12, 2+ settings, impairment, mimic-exclusion; presentations (inattentive/hyperactive-impulsive/combined); DSM-5-TR "several symptoms before 12".

**Mnemonic, "B-I-T-E":** Blurts, Interrupts, Turn-lost, Excess-movement (hyperactivity/impulsivity); pair with "F-A-I-L-S": Forgets, Avoids sustained effort, Ignores direct speech, Loses things, Sloppy mistakes (inattention); learn by the letters, answer by the domains.

**High-yield facts:**
- Heritability ~75%: screen the parents at the child's diagnosis; screen the childhood at the adult's
- Methylphenidate = DAT/NET inhibition, day-one effect, days-week titration; atomoxetine = NET-selective, 2–6 weeks, non-controlled, anti-anxiety bonus
- First-line under 6: parent training alone (guideline constant)
- Stimulants in treated ADHD → lower, not higher, later substance misuse (the paradox question)
- The standard monitoring set: height, weight, BP, pulse, sleep, appetite
- Commonest Indian presentation bias: combined-presentation boys by school ultimatum; the inattentive girl is the missed epidemic
- Two-setting rule: school-only or home-only symptoms = look for the SETTING's problem first
- Schedule X (India) for stimulants: prescription documentation, dispensing records, review-linked repeats

**Classic traps:**
1. Diagnosing ADHD in a sleep-deprived, late-night-gaming child (treat sleep, re-look)
2. Skipping the hearing test in "not listening"
3. Missing absence epilepsy behind the "daydreaming" girl (EEG when spells are stereotyped seconds)
4. Treating comorbid learning disorder as "poor response to ADHD medication"
5. Missing trauma/adversity histories in the dysregulated child (punish-first households hide them)
6. The adolescent feigning symptoms in a coaching city for the "study pill", childhood evidence and collateral decide
7. Atomoxetine abandoned at week 2 for "no effect" (its clock runs weeks; warn at the start)

**Indian exam corner:** expected patterns, "a 7-year-old, 2 settings, impaired school performance: diagnostic approach to ADHD"; "treatment of ADHD in a 5-year-old" (parent training first, the ethics favourite); "methylphenidate monitoring"; "adult ADHD: how to diagnose without childhood records". Indian-context marks: Schedule X handling, classroom prevalence (2–3 per 50), tuition-culture mismatch, coaching-city diversion, the RPwD/severe-case certification note.

## 16. Self-test MCQs

**1. Before age 6, the guideline first-line treatment for ADHD is:**
A. Methylphenidate
B. Parent behavioural training
C. Atomoxetine
D. Dietary elimination
**Answer: B.** The ethics-favourite constant; medication joins for insufficient response at school age.

**2. The DSM-5-TR tightening to reduce adult false positives:**
A. Requirement of several symptoms before age 12
B. Two symptoms in each domain
C. EEG confirmation
D. Teacher-report mandatory
**Answer: A.** Late-onset inattention (sleep, substance, depression) blocked at the childhood-evidence gate.

**3. Atomoxetine differs from methylphenidate in:**
A. Being a controlled Schedule X drug in India
B. Slower onset (weeks), no abuse liability, anxiety comorbidity benefit
C. Working only for hyperactivity
D. Requiring dietary restrictions
**Answer: B.** The NET-selective non-stimulant trade: the diversion-risk choice.

**4. A 9-year-old "does not listen" at school and home, has nightly mobile use till 11:30 and 6.5 hours sleep. First step:**
A. Start methylphenidate
B. Correct sleep for 4 weeks and re-assess
C. EEG
D. Intelligence testing
**Answer: B.** The sleep-starved child is an ADHD impersonator; treat the mimic, then re-look.

**5. The parental paradox "he plays cricket for hours" is answered by:**
A. He cannot have ADHD
B. The interest-dial mechanism: lock-on to novel/rewarding demands, drift on boring-important ones
C. Cricket is therapeutic
D. His teacher is lying
**Answer: B.** Attention as a lock-on mechanism with a motivational gearbox; the engine works; the shifting is the deficit.

**6. The most useful diagnostic document in an adult ADHD assessment:**
A. Last year's blood reports
B. School report cards/teacher remarks from Std 1–8 plus a parent interview
C. A continuous performance test
D. Sleep diary alone
**Answer: B.** Childhood evidence retrofitted: the onset gate lives in documents and the parents' memory.

**7. Baseline monitoring set before starting stimulants:**
A. EEG, MRI, thyroid
B. Height, weight, BP, pulse + cardiac history screen
C. Liver function tests
D. IQ testing
**Answer: B.** The growth-CV set; deeper cardiac work-up only for red-flag histories.

**8. The "treatment causes addiction" fear is best answered by:**
A. It is true; stop treatment
B. Follow-up studies show treated ADHD has LOWER later substance misuse than untreated
C. Stimulants are non-addictive at any dose
D. Switch immediately to diet therapy
**Answer: B.** The evidence paradox: untreated ADHD's failure-and-initiation pathway is the actual risk.
