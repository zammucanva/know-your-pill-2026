# Party Drugs (MDMA, GHB, Ketamine)

## 1. In one line + overview

**The club trio is three different pharmacologies wearing one dress code: MDMA; the serotonin-flooding "empathy" tablet whose emergencies are HEAT and WATER (hyperthermia and water-poisoning); GHB: the sedative whose dose-timing is a knife edge between euphoria and coma, and whose withdrawal is as dangerous as alcohol's; and ketamine: the dissociative whose long-term bill arrives in the bladder and the memory.**

Party-drug medicine is really environmental medicine: the same tablets that are manageable in a calm living room kill in a crowded 35-degree dance floor, because MDMA's toxicity is magnified by heat, crowding and dancing, the classic club-death triangle, while its other death, hyponatraemia, comes from drinking TOO MUCH water in fear of the first. GHB (and its chemical cousin GBL) has built a second, quieter epidemic; the gym-and-nightlife dependence whose 24-hour withdrawal storms can seize and kill, and whose users are misdiagnosed as "just alcohol problems" because the drug is invisible on standard screens. Ketamine's dance-floor career has matured into a distinct chronic patient: the young user with bladder agony, frequency and the shrunken bladder of ketamine-induced ulcerative cystitis, plus the memory complaint of chronic dissociation. India's version of the trio lives in the metro club-and-festival circuits (with predictable New-Year and festival casualty waves in Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Goa), in rising seizure statistics, and in the darknet-postal pipeline, small in population terms, but arriving at the casualty in the worst-dressed emergencies of the young.

## 2. Learning objectives

After this note you can:
- Describe MDMA's mechanism in plain words: monoamine flood with serotonin emphasis, reuptake reversal, the empathogenic high and the midweek empty-warehouse comedown
- Run the MDMA emergency algorithm: the heat-death triangle (hyperthermia, crowding, dancing), the water-death paradox (SIADH-plus-over-drinking hyponatraemia), serotonin syndrome recognition, and the treatment of each
- Manage MDMA's aftermath honestly: the comedown tier, depression and memory concerns in heavy users, the sleep architecture of recovery
- Recognise GHB on both edges: the deep-but-reversible coma with oddly-preserved respiration, and the 1–6-hour-cycle dependence whose withdrawal (tremor, delirium, autonomic storm, seizures) is a medical emergency needing inpatient high-dose baclofen/benzodiazepine/phenobarbital-class protocols
- Diagnose and manage ketamine's chronic bill: the bladder syndrome (frequency, urgency, pain, haematuria, the shrunken bladder), the cognitive and dissociative effects, dependence, and the treatment pathway (abstinence as the only bladder-cure, urology partnership)
- Deliver the harm-reduction package in Indian idiom: reagent testing, the middle-path hydration rule, chill-out zones, the sitter rule, and the "never mix GHB with alcohol" absolute
- Structure the aftercare: brief interventions at the casualty touchpoint, family conversations without catastrophe-talk, and the psychiatric comorbidity screen (ADHD, depression, trauma) that hides under party life

## 4. Epidemiology

**Global.** UNODC frames MDMA/ecstasy use at the order of 10–30 million past-year users worldwide, concentrated in developed nightlife markets but with production shifting and resurging (high-purity "crystal" MDMA era, dosage per tablet doubling from the classic 1990s norms). GHB/GBL: small, underestimated, nightlife-and-chemsex-linked, with forensic attention disproportionate to numbers (drink-spiking and death cases); its dependence clinics are a European urban specialty niche. Ketamine: rising global recreational use plus the separate legitimate explosion of clinical-esketamine; the chronic-bladder syndrome grew into a defined epidemiology in East and Southeast Asia first (Hong Kong's K-generation literature the deepest), now global wherever nightly use persists.

**India.** Population surveys register MDMA-class use at negligible general-population levels, but the metro-and-Goa club circuit's consumption is real and rising by every proxy measure: MDMA seizure statistics climbing through the 2010s-2020s, New-Year casualty surges in metro hospitals, darknet-and-postal supply chains, and the collapse of the classic "ecstasy = expensive metro novelty" boundary as supply prices fell. GHB is scarce and nearly invisible clinically (no screen, no smell, no routine testing; its Indian presence is an inference from case reports and the global supply pattern), ketamine circulates via diversion (the veterinary-and-pharmacy leak) in club and private-party circuits. The Indian user profile: 18–30, urban, English-medium, employed or student, polydrug evenings (alcohol + cannabis + MDMA ± cocaine), meaning Indian MDMA emergencies arrive inside cocktails, complicating the textbook picture.

## 5. Causes & risk factors

- **Circuits and supply**: festival, club and private-party circuits; darknet-postal access; peer-supply as the first door; price-falls widening the funnel
- **Environment (the toxicity multiplier)**: heat, crowding, all-night dancing, poor venue cooling, the difference between a safe-ish evening and a hyperthermia death
- **Psychological**: sensation-seeking; the social-anxiety self-medication pattern (shy person discovering "I can talk with one tablet"); ADHD and dopamine-hungry temperament; trauma and mood disorders beneath the party surface
- **Polypharmacy**: alcohol co-loading (disinhibition, dehydration, GHB lethal-synergy), cannabis, stimulant stacking, the "last tablet of the night" re-dosing pattern
- **Dose and product unknowns**: high-purity crystal era, substitution (PMA/PMMA and cathinones sold as MDMA, the serial-death substitutions), no reagent-testing culture in India
- **Vulnerability tags**: cardiac conduction disease, hyponatraemia-prone states (SSRI users: SIADH synergy), epilepsy, bipolar-spectrum instability; for GHB, any prior sedative dependence; for ketamine: urinary tract sensitivity and nightly use patterns
- **Indian context**: the festival wave-structure (New Year, Holi-season, festival weekends), the VIP-private-party tier where GHB-class drugs circulate least-suspected, and the afterparty-with-no-records problem for epidemiology

## 6. What happens in the body/brain: three short stories

**Story one: the warm flood and the empty warehouse.** MDMA flips the serotonin transporter backwards: instead of mopping up serotonin, the pumps push it OUT, a warm, trusting, touch-friendly flood (the "empathogen" hour; everybody becomes beautiful, every conversation becomes deep). The bill arrives days later: the storage vesicles are drained, the transporter's machinery is exhausted, and Tuesday's brain is an empty warehouse, flat, irritable, teary, sleepless: the midweek comedown. Repeat every weekend for months and the warehouse learns a chronic low stock: the heavy-user studies show measurable mood and memory dips that mostly, but not entirely, recover with months of abstinence; the honest position is a temporary-moron-and-dysthymia phase that patience and sleep rebuild, and a small minority with persistent trouble.

**Story two: heat and water, the two deaths.** MDMA tilts the body's thermostat up while the dance floor tilts the room's temperature up: temperature spirals (the malignant-hyperthermia-like state), muscle cooks (rhabdomyolysis), kidneys fail, clotting cascades, organs fall in sequence, the heat-death, made of environment times chemistry. The second death wears the costume of safety: the frightened user who "knows she must hydrate" drinks litres of plain water (while MDMA's SIADH effect makes the body HOLD the water) sodium falls, brain cells swell: nausea, confusion, seizure, coma (the Leah-Betts-class deaths that taught the world the middle-path hydration rule: sip electrolyte fluid, do not flood). The two deaths' lesson is the note's core paradox: the drug's danger is co-authored by the club and by the countermeasure.

**Story three: the edge and the knife (GHB), the leak (ketamine).** GHB rides a narrow therapeutic ridge: the euphoric-relaxed dose and the comatose dose are separated by fractions of a capful, and the gap narrows with tolerance's paradox; the user needs more for the high but the sedation edge does not move away; the overdose pattern: deep coma, snoring-oddly, breathing preserved-enough-until-it-isn't, and the distinctive cycling (wake groggy, re-sedate, wake again) as levels fall fast (half-life a few hours). Dependence builds on the every-3-4-hours dosing clock (including night doses, the user who wakes at 3 a.m. for a capful); STOPPING produces a withdrawal with alcohol-like-plus qualities (tremor, sweating, hallucinations, delirium, seizures, hypertension storms) but compressed and violent inside 24-72 hours, at times fatal when untreated; standard drug screens show nothing, the invisible dragon of the detox world. Ketamine's chronic story is the bladder's: nightly dissociation comes at the wall's expense, inflammation, ulceration, scarred shrinkage; the young patient peeing every half-hour in agony with a bladder the size of an egg, for whom abstinence is the only real medicine and whose urology visits pile up before anyone asks about the drug.

## 7. Symptoms

**MDMA intoxication (the empathogenic picture):** warmth toward strangers, talkativeness, jaw-clenching and tooth-grinding (bruxism, the gum-chewing tell), sweating, wide pupils, blurred "loving" affect, hours of dancing energy, appetite gone; marked sinus tachycardia with modest BP change.

**MDMA emergencies (what the casualty sees):** hyperthermia cascade (temp rising, muscle rigidity, dark urine = myoglobin, confusion → collapse); hyponatraemia (nausea, headache, confusion, seizures, coma after the water-flood pattern); serotonin syndrome with co-drugs (clonus, hyperreflexia, agitation, heat); hypertensive/coronary events in occult cardiac disease; water-drinking plus heat confusion mixture-states.

**MDMA aftermath:** the comedown week (flatness, irritability, poor sleep with REM-rebound nightmares, tearfulness, craving-for-meaning); heavy-use chronic picture (word-finding and memory complaints, low-grade dysthymia, weekend-anchored life structure); the "loss-of-magic" phenomenon (the high stops delivering warmth after chronic use, its own natural brake, honestly worth telling users).

**GHB states:** intoxication at low dose, sociable disinhibition, mild euphoria, drowsiness; at higher dose: rapid deep coma (minutes), the preserved-snoring respiration, the cycling wake-sedate-wake, vomiting-in-coma (the aspiration risk), bradycardia at depth; withdrawal after dependent-clock use (early-morning tremor, sweating, anxiety, then insomnia-craving, and by 24 hours a storm: hallucinations, delirium, autonomic surge, seizures) more compressed and often more labile than alcohol withdrawal.

**Ketamine chronic picture:** out-of-body "K-hole" episodes on heavy nights; tolerance and craving creep; the bladder syndrome: urgency, frequency (every 20–60 minutes), suprapubic pain, haematuria, worsening through the workday; the cognitive slow-fade: episodic memory and concentration complaints ("my head is a fog"), the social withdrawal of the nightly-dissociated; "K-cramps" (abdominal pain) and hepatotoxicity at the heavy end.

## 8. Diagnosis

**The logic.** In India the diagnosis is the scene plus the syndrome plus the collateral: no routine casualty toxicology confirms MDMA or GHB (specialised assays are research-forensic tier; ketamine is detectable on some screens but seldom requested). The discipline: in ANY young club-context emergency, ask the party questions (what did you take, what was it sold as, how much water, how hot, who else has the batch), pull the friends' phones for the dealer-packet (pill-report culture), and run the bloods the emergency needs anyway, sodium (the water-death flag), creatine kinase and creatinine (the heat-death flag), temperature curve, ECG (conduction, the PMA-substitution cardiotoxicity), glucose, and blood gases in the collapsed.

**Structured assessment:**
- Agent-sold-as vs agent-probably-is (substitution screen in the history)
- Co-ingestions and the timeline of the evening (alcohol units, cannabis, re-dosing)
- Environment history: heat, crowd, duration of dancing, water litres, the toxicity multiplier ledger
- Comorbidity screen: SSRI use (serotonin-synergy), cardiac, epilepsy, bipolar
- For the chronic presentation: the bladder diary (frequency, volumes, pain) and the cognitive screen for ketamine; the dosing-clock history for GHB-dependence suspicion (the 3 a.m. dose question is the diagnosis)

**Differential table:**

| Emergency shape | Think |
|---|---|
| Heat + dark urine + collapse after dancing | MDMA hyperthermia-rhabdomyolysis |
| Confusion/seizures after litres of water | Hyponatraemia (confirm sodium immediately) |
| Coma, snoring, cycling, woke-clear-in-hours, screens negative | GHB/GBL |
| Clonus + hyperreflexia + serotonergic co-drug | Serotonin syndrome |
| Rigidity + hyperthermia + antipsychotic on board | NMS (the casualty's other storm) |
| Chest pain after "ecstasy" | PMA/cathinone substitution or occult cardiac |
| Frequency + suprapubic pain in a young partier | Ketamine bladder: ask the drug before the cystoscope |

## 9. Management

**MDMA emergencies:**
- **Hyperthermia**: aggressive cooling (stripping, ice, fanned fluids, cooled IV), IV fluids, muscle relaxant/anaesthesia-tier escalation for rigid malignant states, CK-renal trajectory monitoring, ICU when temperature runs high; minutes matter
- **Hyponatraemia**: fluid RESTRICTION, salt correction measured to the guideline-gram (acute symptomatic hyponatraemia protocols), seizure control; the historical "drink water" advice is the memory to actively erase
- **Serotonin syndrome**: stop the agents, cooling, benzodiazepines, cyproheptadine in significant cases (the toxicology-standard tier)
- **Agitation and distress**: benzodiazepines; the talking-down floor for the panic states (the Hallucinogens note's skill transfers)

**MDMA aftercare:** sleep-and-recovery weeks, no immediate antidepressant decisions from the Tuesday-tearful state (transient comedown vs true depression, take the four-week history); brief intervention at the casualty touchpoint (the teachable minute: middle-path hydration, chill-out discipline, testing, the loss-of-magic fact); the psychiatric comorbidity treatment that eventually keeps the party in proportion.

**GHB coma:** airway-watch (the preserved breathing fails at depth and with vomit), positioning, observation through the cycling (the wake-clear moment is NOT discharge), no naloxone-logic (not an opioid; the flumazenil question also runs caution: co-benzo uncertainty); supportive care resolves it in hours.

**GHB withdrawal, the emergency discipline:** admit (do not manage as outpatient, the compressed-storm pattern), high-dose benzodiazepine with phenobarbital-backbone or the baclofen-tapering protocols (the European clinical-guidance tier: baclofen 10–30 mg three-times-daily and titrated-taper structures), symptom-monitored environment, seizure precautions, cardiac watch; the taper clock spans weeks for the dependent-clock user; relapse-prevention and the sleep-rebuilding phase follow.

**Ketamine pathway:** the bladder workup (urology partnership, ultrasound capacity, urinalysis, the cystoscopy-and-biopsy tier where indicated), the abstinence contract as the only disease-modifying bladder treatment (early-stage syndrome can recover substantially; the shrunken-scarred late bladder does not), pain-management cooperation (avoid the opioid-road for a chronic-functional-pain syndrome of young people), cognitive-recovery patience, dependence treatment on stimulant-adjacent lines (no substitute pharmacotherapy exists; contingency and motivational structures carry it).

**Harm reduction (the Indian club idiom):** reagent-test-your-tablet (the Marquis-tier kits, scarce in India, ordering them is itself the teachable act), the middle-path hydration rule (about 500 ml per hour of electrolyte-containing fluid when dancing, NOT litres of plain water), chill-out zones and the 15-minute-cool rule, the sitter rule (one straight friend per group), never-mix-GHB-with-alcohol (the lethal-synergy absolute), and the batch-alert etiquette (tell the circle when a batch hurts somebody).
- *India note*: casualty waves are festival-clustered; the emergency tier exists in metro hospitals; the dependence-and-aftercare tier barely does (no Indian GHB-withdrawal literature to speak of; learn from the European protocols; ketamine-bladder urology referral is the practical pathway in metros; costs approx 2026: emergency admission and bloods government-tier near-free to a few thousand rupees private; urology workup ₹2,000–10,000; the harm-reduction tier is DIY-import).

## 10. Indian practice

The Indian clinician meets the trio in three rooms: the New-Year casualty corridor (the hyperthermia-and-hyponatraemia young, the cocktails, the police-notification dance with NDPS-tier optics; treat first, the paperwork after, and the patient-who-swallows-the-remaining-tablet-when-police-enter is a real complication class); the follow-up OPD where the comedown-weekly and the ketamine-bladder young appear, more often brought by mothers than self-referred (the "his friends told us about a party" history is the honest entrance ticket); and the invisible-GHB problem, the misdiagnosis ledger: the young man treated as "alcohol withdrawal with atypical features" failing benzo-standard doses (the GHB storm needs baclofen-plus-phenobarbital-class thinking), the nightclub-unwitnessed collapses called "unknown poisoning" and discharged without the clock-question. The family conversation's honest frame: this tier of Indian user is not the homeless-inhalant child nor the opioid patient of the de-addiction ward; it is the employed college-educated young, and the intervention that works is calibrated for them (brief interventions, credible information, the scene-level harm reduction, and the comorbidity treatment; lecture-moralisation loses this audience entirely). Prevention-level reality: venue regulation (cooling, water-stations, medical corners at mass events) is where the heat-death triangle is actually cut, an advocacy note for any psychiatrist advising an event or city.

## 11. Clinical cases

**Case 1: the two deaths in one room.** New Year's night, a Bengaluru casualty: two 22-year-olds from the same party. One arrives rigid at 41.2 degrees after five hours of dancing, stripped, iced, cooled-saline, paralysed-and-ventilated in ICU, dark urine mapped to a CK in the hundreds of thousands, dialysis debated: survives after ten days (the heat-death path caught at minute 20). The second: found drinking water compulsively "because the internet said to", seizures with sodium of 112: fluid-restricted, hypertonic-saline-corrected, wakes disoriented then clear by morning (the water-death path). Both discharged with the same teachable-minute conversation, and both returned the next New Year (the honest follow-up: harm-reduced, dose-aware, chill-out-disciplined, the harm that was cut: from death to comedown). Teaching points: the twin deaths share one prevention (the middle-path-plus-cooling rule); minute-20 cooling decides the first; sodium decides the second; the touchpoint-brief-intervention is the only addiction treatment most of this tier ever accepts.

**Case 2: the alarm clock dependence.** A 29-year-old fitness trainer in Delhi: two years of "G-water" after evening gym sessions becomes a 4-times-daily clock including the 3 a.m. capful; two self-attempts to stop collapse by hour 30 (hallucinating, hypertensive, one seizure); labelled "atypical alcohol withdrawal" at two hospitals (his drinks were, in truth, minimal). The admission that reads the clock: baclofen-tapered protocol plus phenobarbital-covered nights, six days of monitored storm-weathering, then the rebuild (sleep architecture, the anxiety-below-the-club treated with CBT), and the 3 a.m.-alarm-clock de-conditioning. Teaching points: the night-dose question is the GHB-dependence diagnosis; standard screens are silent; the withdrawal is lethal-adjacent and inpatient-only; the "alcohol-withdrawal-atypical" label is the signal to ask what else sits in the gym bag.

## 13. FAQ

- *"Is ecstasy more dangerous than alcohol, doctor? I only take it at parties."*: Per evening, the risk of the tablet is smaller than the risk of the ROOM: heat, crowding, water-panic and dose-luck co-author the deaths. Managed evenings (cool, tested, middle-path water, a sober sitter) cut most of it; what no management removes is what it is doing to your Tuesdays and, after years, to your memory and mood.
- *"They say one tablet can kill."*: One tablet's chemistry alone rarely does; but one tablet's DOSE-luck (substitution, high-purity crystal) plus one hot room plus four litres of water has killed many; the honest warning is the multiplication, not the single molecule.
- *"I feel low and dumb for days after, permanent damage?"*: The days-after flatness is the empty-warehouse comedown, and it rebuilds with sleep and weeks off. The heavy-every-weekend pattern holds measurable memory-and-mood costs that mostly recover over months of abstinence, mostly, honestly, not provably-always.
- *"My friend cannot wake after a party but he is breathing and snoring, let him sleep?"*: On his side, watched, yes-positioning, but hospital if breathing slows, if he vomits, or if the cycles run deeper than hours: that shape is GHB-class, and the screens will not show it.
- *"I need to pass urine every half hour and it burns, the urologist found nothing infected."*: The question he did not ask: ketamine. Nightly use writes this bill on the bladder wall, and the one treatment that truly works is stopping, early, before the scarring sets the size of your future.
- *"Is the Special-K my brother uses the same as the depression treatment in the news?"*: Same molecule, different universe: clinic doses, monitored, infrequent, alongside a treatment programme, versus nightly unmeasured grams. The bladder and the memory damage live in the second universe.
- *"Water is the safety rule, everyone knows that."*: The rule that replaced it: sip about 500 ml of electrolyte-containing fluid per hour WHILE dancing, not litres of plain water, the deaths taught us the second lesson the hard way.
- *"He has stopped GHB by himself twice, why does he not just do it again?"*: Because the two "stops" were probably the withdrawal storms the family called "madness episodes"; this one is stopped in hospital with medicine, or it is not safely stopped at all.

## 14. Exam & student lens

- **High-yield anchors**: MDMA mechanism (transporter-reversal serotonin flood); the two MDMA deaths (hyperthermia, hyponatraemia) and their antidotes; serotonin syndrome triad (clonus, hyperreflexia, hyperthermia); GHB coma's preserved-respiration and cycling; GHB withdrawal = 24-hour compressed alcohol-plus storm, baclofen/phenobarbital-class inpatient; ketamine bladder (frequency-pain-haematuria, abstinence-only cure); PMA/PMMA substitution deaths

## 16. Self-test MCQs

**1. A dancer at 41 degrees, rigid, dark urine. The intervention whose MINUTES decide the outcome:**
A. Slow IV room-temperature crystalloid and observation
B. Aggressive active cooling NOW (stripping, ice, cooled fluids, with escalation to paralysis-and-ventilation), the heat-death clock
C. Wait for the CK report
D. Fluid-load with 5% dextrose
**Answer: B.** The hyperthermia cascade is minute-timed; the CK report arrives after the fate has.

**2. She drank "plenty of water for safety," now seizes with sodium 112. The correction:**
A. More free water
B. Fluid restriction plus measured hypertonic-saline correction, and erase the litres-advice that caused it
C. Immediate haemodialysis first-line
D. Furosemide alone
**Answer: B.** The water-death paradox's treatment; over-rapid correction adds its own injury, measured grams, monitored.

**3. The coma patient: snoring, breathing acceptably, cycling through wake-and-resedate, all screens negative. The agent and the disposition rule:**
A. Opioids: naloxone and discharge on wake
B. GHB/GBL: observe THROUGH the cycles; the briefly-clear moment is not discharge
C. Alcohol: sleep it off at home
D. Benzodiazepines: flumazenil push
**Answer: B.** The invisible dragon's signature; the cycling sedation decides the observation clock, not the first clear minute.

**4. GHB dependence with the 3 a.m. dosing clock. The withdrawal-management frame:**
A. Outpatient taper: it is "just a sleep aid"
B. Inpatient, with baclofen-phenobarbital-class-high-dose protocols: a compressed, seizure-bearing, at-times-fatal storm
C. Nothing, no withdrawal exists for GHB
D. Disulfiram
**Answer: B.** The clock-user's stop is an emergency; the mislabel "atypical alcohol withdrawal" is the diagnosis's costume.

**5. A 24-year-old with frequency, suprapubic pain and sterile urine cultures, nightly "K" for two years. The disease-modifying treatment:**
A. Long-term antibiotics
B. Abstinence from ketamine: earliest possible, before scarred shrinkage; urology partnership for the damage done
C. Opioid analgesia regimen
D. Anticholinergics alone
**Answer: B.** The bladder's cure is the drug's stop; early syndrome recovers substantially; the late-shrunken bladder does not.

**6. The MDMA + SSRI combination's specific hazard:**
A. Parkinsonism
B. Serotonin-synergy: serotonin syndrome (clonus, hyperreflexia, heat) plus the SIADH-hyponatraemia stacking
C. Purely pharmacokinetic only
D. None known
**Answer: B.** The co-drug ledger: two serotonergic pushes plus water-retention, the casualty's clonus-and-confusion combination.
