# Volatile Substance Misuse (Inhalants)

## 1. In one line + overview

**Inhalant misuse is psychiatry's youngest and most neglected addiction: legal, cheap, child-accessible solvents (glue, correction fluid, thinners, petrol) inhaled for a fifteen-minute dizzy euphoria, running the fastest escalation curve of any intoxicant, killing suddenly (arrhythmia, suffocation, aspiration), and in India concentrated among street children whom the system has already failed once; while its modern offshoot, nitrous oxide (the whippet), has brought the same-chemistry surprise to metro party youth as B12-paralysis.**

The drug is bought at the stationery shop. That single fact organises the whole Indian epidemiology: no dealer, no age-gate, no NDPS schedule, no cost barrier; a tube of glue costs less than chai and delivers a brief, warm, dizzying "glue drunk" that the lonely brain of an unschooled street child finds adequate. The pharmacology is brute: lipid-soluble hydrocarbons enter through the lungs to the brain within seconds, depress it indiscriminately (a "dirty drunk" rather than a clean opioid high), sensitise the heart's rhythm to adrenaline (the sudden sniffing death, a healthy adolescent collapsing mid-inhalation), and with chronic use erode white matter, cerebellum, kidneys and peripheral nerves. The clinical population is double-faced: the visible one, the street child with glue-specked hands, rashes around the nose, and school-less afternoons in railway stations of every metro; and the invisible one: the metro teen filling balloons with nitrous from online-bought canisters, presenting months later with numb feet and an unsteady walk. The note's thesis: this is a child-welfare-condition addiction, no prescription, no detox ward, no agonist exists for it; treatment is rescue, shelter, structure, schooling and hope, delivered through the child-protection system, and the addiction itself is astonishingly responsive to the only thing that ever cures it; a life worth being sober for.

## 2. Learning objectives

After this note you can:
- Name the inhalant families (solvents and glues, fuels, gases/aerosols, nitrous oxide) and their route of one common brain action despite different chemical clothes
- Explain sudden sniffing death in one sentence: catecholamine-sensitised myocardium + solvent → fatal arrhythmia, and its practice rule (beware adrenaline in the acutely intoxicated child)
- Recognise the street presentation: the tells (glue on hands and face, solvent breath, plastic bag, empty tubes, perioral rash), the brief intoxicated picture, the withdrawal pattern
- Diagnose and treat the chronic complications: white-matter-cerebellar cognitive decline, the toluene-renal story (distal tubular acidosis, the hypokalaemic-weak child), peripheral neuropathy, bone-marrow suppression
- Recognise nitrous-oxide myeloneuropathy: numb feet, sensory ataxia, positive Lhermitte, B12-inactivation with normal-ish blood counts, and the treatment (stop + high-dose B12)
- Manage through the child-protection architecture: Childline 1098, Child Welfare Committees, shelter-and-education pathway, NGO-programme partnership, the real Indian treatment delivery
- Counsel families and schools without catastrophe-talk, and run the school-based response tiers

## 4. Epidemiology

**Global.** Inhalant misuse is the signature drug of disadvantaged and indigenous adolescents across continents (North American and Australian indigenous youth, Latin American street children, Eastern European and Central Asian poverty pockets); with the shared epidemiological signature: youngest mean age of first use of any intoxicant (commonly 12–14 or younger), high prevalence among homeless/street children, and a high early-mortality (sudden death, burns, aspiration, trauma). The nitrous-oxide wave (from the mid-2010s onward, accelerated by online canister sales) is the class's entry into white-collar young-adult life, with presentation shifting from psychiatric (dependence, parties) to neurological (B12-myelopathy clinics in Western cities).

**India.** Street-children studies across the metros repeatedly find inhalants as THE dominant drug of the street, 35–70% ranges across Delhi, Lucknow, Bengaluru, Chennai and other surveys (the AIIMS-affiliated "Assessment of Pattern of Substance Use among Street Children" tier of work; Salaam Baalak and NGO clinical experience agreeing), with the toluene tube (dendrite-glue and correction-fluid class) as the workhorse agent, petrol in some pockets, and a polydrug ladder that climbs to cannabis and opioids with age. School surveys register single-digit ever-use; the whippet/nitrous tier is small, rising, and metro-private-school-and-college flavoured. For psychiatrists and paediatricians alike, the rule: any unsupervised, homeless or street-working child in your district is at risk by stationery-shop proximity alone.

## 5. Causes & risk factors

- **Availability (the master cause)**: legal, unscheduled, non-age-gated, sold at every stationery-and-hardware counter; the "dealer" is the retail economy itself
- **Poverty and street life**: runaway and thrown-away children, street-working and begging (including organised-begging exploitation where inhalants keep children docile, a documented cruelty pattern), railway-station and market childhoods
- **Developmental timing**: early adolescence, the reward-seeking brain at maximum, risk appraisal at minimum, and the drug cheap enough for a ten-rupee pocket
- **Childhood adversity**: the aetiology behind the addiction; physical and sexual abuse, domestic violence, parental substance use and death, school failure and expulsion; the sniffing is self-medication for a childhood that hurts
- **Peer culture**: the sniffing gang as family-substitute, initiation, technique, and belonging delivered together
- **Pharmacological trap**: rapid-onset, rapid-offset reward (the craving-and-relapse engine of short-half-life drugs) with instant tolerance growth
- **Nitrous-specific**: party culture, online supply, the "harmless laughing gas" mythology, and zero training about B12
- **Indian context**: absent school-counselling tier, juvenile-justice entry replacing health entry, shelter-bed scarcity, and the toluene-tube retail economy that state-level sale-restrictions to minors (promulgated in some states) barely dent

## 6. What happens in the body/brain: three short stories

**Story one: the dirty drunk.** The solvent vapour is fat-loving; the lungs deliver it to the blood in breaths; the blood-fat barrier is no barrier; the brain is bathed within seconds. What follows is not a receptor-specific high but a general neurological sinking with a front-loaded disinhibition: slurred, giggly, dizzy, flushed, briefly euphoric, then confused and drowsy, a rough imitation of drunkenness that clears in minutes-to-an-hour as the fat-soaked solvent redistributes and lungs exhale it out. The brevity is the trap: craving returns fast, re-dosing is cheap, and the day organises into cycles of intoxication through the plastic bag.

**Story two: the sensitised heart.** The solvent bath does something a beer never does: it renders the heart's rhythm unstable to adrenaline, "sensitised myocardium." Now add the inhalant-session's adrenaline surges (startle, a chase, a police raid, sexual activity, the terror-intensity of bag-asphyxia) and the rhythm can tear into ventricular fibrillation with no warning, in a healthy twelve-year-old, mid-inhale. This is sudden sniffing death, the reason the class's mortality profile reads like an accident column: collapse during sniffing, found with the bag, no autopsy-visible catastrophe beyond the rhythm's own. The practice rule it writes in casualty: adrenaline-class drugs in the acutely solvent-intoxicated child can be the arrhythmia's second author; use only when truly life-mandatory, with the rhythm-strip vigilance on.

**Story three: the slow solvent dementia, the kidneys, and the gas that eats B12.** Months-to-years of toluene-class sniffing is a documented brain-wasting: white-matter degeneration (the solvent dissolves the insulation of the wiring; the pharmacological pun is nearly literal), cerebellar shrinkage (the wide-based stumble), cognitive decline measurable on school-age testing, and the mood-irritability crust of the chronically solvent-exposed child. The kidneys: toluene drives a renal tubular acidosis (the distal tubule fails to acidify, potassium pours out in urine, and the child presents with the class's most dramatic medical emergency: sudden profound weakness, even flaccid paralysis, from hypokalaemia) a sniffing child who cannot stand, with an ECG that may stop the heart politely mid-workup. The marrow: benzene-class companions suppress it. And nitrous, the class's respectable cousin: it oxidises vitamin B12's cobalt; the vitamin is present but inactivated; the myelin machinery of the spinal cord's columns starves over months of weekly whippets; numb feet, buzzing hands, an unsteady gait in the dark, the electric-bolt down the spine on neck flexion, subacute combined degeneration arriving in teens who never missed a meal. The nitrous story's grace: it is the most treatable drug-injury in the addiction catalogue; stop the gas, flood the B12, and the majority recover over weeks-to-months.

## 7. Symptoms

**Intoxication (the fifteen-minute picture):** slurred speech, dazed giggling, disinhibition, dizziness and ataxia (the stumbling circle), facial flushing, nystagmus at higher doses, drowsiness and sleep; the smell of petrol, thinner, or glue on the breath; glue-specks on the hands, fingers and face; the perioral "glue-sniffer's rash" (ring of dermatitis around nose and mouth); empty tubes, plastic bags, petrol-stained clothes in the knapsack.

**Tells in the presenting child:** running sores near nose and lips; paint or correction-fluid stains; hidden tubes in pockets or school bags; chemical smell in the room or the bag; red watery eyes; the child "found dazed" at the railway platform or market by passers-by.

**Withdrawal:** short, mild and irritability-dominated, craving, agitation, headache, tremor, disturbed sleep, brief delirium in the heaviest cases; the syndrome lacks the alcohol-opioid drama, and is correspondingly undertreated; the withdrawal is never the treatment problem; the LIFE is.

**Chronic complications (the months-years bill):**
- Neurological: cognitive decline (attention, memory, school failure), cerebellar ataxia with wide-based gait, tremor, dysarthria, peripheral neuropathy of the numb-feet class, sensorineural hearing loss (the toluene literature's under-taught member)
- Renal-electrolyte: distal renal tubular acidosis, the hypokalaemic weak-or-paralysed child, recurrent "salt-wasting" presentations, kidney stones
- Marrow and other: pancytopenia/benzene-tier suppression, hepatotoxicity, lead encephalopathy with leaded-petrol sniffing (the historical and some regional pockets)
- Psychiatric: the irritability-impulsivity crust, depression, solvent-induced psychosis in heavy users, suicide attempts at tragic rates among street children

**Nitrous-oxide myelopathy:** progressive numb feet with sensory ataxia (stamping gait, worse in dark or eyes-closed), paraesthesia in hands, Lhermitte's sign, spasticity later in the cord's unfolding; often with megaloblastic blood changes, though NOT always, the striking presentations with normal B12 blood levels (functional inactivation) demand the metabolic-workup discipline (homocysteine/methylmalonic acid where available) and the index of suspicion above the level the "normal B12" generates.

## 8. Diagnosis

**The logic.** This is a clinical-diagnosis discipline: no routine Indian toxicology confirms solvent exposure in hours (toluene's metabolite testing exists in research-forensic tiers only); the nitrous screen does not exist as a screen. The diagnosis is: the population question (any street, homeless, runaway or unsupervised child; any metro teen with unexplained neuropathy-plus-party-history) + the tells (smell, stains, tubes, rash, the plastic bag in the belongings) + the syndromes (the fifteen-minute dazed drunk; the hypokalaemic weak child; the wide-based cerebellar preteen; the numb-footed whippet teen).

**Structured assessment:**
- The alone-with-the-child history (street children disclose to a kind non-judgmental questioner far more than to the accompanied interrogation; ask "what do you and your friends sniff?": the plural door opens the singular truth)
- The bag's evidence: belongings search with the child's knowledge (the tube is the diagnosis)
- Medical: potassium and acid-base (the renal-tubular screen), renal function, CBC (the marrow), liver panel, where possible a cognitive or school-function snapshot; neuro exam for the cerebellar-neuropathy bill; in the nitrous case: B12 with the metabolite-workup caveat, CBC for macrocytosis, posterior-column signs
- The child-protection screen: who are the adults, what happened at home, is there a begging-exploitation or abuse pattern, what is the school-and-shelter trajectory (this IS the treatment planning, not an optional social addendum)

**Differential table:**

| Presentation | The clue that points to inhalants |
|---|---|
| Acute dazed ataxic child at a station | Solvent smell, bag/tubes, perioral rash; vitals mostly stable |
| Sudden collapse in a healthy child mid-activity | Sniffing-context scene, sudden-death history; treat arrhythmia + suspect the sensitised heart |
| Recurrent hypokalaemic weakness/paralysis in a teen | Distal RTA pattern + sniffing tells: the toluene-renal screen |
| Progressive school failure with wide-based gait | Chronic solvent cerebellar-white-matter picture; also consider Wilson's, coeliac, alcohol |
| Numb feet + sensory ataxia in a metro teen | Nitrous history (balloons, canisters, "cream chargers"), B12-functional deficiency |
| Drowsy child with pinpoint pupils | Opioids: the street child's other emergency (naloxone logic here, not solvent logic) |

## 9. Management

**Acute intoxication:** calm, low-stimulation environment (the Hallucinogens note's craft transfers), airway watch, cardiac monitoring for the sensitised-heart window; benzodiazepines for agitation-seizures; the adrenaline-caution rule; treat the arrhythmia if it tears (the toxicology-cardiovascular tier).

**Sudden sniffing death:** the resuscitation event, CPR, defibrillation-as-available; the prevention lesson to spread (the scene pattern: bag + startle + collapse).

**The hypokalaemic child:** the emergency triangle, potassium replacement at cardiac-protected rates, acid-base correction, the diagnosis-then-education pathway; the relapse-risk framing on discharge (this child will return unless the life changes; book the child-welfare referral at the same admission).

**Withdrawal:** supportive, brief; symptom-tier benzodiazepines sparingly; the observation window for the heaviest-users' delirium.

**Chronic-treatment: the real enterprise (and no detox exists for it):**
1. **The rescue architecture**: Childline 1098 (the child-to-help line every clinician should have on the wall), the Child Welfare Committee (the statutory door for any child in need of care and protection under the JJ Act), shelter-and-children's-homes, and the NGO de-addiction-and-bridge programmes that operate street-children models (the Salaam Baalak/CONCERN-India tier; the Delhi-and-metro clinical-NGO partnerships)
2. **The ladder of the life**: safe housing → food-and-health stabilisation → schooling or vocational bridging (the NOIDA-tier bridge programmes) → individual counselling for the trauma that pre-dated the tube → group life with non-using peers → family restoration-or-alternative where home is the wound
3. **The pharmacology-free honesty**: no anti-craving agent, no substitution, no detox-detox exists for solvents; some programmes use naltrexone-tier or symptomatic adjuncts without meaningful evidence; the active ingredients are structure, attachment, meaning and time, and the outcome literature of the shelter-and-schooling models, while not randomised, is the only treatment signal this class has ever produced
4. **Cognitive-and-educational rehabilitation**: school re-entry with realistic accommodation for the solvent-fogged cognition (the developmental-disorders tier's practices); vocational training for the older street teen
5. **The medical follow-through**: electrolyte-and-renal surveillance for the RTA child until months-clean; the neuropathy-and-cerebellar course; hearing checks in the heavy chronic
6. **Nitrous pathway**: stop the gas (the only cause-removal), high-dose parenteral-then-oral B12 (the standard myelopathy regimens), neurology comanagement, the party-culture counselling that teaches the B12 mechanism to the peer group (the fact that the gas "eats the vitamin your nerves live on" travels better among teens than any warning)
- *India note*: costs (approx 2026) are almost entirely institutional-NGO rather than pharmaceutical; the shelter-month costs run in the low thousands of rupees through NGO-and-government homes; the clinic's costs are staff-time; B12 costs ₹10–50; the missing resource is beds, counsellors and bridge-schools, not medicines.

## 10. Indian practice

The Indian presentation-reality: paediatric casualties see the acute dazed child (managed-and-discharged-to-the-street; the revolving door the child-welfare referral is meant to break); physicians see the hypokalaemic-weak child as an "electrolyte mystery" (the sniffing screen is the missing habit, the tube-check in the knapsack); psychiatry sees the street teen brought by NGO workers for the irritability-psychosis-and-self-harm complications of the life; neurology-and-OPD see the whippet neuropathy mislabelled as "vitamin deficiency of dieting" or early MS until the party-history is asked. The system map for the clinician: Childline 1098 and the CWC are the statutory spine (a clinician can produce a child before the CWC directly, a power few use); shelter-and-bridge programmes are the treatment terrain; the JJ Act's child-in-need-of-care-and-protection category is the legal door for the sniffing street child (not the criminal door, inhalants sit outside NDPS, and the correction-fluid-and-glue supply to minors is regulated only by the odd state rule, patchily). Prevention practice: school-and-shelter awareness teaching with the honest mechanism (not "you will die" but "it dissolves the wiring your schooling lives on"); the stationery-shop channel advocacy (child-access limits, the industry-substitution of toluene in correction fluids where standards moved); and the NGO-partnership culture that any Indian psychiatrist in a metro can join rather than duplicate; the street-child de-addiction literature of Delhi (the AIIMS-NGO programme reports) is the national reference tier.

## 11. Clinical cases

**Case 1: the child who could not stand.** A 13-year-old rag-picker at Old Delhi railway station, brought by a volunteer: two days of progressive leg weakness, now unable to rise; three years of "dendrite" glue-sniffing with the gang of six he sleeps under the overbridge with. Potassium 2.1, bicarbonate 14, the hypokalaemic-ECG flags: the metabolic emergency treated on the cardiac clock (guarded K-correction, acidosis-management), the distal-RTA-from-toluene diagnosis named, and the treatment continuing past the electrolyte sheet, the CWC production the same week, shelter placement with the gang-mates tracked too (the group is the family; splitting it carelessly is the relapse prescription), the bridge-school assessment of the glue-fogged cognition, and the follow-up that matters: eight months later in the shelter's Class 7, potassium normal at three checks, gait intact, sniffing-episodes twice-relapsed-then-contained, the honest trajectory of the only treatment this class owns. Teaching points: hypokalaemic weakness in a street child = the sniffing screen; the electrolyte is the emergency, the life is the disease; the CWC referral is a clinical act like any other.

**Case 2: the laughing gas that stopped the feet.** A 19-year-old private-college student in Pune: four months of nightly whippet sessions (the canister box bought online "for whipped cream, obviously"), then numb soles, buzzing fingertips, stamping walk in the dark, the electric-shock down the spine when she looks down; B12 level reads "low-normal": the inactivation-trap that misleads; homocysteine (ordered on the mechanism's suspicion) elevated; the spine's posterior-column story on exam. Stop-the-gas, parenteral-then-oral B12, twelve weeks: gait steadied, Lhermitte gone, the soles' map returning; the peer-group counselling session where she teaches her own friends the cobalt mechanism, the class's best prevention poster. Teaching points: "normal B12" does not retire the diagnosis in the gas-user; the whippet history must be asked in every teen neuropathy; this is the addiction-injury with a near-complete recovery arc when caught, a clinical pearl to enjoy.

## 13. FAQ

- *"How can a glue be a drug, it is stationery, no?"*: To the brain it is an instant fat-dissolving intoxicant that reaches it in seconds from the lungs; the shop's category does not matter to the nerve cells; that is why children who cannot buy a cigarette can buy a tube.
- *"Is it very dangerous, will one sniff kill?"*: One session CAN kill suddenly (the heart's rhythm is made unstable to any fright or chase for those minutes) but the more common ruin is slower: the dissolved wiring of school-age learning and the kidneys' salt-drain. Urgent, yes; usually gradual, that is the trap.
- *"He is only twelve, surely he will grow out of it?"*: Twelve is not protection; this class has the youngest ages and the sharpest escalation of any drug; the tube that fills an empty afternoon becomes the day's structure in months. Waiting is the one plan that reliably fails.
- *"The shelter people say he sniffed again, is the treatment not working?"*: Relapse on the street-child path is the rule of the arc, not its failure, the treatment counts by the months in shelter-and-school, not by the single relapse; the gang-mates matter too; we treat the circle, not only the boy.
- *"My daughter uses the cream-charger gas at parties, is that not harmless fun?"*: The gas inactivates the vitamin her nerves' insulation is built with: months of party-nights can leave the feet numb and the walk unsteady. It is the most treatable of drug injuries (caught, stopped, supplemented) and the damage is quiet until the stairs go dark.
- *"Should we punish, or hospitalise?"*: Punishment adds the street to the cause; hospitalisation treats a week of a lifelong condition; the treatment is protection-and-schooling; the child-welfare door is the prescription.
- *"Why does he keep sniffing when it is making his body weak?"*: Because it fills the exact hole the childhood left, hunger, fear, and nobody; the sniffing is the symptom of that hole, and the shelter-and-school is the medicine for it. Blaming the tube is blaming the bandage for the wound.
- *"Can the brain recover?"*: Substantially, if the solvent stops young and the schooling restarts, the young brain rebuilds what the dissolving took; the years-long heavy sniffing of the grown street adult rebuilds less. The urgency is real, and so is the hope.
