# Conduct Disorder & Oppositional Defiant Disorder

## 1. In one line + overview

**Conduct disorder is a pattern of violating others' basic rights and serious social rules (aggression, destruction, deceit, theft, major rule-breaking) that begins as a developmental behavioural story, not a moral verdict; treatment works through the SYSTEM around the child (parenting, family, school, peers) far more than through the child alone, and the child with callous-unemotional traits needs a different, longer, carefully warmed-up approach.**

Understand the ladder first. **Oppositional defiant disorder (ODD)** is the lower rung: anger, argument, defiance toward authority, vindictiveness, exhausting, conflict-filled, but WITHOUT aggression that harms others, theft, or law violations. It is common (roughly 3–6% of children), often improves with parent training, and in most children does NOT progress further. **Conduct disorder (CD)** is the upper rung: the child or adolescent who fights with weapons, cruelly treats people or animals, deliberately destroys property, lies and cons, steals, runs away, or seriously breaks major age rules (school truancy, staying out nights, illegal acts before the courts). Around 2–4% of adolescents meet CD criteria, boys more in the childhood-onset form, girls more in relational-aggression and runaway presentations. The two great prognostic splits: **childhood-onset** (before 10; typically with harsh parenting, ADHD, impulsivity) runs the higher risk of adult antisocial personality disorder; **adolescent-onset** (peer-group and situation driven) mostly desists as the group and the stakes change. And the specifier that changes the plan: **limited prosocial emotions** (guilt-less, empathy-less, shallow affect, unmoved by punishment) which predicts a harder, longer course and demands intervention designed for it. India sees these children through the Juvenile Justice system, observation homes, school expulsions and street life; the clinical window is usually BEFORE the courts, and most Indian clinicians meet them too late.

## 2. Learning objectives

After this note you can:
- Distinguish ODD from CD on the single axis that matters: violation of rights/aggression vs defiance alone
- Apply the DSM-5/ICD-11 symptom-band logic (aggression/destruction/deceit-theft/rule violation) with the 12/6-month duration gates
- Use the two prognostic splits (childhood- vs adolescent-onset, and the limited-prosocial-emotions specifier) to set expectations and plan intensity
- Explain the four mechanism stories: coercive family cycles, hostile attribution bias, the fear-and-punishment learning curve of callous traits, peer contagion
- Deliver the treatment hierarchy honestly: parent management training and family-based multi-system approaches carry the evidence; boot camps and fear-based institutions are iatrogenic
- Treat the comorbidities that drive the aggression (ADHD above all; PTSD; substance; mood) and know the small, defined role of medication
- Navigate the Indian layer: JJ Act 2015 pathway, observation homes, Childline, school discipline, and the poverty/abuse realities that sit under many "conduct" labels
- Give families the long-run numbers without either false hope or doom; including the girl's runaway pattern hiding abuse until asked

## 4. Epidemiology

**Global.** ODD prevalence ~3–6% (age 8–12 band, fairly stable across countries that measure it); CD ~2–4% of adolescents (higher in boys:childhood-onset, converging in adolescent-onset; urban and deprived settings run higher). Longitudinal truth: of childhood-onset CD cases followed to adulthood, a substantial minority (estimates commonly a third to half, depending on the cohort and the severity filter) meet antisocial personality disorder criteria; adolescent-onset CD mostly does NOT. Most children with ODD never develop CD; the ones who do usually have ADHD, harsh parenting, and a family aggression culture stacked underneath. Girls' CD presents later, more relationally (exclusion, rumour-warfare, sexualised exploitation, running away) and is under-referred everywhere.

**India.** No national prevalence survey names CD; school-based and juvenile-justice data mark the clinically visible: NCRB's juvenile-crime statistics (children in conflict with law, tens of thousands of cases yearly, overwhelmingly boys, with the classic offence mix of theft and hurt), observation-home populations that are dominated by poverty, orphanhood, street life and abuse rather than "disorder" alone, and school expulsions arriving at child-guidance clinics as behaviour emergencies. The Indian detection funnel runs BACKWARD: the police see what the clinic should have seen earlier. The system's official pathway is the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection) Act 2015; a child in conflict with law is processed by the Juvenile Justice Board, placed (if needed) in an observation home, and in principle diverted to rehabilitation; the clinical truth is that mental health services meet these children only when someone thinks to refer, which is rare, and the abuse/trauma layer under girls' and street-children's "conduct" is the single most missed diagnosis in this population.

## 5. Causes & risk factors

**Genetic / biological**
- Moderate heritability of aggression/impulsivity (the ADHD/impulsivity link is the main genetic channel; CD as such is less heritable than its engine parts)
- Low resting heart rate (the classic psychophysiological correlate of fearlessness in callous traits)
- Testosterone-loaded adolescence amplifies the aggression channel; traumatic brain injury and lead exposure add real risk; prenatal substance exposure

**Neurodevelopmental / psychological**
- ADHD in up to half of childhood-onset CD, impulsivity supplies the ignition
- Hostile attribution bias: neutral faces read as hostile, the "he started it" cognition
- Callous-unemotional trait path: weak fear conditioning and empathy learning (the amygdala's threat-response lineage), reward-dominant decision style
- Low verbal ability: words are the conflict-exit tool; without them, fists fill the gap

**Family / social (the biggest and most actionable layer)**
- Coercive parenting cycles: harsh punishment → escalation → parental capitulation at peak screaming → the child learns escalation WORKS (the well-replicated mechanism)
- Physical abuse, neglect, domestic violence (violence-model + trauma-anger effects in one)
- Parental substance misuse, criminality, untreated mental illness; inconsistent supervision (the "absent-parenting" axis: street exposure does the parenting)
- Peer contagion in adolescent-onset CD (deviant-peer reinforcement, gang recruitment economics in slums and street hierarchies)
- Poverty, urban crowding, school exclusion: selection-pressure, not destiny

**Indian context**
- Corporal punishment as the culturally-approved response trains exactly the coercive cycle the textbooks describe; the parents are often running the parenting programme they received
- Child labour and street survival economies reward exploitation-skills ("conduct" as adaptation to the observation-home-to-street pipeline)
- Substance-on-street (glue/inhalants first, cannabis later) both mimics and deepens CD; screen every street-referred child
- The runaway girl: trafficking and sexual abuse ride under "conduct problem, immoral character" labels, the assessment must invert the moral frame entirely

## 6. What happens in the body/brain

**Story 1: the coercive dance.** Watch the videos of these families and you see the same dance: the parent commands, the child refuses, the parent commands louder, the child escalates, the parent explodes (or gives in), and BOTH are rewarded. The child who screams loudest gets the parent to quit; the parent who hits hardest gets five minutes of peace. The dance trains itself over a thousand evenings. The treatment insight follows directly: change the STEPS, not the dancers' characters, parent management training teaches the parent to reward the first compliance (micro, immediate), to ignore the escalation window, and to apply short consistent consequences that never arrive at the peak of anger.

**Story 2: the hostile lens.** For a subset of these children, an ambiguous bump in the corridor arrives pre-labelled as an insult. When you measure their reading of neutral faces, the threat-detector is set too hot; aggression feels like self-defence from the inside. Cognitive restructuring can be taught late, but the lesson is also preventive: the child who entered school with language delay and slapped instead of spoke built the hostile lens through a thousand conflicts that began as WORD-failures. This is why treating ADHD, language disorder and learning disorders early is real conduct prevention.

**Story 3: the cold engine (the callous-unemotional path).** Some children arrive with a muted alarm system: punishment doesn't sting, fear doesn't teach, other children's distress doesn't inhibit them. Their aggression is instrumental (to GET things), not reactive (from heat), cool, planned, and guilt-free. The developmental neuroscience lineage ties this to an under-responsive threat system; the prognosis is more guarded, and the treatment must be different in design: consistent consequences WITHOUT the affective escalation (these children don't learn from anger, they learn from predictability), warmth-coaching for parents (attachment-based interventions, paradoxically MORE important here), and reward-based approaches with tight structure. The adult "psychopath" label is NOT a diagnosis for a child; the specifier language exists precisely to give this profile a NON-verdict name in minors.

**Story 4: the street school.** For the street/observation-home child, "conduct" is partly a school: an economy where aggression is the resume, exploitation the salary, and trust the liability. Healing the behaviour without changing the economy fails (return-to-street relapse); the evidence programmes (multisystemic therapy) work precisely by rebuilding the WHOLE ecosystem (family, school, peers, livelihood) around the child. India's scarcity of such programmes is a systems-level wound, but their logic can be approximated: one committed case-worker, family engagement, and a school/employer willing to take the risk.

## 7. Symptoms

**The four DSM/ICD symptom bands (patient-language):**
1. **Aggression to people/animals**: bullying, initiating fights, weapon use in fights (the escalation marker), cruelty to people, cruelty to animals (a serious-signal symptom in children), robbery/confrontation theft, sexual coercion (never normal, always investigated)
2. **Property destruction**: deliberate fire-setting (risk-assessment symptom), deliberate destruction (smashing homes, school property, vehicles)
3. **Deceit/theft**: breaking-in, con-artistry for goods/favours, shoplifting beyond occasional (the pattern, not the incident)
4. **Serious rule violation**: parental-age-inappropriate staying out nights, running away twice+ (the abuse screen triggers here, running away FROM something), truancy before 13, and law violations that reach the Juvenile Justice Board

**ODD's five-symptom band (the lower rung):** loses temper, touchy/annoyed, defies/refuses, deliberately annoys, blames others, spiteful/vindictive, anger-shaped, authority-shaped, NO aggression/deceit/theft. The child with ODD argues for hours but does not break the neighbour's window.

**Presentations that need special attention:**
- The younger aggression pattern: tantrum-era escalation that never civilised by school age; ADHD-impulsive ignition; poor frustration tolerance
- The adolescent group pattern: theft-and-riding gangs, territory fights, substance-tagging; behaviour occurs WITH the group, largely absent alone (prognostically the better shape)
- The relational-aggression girl: exclusion campaigns, rumour-warfare, sexualised reputation-attacks, then the runaway, the standard missed CD presentation
- The callous presentation: calm eyes while describing harm; no guilt language; punishment-fatigue; the parents say "nothing affects him", including, poignantly, love
- Substance-riding conduct: glue/cannabis era begins with changes in FRIENDS first (new phone-less contacts, hostel-ward disappearances)

**The red-flag top five (act immediately):** cruelty to animals, fire-setting with fascination, sexual aggression, running away FROM home (ask what is being escaped), and any weapon use; each changes risk assessment and reporting duties.

## 8. Diagnosis

**The logic (DSM-5/ICD-11 paraphrased):** for CD, at least 3 symptoms across the four bands in the past 12 months with at least 1 in the past 6 months; impairment (social/academic); specify childhood- vs adolescent-onset; specify the limited-prosocial-emotions profile (2 of 4: lack of guilt, callous lack of empathy, unconcern about performance, shallow/deficient affect, only from multiple sources over time, not one sulky interview); severity (mild/moderate/severe by count and harm). For ODD, at least 4 symptoms of the defiant band for 6 months with impairment. ICD-11 aligns closely, with the equivalent prosocial-emotions qualifier.

**The assessment structure (the part that changes everything):**
1. **Symptom mapping from TWO sources** (home and school/observation-home; teacher report for the school-only pattern)
2. **The abuse screen, mandatory**: physical, sexual, domestic violence, online exploitation; girls' running away and "immorality" labels are inverted here; the assessors' oath is: behaviour that shocks must trigger questions, not judgments
3. **Developmental and family map**: milestones, language delay, learning screen (a big minority of CD children have undiagnosed learning disorders, the school-failure → deviant-peer pipeline entry), parenting style inventory, parental substance/mental illness, the extended-family's aggression history
4. **Comorbidity hunt (drives the plan)**: ADHD (ignition), PTSD (trauma-driven aggression; presentation is reactive, triggered, dissociative), substance use (channel and disinhibitor), mood disorders (irritable bipolar/DMDD-spectrum; DMDD is for chronic irritable NON-episodic mood, not instrumentality), autism's rigidity crises (rule-violations from incomprehension, not calculation)
5. **Intelligence/learning testing** where indicated; **speech-language screen** (the words-are-the-exit-tool finding)
6. **The environment audit**: supervision gaps, street hours, peer economy, the child's income sources (theft can be a LIVING, plan the replacement), school's standing offer (suspended? last-chance notice?)
7. **Legal-positioning history**: JJ Board contacts, observation-home stays (and what happened there; institutional abuse is real and re-traumatising)

### Differential table

| Looks like CD/ODD | The distinguishing feature |
|---|---|
| ADHD impulsivity | Fast, unaimed, regretted acts; no planning or profit; parents describe accidents and heat, not con-artistry |
| PTSD/trauma aggression | Trigger-linked, hypervigilance/startle, dissociation; the history does the separating (screen ALL "CD" children) |
| Substance-fuelled behaviour | Onset tracks substance; intoxication windows; intoxicant screen positive |
| Bipolar disorder | Episodic euphoria/decreased-sleep grandiosity between outbursts; family history; sustained states, not character |
| DMDD | Chronic severe irritability with tantrums across contexts, mood-spectrum, no instrumentality or profit |
| Autism crises | Fixed-interest/preference rigidity, sensory triggers, social incomprehension; the "rule violation" lacks predatory shape |
| Adjustment disorder | Reaction bounded to a recent identifiable stressor, ≤6 months post-onset, no pre-existing pattern |
| Street-survival adaptation (not disorder) | Behaviour confined to survival economy, absent in protected contexts, no pre-street pattern; the "CD" label is an occupational injury of poverty |
| ODD | Defiance band only, no rights violation, no aggression/deceit/theft |

## 9. Management

**The frame: CD is treated through systems; ODD is treated through the parent-child dance; and the callous profile is treated with a different manual; mis-prescribing the manual is the commonest treatment error.**

**1: Parent management training (the first-line, for both ODD and childhood-CD).**
- The core behavioural package: immediate contingent praise for compliance (the 5:1 attention ratio), effective commands (one at a time, stated positively, within arm's reach, backed up within seconds), planned ignoring of negative-attention behaviour, consistent short consequences (response cost, brief time-out) delivered CALMLY at low-intensity moments, a home token economy, and supervision architecture (the "where-who-when" knowledge of the child's day)
- Dose honesty: 8–20 structured sessions minimum; single-session advice fails; group-based parent programmes (the Incredible Years/PMTO lineages) are the best-value delivery
- *India note: formal programmes are metro-concentrated; the clinical approximation (4–6 clinic-based parent-coaching sessions using the package above with weekly phone follow-up) is far from nothing, and the grandparents need the same training (the inconsistency between generations is the Indian programme-killer; the "he obeys only when grandfather is home" report is the fingerprint).*

**2: Family and multisystem approaches for CD (the evidence core for moderate-to-severe CD).**
- Multisystemic therapy (MST): the best-evidenced intensive programme, a case-worker with 3–5 families, working IN the home/school/peer ecology for 3–5 months; the outcomes in trials are strong (reduced re-arrest and out-of-home placement)
- Functional family therapy (FFT): 12–14 sessions working the family's interaction functions beneath the behaviour
- Treatment foster care (the Oregon model): a specialised foster family as the intervention (for children whose home cannot be made safe fast enough)
- *India note: certified MST/FFT programmes barely exist in India; the honest approximation is a "case-manager model": one counsellor (NGO, school, or district mental-health programme) who does the family-work, school liaison, peer-plan and follow-up across 3–6 months, with Childline (1098), the JJ Board's probation officers, and local NGOs as the network. Write the plan like an MST skeleton and staff it with whoever can hold it.*

**3: School and peer interventions.**
- The school behaviour plan: report-card system, check-in/check-out mentoring, anti-bullying enforcement BOTH directions (CD children are also bullied)
- Deviant-peer DECONTAMINATION: supervised after-school sport/occupation substitution (idle peer-hours are the relapse window); vocational skill-building for adolescents (a wage replaces theft)
- Screen-time and gang-channel management; for street-children: the pathway is re-enrolment + shelter + de-addiction, and the order matters

**4: Medication (the honest small role).**
- No drug treats CD itself; medication treats the DRIVERS: ADHD (stimulants/atomoxetine, treating ADHD in CD children measurably reduces aggression: the strongest pharmacological evidence in this field), severe explosive aggression unresponsive to structure (risperidone has short-term evidence in extremely dysregulated presentations, weight/endocrine monitoring, lowest dose, defined review; an Indian practice caution: do NOT let risperidone become the boarding-school discipline tool), PTSD (trauma therapy; SSRIs for comorbid anxiety), substance use disorders (the relevant note)
- The "anger-control syrup" or "behaviour tonic" from the local pharmacy: placebo commerce, say so plainly

**5: The callous-profile plan (the different manual).**
- Consequences must be PREDICTABLE and consistent, not emotional (anger teaches nothing here; predictability does)
- Reward-heavy structure: earn privileges visibly, lose them without drama
- Parent warmth-coaching: these children's empathy needs explicit cultivation, modelling, labelling emotions, perspective-taking games (the attachment-based interventions developed for this profile)
- Longer horizon, smaller increments; protect the family from burnout with respite and realistic milestone-setting
- Anticipatory guidance on legality: better outcome data exist for early intervention than for any later-period rescue

**6: The girl who ran (the inverted pathway).**
- The protocol: safety first (where does she sleep tonight; trafficking risk assessment), abuse disclosure work at her pace (the Child Trauma note's rules: one skilled interview, no repeated interrogation, no promises you can't keep), then trauma therapy, THEN the behaviour work, and the family's handling of her "character" (the honour lecture) is itself a treatment target
- Medical care: pregnancy/STI screen in relevant contexts with consent protocols, injuries documented

**7: Institutional care (the last resort with a warning label).**
- Observation/residential homes only when home is unsafe or unbuildable, and with active mental-health input (institutionalisation without treatment deepens the CD course)
- Boot camps, "military academies" and fear-based programmes: the evidence is not just absent; several reviews show harm (abuse rates, suicide risk, post-traumatic outcomes). Indian parents pay for these abroad and domestically; the clinical duty is to say NO clearly
- Juvenile Justice pathway navigation: the JJ Board's rehabilitation mandate, diversion where possible, the child's right to education within institutions, and the mental-health assessment's role in the Board's decisions

*India note (approx 2026): the free/low-cost spine: district mental-health programme psychologist, JJ Board probation officers, observation-home visiting psychiatry (where it exists), NGO networks (Childline 1098 for street/abuse referrals), school counsellor for the school-plan half. Private conduct-disorder programme packages in metros cost ₹50,000–2,00,000+ (wildly variable, evidence-opaque), before paying, ask for the named model (PMT/MST-logic/FFT-logic) and its measurable targets.*

## 10. Indian practice

- **The funnel runs through the wrong doors.** India's CD children pass first through the teacher's stick, then the school's expulsion, then the police, then the observation home, and only accidentally through a clinic. The highest-yield system intervention is a standing referral arrangement: every expelled child and every first-time JJ-Board child gets a mental-health assessment (ADHD, learning disorder, trauma, substance, the four drivers). One letter to a school board or a District Magistrate's office can institutionalise that funnel; clinicians who have done it report the highest career-yield of any intervention.
- **The punishment culture needs reframing, not contempt.** The parents who beat the child are usually running the programme THEY were raised in; the sting-in-the-tail sentence for the clinic: "the beatings have bought you this: he no longer fears the stick; you have completed the fear-training, and the next teacher who raises a hand becomes just another person to defeat." Then offer the actual programme.
- **The observation-home reality.** Conditions range from custodial to genuinely rehabilitative; the mental-health professional's roles there: screen for trauma (it is nearly universal), diagnose the drivers, treat substance withdrawal, protect the youngest from exploitation inside, and write assessments that the JJ Board can actually use for disposition decisions.
- **The marriage-market immunity of boys' aggression.** "Boys will be boys" delays help by years: the Std-6 weapon-carrying boy has a childhood-onset presentation that the family narrates as normal male energy. The counter-frame that lands: "the same aggression that reads as boyhood at 12 reads as criminal record at 16; that is the road."
- **The domestic-violence engine.** A large share of Indian CD sits on a father-to-mother violence base: the son's aggression is the curriculum he watched. Screen every CD family; treat the violence (safety planning, the mother's own mental-health care) or the child's programme competes with his home curriculum every night.
- **The school's last chance.** Indian schools hold expulsive power and use it without clinical input. The clinician's letter converting expulsion into "final-probation WITH a behaviour plan" is a life-trajectory intervention; keep a template.
- **The Dalit/poverty/tribal over-representation caution.** Street-round-ups and "conduct" labels fall disproportionately on poor, migrant and marginalised children whose behaviour is survival adaptation; the clinical assessment must include the "is this disorder or economy?" question (the differential table's street-survival row); otherwise the clinic becomes the system's moral cover.

## 11. Clinical cases (de-identified)

**Case 1: the boy with the bicycle gang.** A 13-year-old Nashik boy arrived at the district child-guidance clinic with a police warning slip: third incident of bicycle theft with a peer group, plus a two-year school history of bullying juniors, and, the mother's whispered list, "he cut the neighbour's puppy's ear last year with his friends, and when I asked, he smiled." Fighting since Std 3 (childhood-onset pattern), suspended twice; the father: "I beat him till my hand hurts; he looks at me like I am wasting his time." No guilt language in the interview; school report: "charming when he wants something; the marks of a leader are visible; uses juniors to do the stealing." Assessment: ADHD screen negative; cruelty-to-animal and weapon-adjacent pattern plus the 12-month symptom-band counts met CD criteria with the LIMITED PROSOCIAL EMOTIONS specifier (guilt-absent, empathy-absent, unconcerned about performance, shallow charm); the family audit: father harsh-then-capitulating, domestic shouting model, parents both at work till 9 pm (the 5–10 pm unsupervised street window was the entire offence timetable). Management: the different manual, stated as such to the parents, predictable consequences without affect (the father re-trained from "louder stick" to "calm loss-of-privilege ledger"), visible reward economy (the cricket-bat earn-plan), supervision-architecture rebuild (tuition-to-dinner chain closed the window; the mother returned to early-shift work), school behaviour plan with check-in mentoring (the school converted expulsion to final-probation on the clinic's letter), deviant-peer substitution via the municipal cricket academy (a wage-replacement plan: earned pocket money matched to the theft-average), and warmth-coaching for both parents with explicit empathy-cultivation homework (emotion-labelling at dinner, films with perspective-taking discussion). Twelve months: no police contact, two school-incident-free terms; the "puppy" conversation had a second run at month 9 (he said the neighbour's dog had cost him a friend's respect, the instrumental logic visible, the guilt still thin, the trajectory bent but the follow-up map kept long). Teaching points: (1) the childhood-onset + callous profile called the DIFFERENT manual on day one; (2) the parents' beatings were the fear-training's completion, not the treatment; (3) closing the unsupervised window did more than all consequences; (4) the wage-replacement economics of theft; (5) honest prognosis with a maintained follow-up horizon, bent, not finished.

**Case 2: the girl who was labelled, not asked.** A 15-year-old from a peri-urban Bhopal settlement was referred from an observation home after her fourth runaway episode; school reports called her "morally corrupted, leads younger girls astray"; the home's register said "conduct disorder, repeat absconder, manipulative." The referral psychiatrist's intake inverted the frame: safety assessment, then the abuse screen, a slow, single-session, non-leading disclosure protocol. Over two sessions: the stepfather's abuse since 12 (mother's work-hours), the flight-to-a-friend's-house each time (never to the streets; the "runaway" pattern was escape, not exploitation, though trafficking vulnerability was rising each round), and the "leading girls astray"; she had taken a younger cousin along on the last escape (protective from the inside, delinquent from the register). Assessment: PTSD (nightmares, hypervigilance, the reactivity pattern), secondary conduct behaviours (lying and absconding as survival tools), no CD antecedents before 12 (pre-morbid pattern absent, the adolescent-onset-looking shape with the trauma engine). Management: safety first (maternal-aunt placement supported; the stepfather case filed under POCSO, mandatory reporting applies), trauma-focused CBT over 14 sessions (the dissociation and nightmare work first), the mother's own counselling (the betrayal's handling, support rather than blame preserved the placement), re-enrolment in an open-schooling route with a woman mentor-teacher, the register's label formally revised (the "manipulative" entry removed; documentation advocacy is treatment), and no medication except brief sleep-phase melatonin. One year: no further absconsion; school records positive; the nightmares at "monthly, not nightly". Teaching points: (1) the girl's "conduct" was the symptom of the thing done TO her; the abuse screen is mandatory, not optional; (2) "running away" must always be read as FROM; (3) one skilled disclosure interview, never repeated interrogation; (4) re-labelling the register is a clinical act; the observation home's paperwork follows the child; (5) the single best predictor of her outcome was the placement question, safety first, everything else second.

## 13. FAQ: asked the way parents and caregivers ask it

**"Is my child a psychopath?"** No: that word is not a childhood diagnosis and should never be used about a child. What you may be describing is the limited-prosocial-emotions profile, reduced guilt and empathy, which is a real, nameable pattern with a treatment approach built for it. It carries a harder road, not a verdict; children with this profile improve with the right consistency-based programme, especially when started early.

**"Will he become a criminal?"** The honest spread: adolescent-onset behaviour with the peer group mostly fades with maturation; childhood-onset with the callous pattern has a materially higher risk of adult antisocial problems. What bends it: treating the drivers (attention problems, trauma, substance), the parent programme, supervision, and keeping him in school. We are managing odds, and the odds are movable.

**"We beat him and nothing works. What now?"** The beatings are part of the machinery keeping this going; they taught him escalation and are now completing his fear-training. The programme that works replaces anger with consistency: small immediate consequences he can predict, rewards that arrive before he escalates, and parents who mean it calmly. It feels counter-intuitive; the trials support it.

**"Is it the company he keeps?"** Partly, genuinely: for the adolescent-onset pattern, peers are the engine. But the fix is not just forbidding friends (that launches a war you lose). It is substitution: supervised activities that occupy the same evening hours, plus the family work, plus keeping him in school. Where a deviant peer group is supplying money and status, you have to replace the money and status, not just lecture about them.

**"Is there a medicine for this?"** No medicine treats conduct disorder itself. Medicines treat what is DRIVING it in a given child: attention-deficit (the best-evidenced: treating ADHD reduces the aggression), trauma, substance, mood. If someone prescribes a "behaviour syrup", that is commerce, not medicine.

**"Should we send him to a strict boarding school / military-style academy?"** The evidence says these fear-based institutions do not just fail; several reviews show harm, including abuse and worsened trauma. If the home cannot hold him, the evidence tier is treatment-oriented foster placement with therapy. A "strict" environment without treatment is just a more organised place to get worse.

**"She keeps running away; she is spoilt and stubborn."** A runaway is running FROM something as often as TO something. In girls especially, repeated absconding should always trigger an abuse assessment; home is the first suspect, not the last. We ask carefully, once, and protect first. If abuse is confirmed, the "conduct problem" usually turns out to be a normal child escaping an abnormal situation.

**"He is cruel to animals, is that a phase?"** No. Animal cruelty is a serious signal, not a phase: it marks the high-risk end of the aggression spectrum and warrants immediate professional assessment. It is also assessable and treatable, but it goes to the front of the queue, not the watch-and-wait pile.

**"The school has given a last warning. What do we do?"** Turn it into the plan's lever: ask the school for final probation WITH a written behaviour plan (we will write it) instead of expulsion; seating and mentoring adjustments, a daily report card, check-in points. Schools accept this more often than parents expect, and staying IN school is one of the strongest protective factors this child has.

**"How long does treatment take?"** Longer than families want to hear: months of programme work, follow-up in years. ODD often improves within 8–20 structured sessions; CD needs the family-system work plus the school and peer reconstruction. We set small dated targets, first: no police contact for 90 days; then: one incident-free term. Progress in this condition is measured in quiet weeks, not transformations.

## 14. Exam & student lens

**The one-line band structure:** CD = 3+ symptoms across aggression/destruction/deceit-theft/rule-violation (12-month frame, 1+ in 6 months) with impairment; ODD = 4+ defiant-band symptoms (6 months) without rights violation; both with onset subtypes; CD's specifier: limited prosocial emotions (guilt-less, empathy-poor, performance-unconcern, shallow affect; needs multi-source longitudinal evidence).

**Mnemonic, "The A-D-D-R bands":** Aggression, Destruction, Deceit-theft, Rule violation (conduct's four bands); ODD remembered as "the angry four + one": touchy, defiant, annoying, blaming, vindictive.

**High-yield facts:**
- Childhood-onset CD: male-predominant, ADHD comorbidity ~50%, harsh-parenting association, higher adult ASPD risk (a third to half in severe cohorts); adolescent-onset: better prognosis, peer-driven, mostly desists
- Most ODD never becomes CD; the progression stack = ODD + ADHD + harsh parenting + family aggression culture
- Cruelty to animals and fire-setting = the serious-signal pair; runaway = abuse-screen trigger
- Treatment evidence hierarchy: parent management training → MST/FFT-style family-system work → treatment foster care; BOOT CAMPS: harmful (the exam favourite)
- Pharmacology: no drug for CD; treating comorbid ADHD = the best-evidenced drug-reduction of aggression; risperidone's short-term role in severe explosive dysregulation with monitoring caution
- Low resting heart rate = the classic callous-trait correlate (physiology viva favourite)
- Girls' CD: relational aggression + runaway presentations, abuse under-layer, under-referred
- Indian legal layer: JJ Act 2015 (Juvenile Justice Board, observation home, rehabilitation mandate; mental-health assessment's role)

**Classic traps:**
1. Calling street-survival adaptation a conduct disorder (economy, not psychopathology)
2. Missing trauma/PTSD under reactive aggression: the abuse screen skipped in boys too
3. The "manipulative girl" label hiding trafficking/abuse
4. Prescribing the standard anger-escalation parenting to the callous profile (the manual mismatch)
5. Risperidone as boarding-school discipline
6. Mistaking DMDD or bipolar irritability for CD instrumentality
7. Expulsion-endorsing letters from clinics (the opposite of the treatment plan)

**Indian exam corner:** expected patterns, "a 12-year-old with fighting, stealing and truancy: diagnosis, differential, management"; "conduct disorder with limited prosocial emotions: specifier and its significance"; "ODD vs CD differentiation"; "juvenile delinquency and mental illness: the JJ Act's rehabilitation framework". Indian-context marks: the expulsion-to-clinic referral funnel idea, Childline 1098, observation-home mental-health roles, domestic-violence engine, the boot-camp-harm warning.

## 16. Self-test MCQs

**1. The single axis separating ODD from CD:**
A. Age of onset
B. Violation of others' rights / aggression (present in CD, absent in ODD)
C. Gender ratio
D. Duration of symptoms
**Answer: B.** Defiance can be exhausting; rights violation and aggression promote it to CD.

**2. The CD specifier that deepens the treatment plan:**
A. With anxiety
B. With limited prosocial emotions (guilt-less, empathy-poor, shallow affect)
C. With hyperactivity
D. With psychosis
**Answer: B.** The callous profile calls the different manual: predictable consequences, reward-heavy structure, warmth-coaching.

**3. The best-evidenced pharmacological route to reducing aggression in conduct problems:**
A. Treat comorbid ADHD with stimulants/atomoxetine
B. Antipsychotic polypharmacy
C. Benzodiazepine courses
D. Mood-stabiliser routine for all CD
**Answer: A.** The ignition treatment, no drug treats CD itself.

**4. Childhood-onset (before 10) CD, compared with adolescent-onset:**
A. Runs a lower risk of adult antisocial personality disorder
B. Carries higher ADHD comorbidity and higher adult antisocial risk
C. Always remits by 18
D. Is more common in girls
**Answer: B.** The prognostic split every exam loves.

**5. A 15-year-old girl presents after her fourth runaway episode, labelled "manipulative". The mandatory next step:**
A. Behaviour modification for absconding
B. Abuse/trauma screen with a single skilled disclosure interview and safety assessment
C. Immediate residential placement
D. Family lecture on discipline
**Answer: B.** Running away is FROM something until proven otherwise; girls' "conduct" hides abuse at rates that make the screen non-negotiable.

**6. Parents ask about a "military-style boot camp" they saw advertised. The evidence-based reply:**
A. Effective if strict enough
B. Evidence shows harm, abuse rates, trauma, worsened outcomes; treatment-oriented alternatives only

**Answer: B.** The harm evidence is the exam point and the clinic's clear NO.

**7. The physiological correlate of the callous-unemotional profile:**
A. Elevated resting heart rate
B. Low resting heart rate with under-responsive threat system
C. Hypertension
D. Tachycardia on standing
**Answer: B.** The fearlessness correlate: physiology viva classic.

**8. Under India's Juvenile Justice Act 2015, a child in conflict with law is:**
A. Tried as an adult without exception
B. Processed by the Juvenile Justice Board with a rehabilitation mandate and observation-home placement where needed
C. Institutionalised without mental-health assessment

**Answer: B.** The Board's rehabilitative frame: the clinic's assessments feed its decisions, and education continues in custody.
