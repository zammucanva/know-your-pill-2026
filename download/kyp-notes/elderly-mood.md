# Mood Disorders in the Elderly

## 1. In one line + overview

**Depression in old age is common, dangerous, chronically under-treated (and often misread as "just ageing": the elder who stops eating, stops walking, complains of body pains and "becoming a burden" is not fading naturally but running a treatable illness) while two geriatric signatures demand special skill: the "pseudodementia" mask (depression imitating dementia, reversible with treatment) and late-onset mania (which is often SECONDARY (a stroke, a thyroid storm, a medicine) and demands a workup before a label).**

The Indian numbers say this plainly: the National Mental Health Survey 2015–16 found depression in roughly one in twelve elderly Indians, higher in urban metros, with a vast treatment gap; international geriatric surveys run similar or higher among the medically ill. Yet the default Indian reading is "old age weakness" (budhapa, kamzori, tension), the elder's three favourite disguises, and the family's three favourite dismissals. The clinical differences that matter: elderly depression presents more through the BODY (pain, weakness, appetite loss, sleep inversion) and through PSYCHOMOTOR change (the slowed, stooped, sighing elder; or the agitated wringer-of-hands) than through weeping sadness; guilt and nihilism arrive intact ("I am a burden; the family would be free without me" (the sentence that is a suicide-risk flag in this population, with the elderly dying by suicide at higher completion rates than the young) see the Elderly Suicide note, M8, for that half). The "pseudodementia" presentation (the apparent dementia that is actually depression's retrieval-freeze) is the diagnosis whose miss costs a person's remaining function, and whose catch restores it. And the treatment laws are geriatric: antidepressants work well in elders but with elder-specific cautions (SSRI hyponatraemia, falls, interactions), the eventual dose must be FULL not timid ("start low, go slow, but GO"), ECT is often the best medicine this population has for melancholic and psychotic depression, and late-onset mania is a brain-workup before it is a bipolar label.

## 2. Learning objectives

After this note you can:
- Recognise elderly depression through its costumes: somatic, psychomotor, "burden-talk", the pseudodementia mask, and the apathy that mimics "just ageing"
- Differentiate depressive pseudodementia from true dementia at the bedside (the ten-clue set), and know the rule that the differentiation is also made by TREATMENT RESPONSE
- Explain the vascular-depression story and the loss-stack story (why late-life depression clusters with strokes, and with bereavement-relocation-illness stacks)
- Screen every elderly depression for suicide risk (the elderly lethality pattern) and for cognitive, medical, medication and sensory co-riders
- Prescribe antidepressants in elders with the geriatric discipline: SSRI first (sertraline/escitalopram), the hyponatraemia-falls-bleeding cautions, mirtazapine's niche, TCA avoidance, the full-eventual-dose law, the longer-clock (8–12 weeks) and longer maintenance
- Use ECT without film-era fear in the melancholic, psychotic, food-refusing and medication-intolerant elderly
- Work up late-onset mania as SECONDARY until proven otherwise (stroke, thyroid, dopaminergic drugs, steroids, dementia, delirium) and manage elderly bipolar with lithium's geriatric rules (the narrow-margin kidney, the interactions)
- Handle Indian realities: the migration-empty-nest elder, the "budhapa" dismissal, the pain-complaint route through physicians, the joint family's protective-and-pressuring double edge

## 4. Epidemiology

**Global.** Major depression in community-dwelling elderly: roughly 2–5% (rising steeply among the medically ill, hospitalised and institutionalised, where rates reach a fifth or more); subsyndromal depression several-fold higher (the mixed zone of "not quite criteria, quite impaired"). The course is longer and more relapsing than young-adult depression; the suicide risk is higher in lethality (attempts fewer, completions more; see the Elderly Suicide note). Sex: women treated more, men dying more. The first-onset layer: late first-life episodes carry the vascular-depression signature (deep white-matter hyperintensities on imaging, executive dysfunction, poorer antidepressant response-alone, better augmentation-and-exercise outcomes), the subtype that changed the field's model from "reactive to losses" to "brain-vessel-burdened".

**India.** NMHS 2015–16: geriatric depression ~8–9% (roughly one in twelve elders), higher in urban metros (nearly one in five in the urban-elderly band of the survey's reporting; a counter-intuitive finding: the Indian city's elder is lonelier than the village's, against the modernisation narrative), with a treatment gap above 85%. The structural drivers: the migration-empty-nest pattern (children abroad or in distant metros; the two-person and one-person elderly households rising), the joint family's slow dissolution into "near but not with" arrangements, the economic dependency layer, the chronic-pain and multimorbidity freight, and the grief stacks that the long-lived accumulate. The presentation routes: the physician's OPD via pain and "weakness" complaints (the depression hidden under the presenting symptom), the faith-temple route, and, too often, only at the family's crisis point.

## 5. Causes & risk factors

**Biological**
- The vascular-depression engine: lacunes and white-matter hyperintensities disrupting frontostriatal mood circuitry, the stroke-depression comorbidity (post-stroke depression affects roughly a third of stroke survivors at any point in year one, a two-way street: depression also impairs stroke rehabilitation)
- Multimorbidity freight: hypothyroidism, B12/folate deficiency, anaemia, cancer (the pancreatic-depression classic), cardiac failure, Parkinson's, chronic pain (the arthritis-depression loop), and medications (steroids, beta-blockers-era discussions, the benzodiazepine decade)
- Sensory and mobility loss (the world shrinking to one room and one channel)
- The neurodegeneration risk: late-life depression as a prodrome-or-risk-marker of dementia (the association is real; treating depression and monitoring cognition is the practical translation)

**Psychological**
- The loss-stack: bereavement (the spouse of 50 years), retirement (the identity organ removed), relocation (the familiar lanes lost to the children's city), the friend-cohort's attrition (the phone-book thinning)
- The loneliness architecture: the migration-empty-nest pattern, the "visiting elder" identity (the six-month visa parent), the afternoon silences
- Rigid coping styles meet irreversible losses; the young's distraction-strategies unavailable to the immobile
- The "burden-schema": self-worth calibrated to contribution; dependency felt as debt ("eating and doing nothing")

**Social / Indian context**
- Status inversion inside the joint family (the patriarch-to-patient slide; the matriarch-to-guest slide in the daughter-in-law's kitchen)
- Economic dependency and the pension gap (the indignity layer)
- The stigma-complex: depression read as "tension", "old-age nature", or family-shame; the temple-route before the clinic-route
- The protective structures worth naming: the temple-committee role, the morning-walk group, the grandchildren's homework, the Indian elder's engagement scaffolding (the MCI note's principle: retention of roles is protective medicine)

## 6. What happens in the brain

**Story 1: the watershed engine (vascular depression).** The mood circuits run through frontostriatal territory, the brain's watershed zones, where small-vessel disease strikes first. Hypertension and diabetes quietly degrade the white-matter highways; the mood system's connectivity thins; and the first depression of a 70-year-old arrives with a specific signature: executive slowing, apathy, less guilt-psychology, more braking-failure, and a tougher response to antidepressants ALONE (the trials' lesson: add the augmentation, add the exercise, treat the vessels). The clinical translation: the elder's first depression is a cardiology-and-neurology consultation as much as a psychiatric one; the BP, the sugars, the vessels are part of the mood treatment.

**Story 2: the retrieval-freeze (pseudodementia).** Depression freezes RETRIEVAL; dementia destroys STORAGE. The depressed elder files the memories intact but cannot open the drawer, so the answers to your memory questions are "I don't know" and "I can't remember" and EFFORT is absent (the shrug), while the dementing elder CONFABULATES the wrong answer with genuine effort ("the doctor came yesterday", no one did). The giveaway behaviours: the depressed elder keeps the date through the news and forgets the breakfast they didn't eat; the dementing elder eats the breakfast and forgets the year. And the critical clinical law: many elders have BOTH layers (the depression riding on early dementia); the differential is finally made by the TREATMENT TRIAL: treat the depression properly, then re-test cognition; what recovers was depression; what remains is dementia's true floor. The pseudodementia catch can restore a family's wrongly-sold house; the miss can institutionalise a reversible person.

**Story 3: the loss-stack physics.** Late-life mood is a load-bearing calculation: each loss (spouse, role, home, friend, function) removes a strut; the structure still stands until one more. The Indian elder's particular physics: the struts were built INTO the family structure (identity-as-provider, kitchen-authority, ritual-role), and modernisation removes them all in one decade (the children's migration, the daughter-in-law's kitchen, the society's ageism). The treatment's non-drug half is strut-REBUILDING: new roles that carry weight (the temple committee's accounts, the grandson's homework supervision, the building's WhatsApp admin), plus the deliberate re-connection architecture (the walk group, the elder-association, the phone-tree of the surviving cohort). The pills lift the mood's floor; the struts carry the load.

## 7. Symptoms

**The depressive episode, geriatric edition:**
- **The somatic costume** (the Indian front door): chronic pain complaints (joints, back, "whole-body pain"), "weakness", burning feet, appetite loss with weight decline, constipation-era complaints, multiple "gas/indigestion" visits, with the workup repeatedly normal
- **The psychomotor signs**: the slowed elder (the long pauses, the sighing, the stoop, the stopped cooking) or the agitated elder (the wringing, the pacing, the "can't sit" restlessness; the geriatric agitated depression that mimics anxiety)
- **Sleep inversion**: early waking (the 3 a.m. stare), the lost afternoon nap structure, the day-night drift
- **The burden-talk**: "I am a burden", "the family spends on my medicines for nothing", "it would be better if God took me"; EVERY such sentence is a suicide-screen trigger in this population (the elderly attempt less, die more, the Elderly Suicide note)
- **Guilt and nihilism** intact with age: "I ruined my sons' lives", "my sins are catching up", the religious-guilt layer in the devout
- **Apathy and anhedonia-by-attrition**: the stopped visits, the abandoned morning walk, the temple stopped, the "nothing interests me", misread as "lazy old age" for months
- **Cognitive complaints**: the pseudodementia presentation above ("my memory is gone", with the don't-know answers)
- **Psychotic features** in the severe band: nihilistic and guilt-content ("my insides have rotted", "I have ruined the family's finances", the poverty-of-self-delusions), hypochondriacal delusions
- **The mask-of-smiles** brief: many elders, schooled in "not complaining", present cheerful at interview and symptomatic only in the informant report; the collateral interview is the examination

**The bipolar side (the late-onset presentations):**
- **Late-onset mania** (first episode after 50–60): the classic SECONDARY-suspicion territory (the patient with no psychiatric history who suddenly has a manic picture: elation or irritability, decreased sleep, pressure, disinhibition, grandiosity) hunt the cause: recent stroke (especially right-hemisphere and orbitofrontal territory), hyperthyroidism, dopaminergic medications (levodopa, the agonists), steroids, stimulant-adjacent remedies, delirium's mixed picture, frontotemporal dementia's disinhibition phase, or a genuine bipolar diathesis finally firing
- **Elderly bipolar I (the established disease grown old)**: recurrent episodes with the mania softening and the depressive poles dominating late-life; the mixed-agitated states; and the long-maintenance question
- **The rapid-signal flags**: any first manic presentation in an elder is a MRI + TSH + medication-audit + cognitive-assessment event before it is a psychiatric label

## 8. Diagnosis

**The logic (DSM-5-TR paraphrased):** the same episode criteria as adults (2 weeks, 5+ symptoms, mood/anhedonia inclusive), with the geriatric reading: somatic symptoms counted with care (overlap with medical illness; anhedonia, guilt, psychomotor change, and early waking carry more weight than appetite/sleep complaints alone); persistent depressive disorder ≥2 years; the bereavement-adjacent discipline (the Bereavement note's rules; major depression within weeks of a spouse's death is not "just grief" but often both). Persistent, treatment-resistant, first-episode-after-60 depressions: consider the vascular-depression subtype's signature (executive dysfunction; apathy; MRI's white-matter burden).

**The assessment structure:**
1. **The collateral interview as examination** (the co-resident child, the cook, the neighbour; the stopped-activities list is more diagnostic than the elder's brave smile)
2. **The suicide screen**: the burden-sentences, the passive wishes, the stockpiling of medicines, the "affairs-in-order" behaviour (the Elderly Suicide note's full architecture)
3. **The cognitive screen** (MoCA-tier + the pseudodementia's ten-clue differentiation, table below), with the serial-cognition discipline: re-test after mood treatment
4. **The medical and medication audit**: TSH, B12, CBC, glucose, renal, the cancer-index-of-suspicion where the picture fits (weight loss audit), the pain assessment (the arthritis-depression loop), the full drug list with the anticholinergic and sedative counts, alcohol (the quiet-elder-drinker under-recognition)
5. **The sensory-mobility audit**: hearing, vision, the fall history (depression's motor-fluency cost AND a falls-risk input to treatment)
6. **The loss-stack map**: recent bereavements, relocations, the migration-children map, the roles lost and the roles remaining
7. **The bipolar-side workup for late-onset mania**: MRI (the stroke-frontotemporal hunt), TSH, the medication timeline (levodopa-agonists, steroids), the delirium screen, the family psychiatric history (the genuine-bipolar marker)
8. Rating scales (named only): GDS (the geriatric self-report with the yes/no format built for elders), PHQ-9, CSDD for the dementia-overlap cases

### Differential table: and the pseudodementia's ten clues

| The clue | Pseudodementia (depression) | True dementia |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | Weeks-months, datable, often after a loss | Insidious, years |
| Course | Rapid cognitive "decline" | Slowly progressive |
| The answers | "I don't know / can't remember", absent effort | Near-miss confabulation: genuine effort |
| Insight into memory | Complaints loudly, early | Little complaint; the FAMILY complains |
| Orientation | Preserved (date-through-news intact) | Lost early |
| Effect of cueing | Recognition improves with cueing | Cuing fails; storage is gone |
| Performance variability | "Don't-test-me" inconsistency; worst when sad | Consistently poor |
| Functional gap | Ability intact though unused ("I can't cook") | Ability genuinely lost |
| Mood architecture | Dysphoric, guilt-laden | Affect often shallow-labile |
| Post-treatment re-test | Scores RECOVER substantially | The floor remains |

**The surrounding differential:** normal grief (the wave-pattern; see the Bereavement note); hypoactive delirium (days-onset, inattention: the Delirium note); hypothyroid/B12 states; medication sedation; the apathy of early dementia (the distinction from depression's anhedonia: apathy without dysphoria; both can co-ride); Parkinson's disease's own depression (half of Parkinson's patients, under-treated); post-stroke depression (the timeline lock to the event).

## 9. Management

**The frame: treat co-riders first, treat depression FULLY (not timidly), treat cognition serially, protect against suicide, and rebuild the struts.**

**1, The co-riders:** hypothyroid/B12 replaced; pain managed properly (the undertreated arthritis-pain depression); the anticholinergic-sedative load deprescribed (the benzodiazepine decade's taper, the Benzo note's bridge); the sensory channels aided (hearing aids as mood treatment); the medical illnesses optimised (the cardiac-and-diabetic clinics already in the family's routine; add the mood to the same follow-up visits).

**2: Antidepressant pharmacotherapy (the geriatric discipline):**
- **SSRIs first-line**: sertraline and escitalopram (the best geriatric evidence-tolerability balance; citalopram's QT-dose ceiling remembered): start low (sertraline 25–50 mg) but titrate to the FULL effective dose over weeks: the geriatric sin is not starting; it is STAYING at the starter dose ("start low, go slow, but GO"); expect the longer clock (8–12 weeks for full response in elders, vs 4–6 in younger adults)
- **The elder-specific watch-list**: hyponatraemia (SSRI-induced SIADH; check sodium within the first month in elders, more so with diuretics; the confusion-lethargy presentation), falls (sedation and orthostasis, the evening-dose choices, the get-up-slowly counsel), GI bleeding with NSAIDs (the arthritis-pain co-prescription's interaction, counsel + PPI where both must run), QT where applicable, serotonin-syndrome in the polypharmacy, and the agitation-activation watch in the first two weeks
- **Mirtazapine's niche**: the appetite-lost, insomniac, wasting elder (the appetite-restoring, sleep-restoring atypical; the sedation used for the first two weeks at night)
- **Avoid or reserve**: TCAs (the anticholinergic-orthostatic-fog ledger in elders, the one class whose harms reliably outweigh its benefits here except in specific pain-adjuvant niches at micro-doses); MAOIs (museum-era for this population)
- **Duration**: first episode treated to full remission then 12+ months; second-or-more episodes (most elderly depressions are recurrent by the time they reach us) → 2–3 years or indefinite maintenance; the taper-never-abrupt rule
- **The augmentation moves for the vascular-depression subtype**: the trials' lesson, SSRI-alone underperforms; add bupropion (the activation-fit where no seizure-cardiac contraindication), atypical-antipsychotic augmentation (aripiprazole low-dose; the metabolic-mortality cautions of the Late-Life-Psychosis note apply), lithium's geriatric version (below), and NON-DRUG: the exercise prescription (the strongest single geriatric-augmentation evidence, the walk-group again)
- *India note (approx 2026): sertraline ₹50–150/month, escitalopram ₹60–200, mirtazapine ₹80–250; the hyponatraemia check = ₹100–200 at district labs, build it into the month-one review; the recurring Indian failure is the eternal starter-dose ("25 mg for six months, no improvement, told antidepressants don't work in old people")*

**3, ECT (the geriatric best-kept secret):** for the melancholic-psychotic band, the food-and-fluid-refusing wasting elder, the intolerant-to-drugs picture, and the severe suicidal states, ECT in elders is fast, effective and SAFE (the modern-modality evidence; the cognitive side-effects largely transient, monitored; the frail-body advantage: no drug-interaction ledger). The Indian film-era fear is the barrier to the best medicine this population has; the consent-and-family-education work is the treatment's first session.

**4, Psychotherapy (the under-used tier):** problem-solving therapy (the best geriatric-adapted evidence, short, structured, concrete: the bills, the stairs, the loneliness each become problems with steps); CBT's behavioural-activation module (the scheduled pleasant-events work for the anhedonia); brief dynamic work for the loss-stack's meaning-layer; reminiscence/life-review therapy (the geriatric-specific modality with real evidence, the structured telling of the life story to a listener; the Indian adaptation lands beautifully: the family-sanctioned "telling the grandchildren" formats). Delivery reality: therapist scarcity → the physician-plus-family-delivered versions (the activation-scheduling taught to the daughter-in-law, the walk-group prescribed to the neighbour cohort).

**5: The strut-rebuilding programme (the non-drug half of every plan):** role-restoration (the temple-committee accounts, the grandchildren-homework supervisorship, the building-association duty: RETAINED roles, not token roles); the re-connection architecture (the morning-walk group, the senior-association, the phone-tree of the surviving cohort, the WhatsApp-family-admin role); the environment's engagement engineering (the plants, the cooking-supervision-not-substitution, the MCI note's scaffolding principle); and the migration-children protocol (the fixed video-rituals with the distant children, scheduled, predictable, role-bearing: "Sunday you check Sagar's homework on the call").

**6: Late-onset mania management (the workup-first discipline):** the MRI-TSH-medication audit BEFORE the bipolar label; the secondary causes treated (the stroke-rehab team engaged; the levodopa-negotiation with neurology; the steroid-wind-down with the prescribing specialty); and only then the genuine-late-bipolar treatment: the atypical antipsychotics at geriatric doses (the Late-Life-Psychosis note's cautions), lithium's geriatric rules (start ~150–300 mg, levels at the lower therapeutic band ~0.4–0.7 in elders, the renal-function check always; GFR has fallen by decades; the interaction lecture: NSAIDs and ACE-inhibitors RAISE lithium levels, the arthritis-pain-killer-plus-lithium emergency; the thirst-urination-confusion toxicity triad taught to the family), valproate's elder-niches (tremor-interaction caution with lamotrigine-the-elderly-rash-window), and the maintenance's longer horizon.

**7, The serial-cognition discipline:** every treated pseudodementia gets the dated re-test (3–6 months post-remission): what recovered was depression; what remains is the dementia floor, the family counselled on both numbers; the MCI-note's follow-up map then applies.

*India note (approx 2026): the delivery spine, the geriatric-clinic/psychiatry-OPD at medical colleges (low-cost), the family-physician co-management with the month-one sodium check, the district-lab tier (TSH/B12/Na ≈ ₹500–1,000), the walk-group and temple-role prescriptions (cost: nothing), the ECT course at government hospitals (₹100–500/session-era pricing at public tier; private ₹2,000–5,000): the modality whose under-use most costs Indian elders.*

## 10. Indian practice

- **The "budhapa-kamzori" dismissal kills.** The Indian elder's depression is read as ageing's weather for an average of years before presentation ("it's budhapa"; "she is just tired"), and the completion-risk of the untreated elderly cohort (the Elderly Suicide note) runs alongside that delay. The physician-OPD intercept (the pain-and-weakness complaints carrying the hidden mood) is the system's single best catch-point: the two-question screen plus the collateral "what has she STOPPED doing?" converts the soma into the syndrome.
- **The migration-empty-nest pattern is the new Indian aetiology layer.** The Bangalore-and-Dubai children's parents (the two-person households of Lucknow's retired colonies) present with the loss-stack's full architecture; the treatment's family-module is largely REMOTE work, the children's structured-engagement prescription (the fixed video-rituals, the role-bearing calls, the annual-depression-screen question asked by the NRI son on his visit: "what has Papa stopped doing this year?"). The son-asks-the-question moment at festival visits is the Indian geriatric depression's best screening instrument.
- **The pseudodementia family-catastrophe prevention.** The wrongly-labelled demented elder gets the property-transferred, the driving-stopped, the person-institutionalised pathway; on a depression that would have lifted in eight weeks. The catch discipline: the ten-clue differentiation at first assessment + the treatment-trial re-test before any "dementia" verdict is announced to the family (or the lawyer). The sentence that undoes the harm: "let us first treat the depression and THEN measure the memory".
- **The joint family's double-edge management.** The joint family protects (observation, structure, the scaffolding-roles) AND pressures (the status-inversion humiliation, the dependency-debt schemas, the daughter-in-law's kitchen-theft of the matriarch's last role). The clinical work: the role-restoration negotiation (the mother-in-law's SUPERVISION role defined with the daughter-in-law, the recipe-consultant honoured in practice, not the guest at the table); the "burden-talk" confronted as the risk-sentence it is; and the family's own caregiver-burden addressed (the daughter-in-law's depression screen runs in the same house).
- **The temple-remedy route as ally.** The Indian elder's faith-practice is a strut (the morning routine, the congregation, the role); the clinical frame keeps it: "the temple visit IS part of your treatment; the mood-medicine and the temple are on the same team". The faith-healer route for the severe-psychotic band gets the respectful-redirect (the Late-Life-Psychosis note's discipline).
- **The pills-and-frailty conversation.** The Indian family's medicine-fear triad at this age ("addiction", "kidney damage", "sleeping tablets") is met with the honest ledger: these are not addictive, they are checked for elder-specific harms (the sodium, the falls), and the untreated illness's ledger (weight loss, the suicide risk, the stroke-rehabilitation failure) is the longer one. The benzo-conversion question ("her old sleeping tablet?") gets the taper-bridge answer; the night sedative is usually the wrong medicine that has been treating the right illness.
- **The widowed-male vulnerability.** Indian widowers (the least-supported cohort: the man whose household-knowledge was outsourced to his wife for 50 years) present with the fastest declines; the practical programme (the cooking-class-for-widowers tier of interventions, the men's-walk-group, the role-invention) looks unglamorous and works.

## 11. Clinical cases (de-identified)

**Case 1: the woman whose family was already selling the house.** A 76-year-old Pune widow was brought by her son with a note from the family's physician: "memory loss, probably dementia; family wants to plan." The son's real agenda surfaced in the collateral interview: the property-deed and bank-authorisation work "before she gets worse", and the daughter-in-law had quietly taken over the kitchen three months ago. The picture: four months of "memory loss" (missing the fixed-deposit dates, forgetting the medicines, which the daughter-in-law now administered, the symptom and its cause in one fact), stopped cooking, stopped temple, stopped the morning walk; the 3 a.m. waking; 7 kg lost; the body-pain complaints that had toured three orthopaedists; and the sentence her daughter reported from a phone call: "what is the use of my staying now; I eat and sit like a sack". On testing: she answered "I don't know" to most memory items, shrugged at the clock, orientated fully to date-through-news, and brightened measurably when the conversation turned to her granddaughter's exams (the variability clue); the daughter-in-law's list of stopped-activities was diagnostic; PHQ-9-tier screen: severe; the ten-clue table ran strongly pseudodementic; the audit: B12 borderline-low, TSH normal, sodium normal, no anticholinergic load beyond the bladder tablet (stopped), the alprazolam 0.25 mg nocte for two years (tapered). Diagnosis: major depressive episode, severe, with pseudodementia presentation, on a loss-stack (the husband's death 14 months prior; the sister-friend's death 6 months prior; the kitchen-role loss 3 months prior) and a borderline B12 layer. Management: the property conversation PAUSED explicitly (the clinic's letter: "the memory picture is currently unproven; re-assessment after treatment"); sertraline started 25 mg, titrated weekly to 100 mg (the GO law); the B12 replaced; the daughter-in-law's kitchen-role NEGOTIATED to supervision (the recipe-consultant contract, her idea once named); the walk-group re-connected (two temple-cohort women assigned the daily-collection duty, the Indian version of behavioural activation with social enforcement); problem-solving therapy (6 sessions: the medicines-box system, the fixed-deposit calendar, the stairs-anxiety each got a plan); the son's remote-engagement prescription (the Sunday-homework-call role for the granddaughter's maths). At 8 weeks: cooking twice weekly, PHQ-9 halved, appetite returning; at 4 months: the re-test, MoCA-tier score up 7 points from baseline, orientation intact, memory "good enough that the bank asked nothing when I went myself". The dementia label never resumed. The son's verdict (in the file, verbatim, because it belongs in teaching): "we were selling the house to fund a disease she did not have." Teaching points: (1) the "memory loss" was the family's physician-note and the depression's symptom in one presentation; the ten-clue differential plus the STOPPED-activities list did the diagnosis before any test; (2) the property-PAUSE letter is a clinical instrument in Indian practice; (3) the daughter-in-law kitchen-negotiation was the strut-rebuild and the symptom's cause both; (4) the sertraline went to full dose; the starter-dose-forever sin would have "confirmed" the dementia; (5) the treatment-trial re-test is the pseudodementia's final judge.

**Case 2: the first mania at 66.** A 66-year-old Ludhiana businessman with no psychiatric history was brought by his sons after a three-week changed state: sleeping two-to-three hours ("I don't need sleep; the factory plan is complete"), talking fast, spending large sums on "the new venture", religious-preaching to the staff, and initial elation shading into irritability and one late-night confrontation with a supplier. The family's proposed theory: "stress of business". The clinic's order-rule ran (the late-onset-mania discipline): the MRI first, a right-hemisphere orbitofrontal-adjacent infarct, dated by the radiologist as recent-weeks; the TSH: suppressed (the missed hyperthyroidism, his weight loss and palpitations for two months, read as "work stress"); the medication list: a steroid course from a local practitioner for "weakness" six weeks prior. The event-hunt: he had stopped his blood-pressure medicine a year ago ("felt fine"); the BP in clinic: 178/104. Diagnosis: secondary manic presentation, post-stroke mania on a hyperthyroid-and-steroid layer, with uncontrolled hypertension as the engine's engine. Management: the causes treated (the thyro-toxicosis managed with the endocrinology team; the steroid already weaning; the stroke workup and secondary-prevention with the physician: BP restored to treatment; the family counselled on the mania's secondary nature); the acute agitation managed with quetiapine at geriatric dose (12.5–25 mg titrated, the vascular-patient caution), tapered as the thyroid and steroid layers cleared; the family-education on the recapitulation risk-signs (the sleep-drop as the first alarm; the Bipolar note's discipline adapted: "when his sleep falls below 5 hours for two nights, call us"). At three months: euthymic, thyroxine-controlled, anticoagulant-and-BP regime running, the "venture" quietly dissolved with the sons' management; no psychiatric diagnosis carried forward (the secondary-manic presentation resolved with its causes), but the file carried the watch-note: any recurrence earns the workup-first order again. Teaching points: (1) the FIRST mania after 50 is a medical workup before it is a psychiatric label: MRI, TSH, steroids, stroke, the whole ladder; (2) three secondary engines were stacked in one patient (infarct, thyroid, steroid); the unexamined stack produces the wrong lifelong label; (3) the treatment of secondary mania is the treatment of its causes with the smallest psychiatric-scaffold; (4) the sleep-drop alarm taught to the family is the early-warning instrument for ANY future episode; (5) "work stress" was doing what Indian families' first-explanations do; covering a hypertension that had already stopped its medicines.

## 13. FAQ: asked the way families ask it

**"He is 78. Isn't this just old age?"** Old age slows the body; it does not stop the appetite, stop the cooking, stop the talking and wake him at 3 a.m. with "what is the use". Those are symptoms of an illness, one that affects roughly one in twelve Indian elders and treats well at any age. The "just old age" sentence is the reason Indian elders average years before anyone asks the mood questions.

**"Is it dementia? His memory has gone."** It may be, or it may be depression wearing memory's costume: the memories filed but the drawer jammed. The two are separated at assessment (the pattern of the answers, the speed of the decline, what the family reports) and finally by TREATMENT: we treat the depression fully and re-test the memory; what returns was never gone. Before any "dementia" verdict, and any property decisions built on it, that trial belongs in the file.

**"Aren't antidepressants dangerous at this age, kidneys, falls, addiction?"** They are medicines that respect age: we start low, rise to the full working dose, and run the elder-specific checks, the sodium test in month one, the falls-counsel, the bleeding-audit with his arthritis medicines. They are not addictive. And the honest comparison is with the untreated illness's ledger: the weight loss, the stroke-rehabilitation that fails, the suicide risk that runs highest in exactly this age group. The medicine is the safer side of that ledger.

**"ECT? Like in the old films? You want to shock my mother?"** Modern ECT under anaesthesia is the fastest and often the safest treatment we have for the severe elderly depressions, the ones refusing food, the psychotic guilt-states, the failed-medication pictures. The memory disturbances it causes are mostly brief and monitored. In frail elders it is often gentler than the drug-alternatives. The old films were made before the anaesthesia era; the treatment your mother would receive has nothing in common with them.

**"She has stopped everything, temple, cooking, walks. We tell her to try. She says she cannot."** That "cannot" is the illness's signature; the will-engine itself is part of what is depressed; willpower-advice is aspirin for a fracture. The treatment order: medicine-and-therapy to lift the floor, THEN the scheduled restarts (the walk-partner arriving at the door, the cooking starting with supervision-not-alone, the temple visit with the fixed companion). Action returns after structure, not after lectures.

**"She says she is a burden and God should take her. Is that normal old-age talk?"** No, and treat it as the emergency-adjacent sentence it is. The elderly hide frank suicidal statements behind exactly this "burden" grammar, and this population's attempts succeed more often than the young's. We assess her the same week: the questions asked directly, the medicines-secured audit, the plan made with the family. The sentence is her depression speaking; answered with the right treatment, it goes quiet.

**"The GP gave her a sleeping tablet years ago. Can we just continue that?"** That tablet is likely a benzodiazepine, at her age it manufactures daytime fog, night-falls, memory-complaints that imitate dementia, and a dependency that will need a gradual taper. It was treating the symptom of the very illness we should treat properly. We will bridge-and-taper it while the real medicine takes over; the sleep architecture returns with the mood.

**"All her friends are dying and the children are abroad. What can medicine do about loneliness?"** Medicine lifts the floor; the structure rebuilds the load-bearing walls, and that half is ours to engineer together: the walk-cohort (two women assigned the daily-collection duty), the temple-committee role, the grandchildren's homework-supervision on the Sunday call, the senior-association's phone-tree. The children's remote-engagement prescription is real treatment: fixed, role-bearing, predictable contact. Loneliness is a treatable architecture, not just a feeling.

**"Her memory improved after treatment, so it was depression. Is she now safe from dementia?"** Partially protected, not guaranteed: late-life depression raises later dementia-risk too, which is why the recovered pseudodementia stays on the follow-up map (the re-test dates in the file), the vascular risks are treated as brain-protection, and the family keeps the three-signs notebook. What is certain: she now has her true baseline, measured on a treated brain, the honest floor.

**"How long will she need the medicine?"** The first episode: about a year at the full working dose after recovery, then a slow taper. If it has come back before (most elderly depressions have), we talk in years, and for the third episode we talk about staying on. The decision is review-based, dated, and reversible; the prescription-and-forget pattern is the one that harms.

## 14. Exam & student lens

**The geriatric skeleton for viva:** episode criteria with geriatric weighting (anhedonia/guilt/psychomotor/early-waking over somatic); the costumes (somatic, pseudodementia, apathy); vascular-depression subtype; the dose law; the watch-list; ECT's positions; late-onset mania = workup-first; lithium's elder-rules.

**Mnemonics:** the dose law; "Start LOW, go SLOW, but GO" (the eternal-starter-dose sin); the watch-list: "HY-FIB" (HYponatraemia, Falls, Interactions, Bleeding-with-NSAIDs); the late-onset-mania workup: "M-T-M-S-D" (MRI, Thyroid, Medications, Stroke-history, Delirium-screen).

**High-yield facts:**
- NMHS 2015–16: ~1 in 12 Indian elders with depression; urban-elderly higher; treatment gap >85%
- Post-stroke depression affects ~a third of stroke survivors in year one, bidirectional with rehabilitation outcomes
- Pseudodementia: retrieval-freeze vs storage-failure; the final judge is the treatment-trial re-test
- Elderly suicide: attempts fewer, COMPLETIONS higher, burden-talk = the screen-trigger (see Elderly Suicide note)
- SSRI elder-risks: hyponatraemia (SIADH; month-one sodium), falls, GI-bleeding with NSAIDs, QT (citalopram ceiling)
- Sertraline/escitalopram = first-line balance; TCAs avoided in elders; mirtazapine for the wasting-insomniac
- The clock: 8–12 weeks for full geriatric response; maintenance longer (2–3 y / indefinite for recurrent)
- ECT: fast, effective, SAFE in elders, the melancholic/psychotic/wasting/intolerant tier's best option
- Late-onset mania: secondary until proven (stroke/ thyroid/ steroids/ dopaminergics/ FTD/ delirium); lithium band ~0.4–0.7, NSAID/ACE interactions raise levels
- The strongest geriatric augmentation: EXERCISE (the walk-group prescription) + problem-solving therapy/reminiscence evidence

**Classic traps:**
1. "Budhapa" dismissal running for years (the pain-weakness OPD route never screened)
2. The eternal starter-dose (25 mg sertraline forever, then "antidepressants don't work in elders")
3. The pseudodementia verdict passed to the lawyer before the treatment-trial re-test
4. Missing the hyponatraemia confusion at week six (read as "dementia worsening")
5. Late-onset mania labelled bipolar and loaded with lithium before the MRI/TSH/steroid audit
6. The NSAID-plus-SSRI bleeding and the NSAID-plus-LITHIUM toxicity in the same arthritis patient
7. The benzodiazepine decade maintained because "she needs her sleep tablet"
8. The burden-talk accepted as normal old-age philosophy
