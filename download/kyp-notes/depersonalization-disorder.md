# Depersonalization / Derealization Disorder

## 1. In one line + overview

**Depersonalization-derealization disorder (DPDR) is the mind's emergency detachment switch stuck in the ON position without an emergency: the person feels unreal, robotic, watching themselves from outside, or moves through a world that seems flat, dreamlike, behind glass, while KNOWING all along that this is a feeling, not a fact; that intact insight is precisely what separates it from psychosis, and the fear of "going mad" is usually the illness's loudest symptom.**

Transient depersonalization is nearly universal, about half of all people experience a passing episode (exhaustion, fevers, the motorway-hypnosis of long drives, grief, a bad trip), and the isolated experience is normal, not pathological. The disorder begins when the detachment persists (weeks-to-years), causes real distress or impairment, and drags its anxiety-loop behind it: feeling unreal is frightening; fighting the feeling strengthens it; monitoring one's unreality (constantly checking "do I feel real yet?") deepens the glass wall. The clinical population is young (onset typically teens-to-twenties), often anxious, frequently post-trauma or post-cannabis, and commonly misrouted in India into two wrong clinics: the psychotic frame ("she says the world looks false, antipsychotics") or the spiritual frame ("a higher state, go deeper"), when the correct address is the anxiety-dissociation tier where explanation, attention-training and comorbidity treatment do most of the work. The note's quiet good news: this is one of the conditions where the diagnosis-given-calmly is itself the half-cure.

## 2. Learning objectives

After this note you can:

- Separate DPDR from psychosis (the insight axis), from temporal-lobe auras, from panic's DP surges, and from PTSD's flashbacks, the four front-door confusions
- Trace the anxiety-maintenance loop (fear-of-unreality → monitoring → attention-amplification → deeper unreality) and design its CBT break

- Take the aetiological history that matters: early emotional neglect/abuse, panic, cannabis, sleep deprivation, intensive meditation, and the organic screen list
- Handle the Indian spiritual frame respectfully, the meditation-dissociation boundary conversation

## 4. Epidemiology

**Global.** Transient depersonalization experiences: roughly half the population at least once. The persistent disorder: around 1–2% of the general population in the classic estimates, with clinic samples skewing young, male-or-female depending on setting, onset commonly 15–25, and chronic courses measured in years (a mean duration of years-to-decade in specialty cohorts, with waxing-and-waning rather than remitting patterns). Comorbidity is the rule: panic disorder, depression, PTSD, social anxiety, obsessive-spectrum traits; cannabis is the classic precipitant (a first-presentation trigger in a notable minority, with the "I never came back from the joint" history a clinic recognisable sentence).

**India.** No specific Indian DPDR surveys exist (the dissociative presentation in India famously runs through possession-and-fugue channels; the dissociative-conversion tier this book's missing chapter carries); the realistic Indian clinical map: DP presentations arriving through (1) the somatic-and-anxiety front door ("I don't feel anything, saab; my body feels like a machine"), (2) the family-brought psychosis-suspicion route ("she says the world looks false"), (3) the post-cannabis student route of metro clinics, and (4) the intensive-meditation route, a genuine Indian-specific tier: retreat-goers and sadhana-intensives in whom the detachment arrives during practice and persists, wearing enlightenment's clothing, needing a clinician who can honour the tradition while drawing the clinical line. The treatment-gap reality: most Indian DPDR is undiagnosed (absorbed into "tension", "gas", or faith-explanations) or over-diagnosed as psychosis; both errors are common, and both are correctable with the two-question screen: "does it feel unreal, and do you KNOW it is a feeling rather than a fact?"

## 5. Causes & risk factors

- **Trauma (the deepest root)**: early emotional abuse and neglect; the childhood in which feelings were unsafe to feel; the detachment-brake first engaged then as protection and never fully disengaged; adult traumas (assault, disaster, combat) as the acute trigger tier
- **Panic and anxiety**: the interoceptive-catastrophe family, the first panic attack's surge frequently triggers the detach ("I left my body"), and the panic-disorder patient's DP is the comorbidity's texture
- **Substances**: cannabis (the classic trigger; acute DP that persists in a minority; the high-potency-era amplifier), hallucinogens (transient DP; HPPD-adjacent persistence in some), ketamine, alcohol-benzo withdrawal windows
- **Physiological**: severe sleep deprivation, fevers, migraine auras, epileptic auras (temporal-lobe tier, the organic screen), hypoglycaemia, dehydration-and-exhaustion states (the pilgrimage-and-exam-season pattern)
- **Temperament**: high-anxiety, absorption-prone, dissociation-prone personalities; the dissociation-spectrum trait architecture
- **Meditation-induced tier**: intensive practice in vulnerable practitioners, the detachment arising within the meditative frame and persisting beyond it (a documented phenomenon in contemplative-clinical literature; the Indian volume is larger and under-reported)
- **Indian context**: the family-invalidating environments where chronic emotional neglect is neither named nor treated; high-potency cannabis availability; the meditation-and-retreat density that makes the practice-induced tier proportionally larger

## 6. What happens in the mind: three short stories

**Story one: the emergency brake that stays on.** The brain carries an inbuilt circuit-breaker for unbearable experience: when affect exceeds what the system can process, prefrontal systems inhibit the limbic emotional signal, pain still registers as information but arrives stripped of its colour (the battlefield analgesia-and-detachment reports are the textbook example). The Sierra-Berrios "cortical-limbic decoupling" model describes DPDR as this brake failing to release: the inhibition that should have lasted minutes-to-hours keeps filtering the emotional colour off experience (hence the flat world, the robot-body, the observer-self) while the knowing systems run perfectly (the person is fully oriented, lucid, and aware that something is WRONG with the feeling, not with the world's facts). The brake's stuck-on state is the disorder's platform; the illness's engine is the next story.

**Story two: the loop that feeds the glass wall.** The felt-unreality frightens; the frightened mind does what anxious minds do, monitors, checks, scans ("am I real now? how about now?"); attention devoted to a sensation amplifies it (the white-bear law of monitored experience); the amplified unreality frightens further. Round and round: the symptom maintained and deepened by its own rescue attempts. This is the loop CBT-for-DP dismantles, and it is why reassurance-naming ("this is a known, safe, non-psychotic phenomenon") is genuinely anti-disease: it removes the loop's fuel, the catastrophe-interpretation. The clinical corollary: the patients who improve fastest are the ones who stopped fighting it first.

**Story three: the two doors into the stuck state.** Door one: the sensitive brain (anxious, absorption-prone, often an early-neglect childhood) drifting into chronic DP under sustained stress-and-sleeplessness. Door two: the trigger event (a panic surge, a cannabis high, a trauma) slamming the brake on hard, with the detachment outlasting the trigger by months-to-years. The doors predict slightly different courses (door-two patients more often remit; door-one patients more often wax-and-wane), and both need the same first prescription: explanation, de-fearing, and the comorbidity treatment.

## 7. Symptoms

**Depersonalization (the self-side):** "I am a robot", "I watch myself act", "my body is not mine", "my feelings belong to someone else", "I am behind my own eyes like a passenger", "my face in the mirror is a stranger's" (a distressing classic); emotional numbness: love, fear and grief arriving as information without their felt-temperature; the sense of being an actor; memory's feel changed ("my memories are not mine, they are stories somebody told me", WITHOUT any actual amnesia).

**Derealization (the world-side):** flat colours, two-dimensional scenery, the world "behind glass" or through gauze, dreamlike or cinematic framing, fog-and-distance, sounds arriving muffled, familiar places feeling stage-set false.

**The metacognitive signature, the diagnostic key:** reality testing INTACT: the patient knows these are feelings, not facts; volunteers the fear of going mad; no delusional elaboration; functioning preserved (often maintained at real cost), insight full. The ancillary set: the monitoring-and-checking behaviours, the mirror-avoidance or mirror-fixation, the "test my reality" rituals, the chronic hypervigilant self-observation, the secondary depression-and-anxiety, and the family friction ("you look normal, what do you mean unreal?"): the invalidation that deepens the loop.

## 8. Diagnosis

**The logic.** The three gates: persistent depersonalization/derealization, clinically significant distress or impairment, and intact reality-testing with the absence of a better explanation (the mimics below must be excluded, organic, substance, and the dissociative-amnesia/possession tier of the wider family). The interview technique: patients describe DP poorly under cross-examination and precisely under quotation-invitation ("describe the exact moment the world changed, as if speaking to a film director"); ask the insight question directly ("do you believe the world IS false, or does it merely FEEL false?"): the answer's shape is the diagnosis's hinge.

**Structured assessment:**
- The trigger history (first episode: substance? panic? trauma? retreat? fever?) and the course since (wax-wane, persistent, episodic)
- The screens: epilepsy-aura pattern (stereotyped seconds-to-minutes, post-ictal features), migraine, thyroid, glucose, sleep-deprivation ledger, substance ledger (cannabis potency)
- The comorbidity map: panic (the full chapter), depression, PTSD, OCD-spectrum
- The trauma history taken gently (the early-emotional-neglect soil; the adult triggers)
- The severity-and-tracking instruments: the Cambridge Depersonalization Scale and its derivatives (named here; public-domain status varies; use as severity trackers, not diagnoses)
- The family's frame: what they have been told, and what they now believe (psychosis? spirit? attention?)

**Differential table:**

| Mimic | The separating feature |
|---|---|
| Psychosis (delusions of passivity/control) | The patient believes the unreality as FACT ("forces make me unreal"), delusional certainty, other psychotic fabric; DPDR says "it FEELS unreal, I know it is not" |
| Temporal-lobe epileptic aura | Seconds-to-minutes, stereotyped each time, automatisms or post-ictal clouding; DPDR runs hours-to-years, variable |
| Panic attack's DP surge | The 10–30-minute cardio-respiratory storm with DP as its passenger; DPDR persists between attacks |
| PTSD flashback | Re-living a specific trauma with its sensory signature; DPDR is detachment FROM the present, not re-entry into the past (both may coexist) |
| Acute drug intoxication / HPPD-class | The substance timeline; the persistence beyond clearance defines the disorder |
| Depressive anhedonia-and-numbness | Mood congruent, lifted with the depression's remission; DPDR's numbness stands even in euthymic stretches |
| Meditation-related states | Arising within practice; the boundary conversation (below): distress-and-impairment, not depth, marks the disorder |
| Psychotic depression's nihilism | Delusional guilt-and-nonexistence (the Cotard family) vs the knowing observer of DPDR |

## 9. Management

**The first prescription is the consultation itself:** the full explanation; what the brake is, why it stuck, that the symptoms are known, common, non-psychotic, and not a brain-rot; the explicit contrast with madness delivered in the family's presence; the natural-course honesty (many remit; the rest wax-and-wane; suffering is treatable even when the feeling lingers). Document the improvement after this conversation: it is routinely the largest single-session gain this disorder offers.

**The behavioural-cognitive tier:**
- **Attention re-training**: the monitoring is the engine; structured external-attention tasks (the sensory-engagement drills: naming five objects' detail, active listening tasks, absorption hobbies) plus scheduled "checking holidays"; attention-training therapy: the attention-control lineage adapted
- **Grounding-and-absorption techniques** for spikes (cold-water face-wash, textured objects, strong sensory input, brief exercise bursts)
- **The acceptance-and-de-fighting frame**: the counter-intuitive core; stop battling the glass, let it stand, re-engage life THROUGH it (ACT-consistent); the loop starves when the war ends
- **CBT-for-DP structure**: thought-record work on the catastrophic interpretations ("unreal = dying/mad"), behavioural experiments against the safety-behaviours (the reality-checking rituals scheduled-down), the rumination-and-monitoring diary
- **Comorbidity treatment as DP-treatment**: panic-disorder CBT-and-SSRI; depression's full treatment; PTSD's trauma-focused work where the soil demands it, each comorbidity lightened reliably lightens DP
- **Lifestyle floor**: sleep repair, cannabis-and-stimulant abstinence (non-negotiable in the post-cannabis form), the retreat-hiatus conversation

**Pharmacology, the honest position:** no drug is approved for DPDR, and none reliably removes it, say this plainly (the credibility it buys repays the disappointment). What is real: SSRIs treating the panic-and-depression riders (with DP softening secondarily in perhaps a quarter-to-a-third of comorbid cases); lamotrigine's modest open-trial tier (the antiepileptic logic: quieting the kindled limbic signal, low-evidence, honest-to-label); the combination-SSRI-plus-lamotrigine reports; nothing else earns a routine trial.
- *India note*: the anti-psychotic mis-prescription is the error to actively prevent (the family-brought "she says the world is false" patient who leaves casualty with risperidone and a stigmatised self-concept); the meditation-boundary conversation is the Indian-specific skill (below); costs approx 2026: the consultation-and-psychoeducation tier through DMHP/tele-counselling free-to-nominal; CBT scarce-and-metro (₹600–1,500 per session private; NGO tier lower); SSRIs generic ₹40–200 monthly; lamotrigine ₹150–400.

## 10. Indian practice

Three Indian presentation-doors and their crafts: (1) **The spiritual door**, the intensive-meditation practitioner whose practice-induced detachment persisted: the boundary conversation is the clinical art. Honour the tradition's own distinction (the contemplative literature itself separates wholesome equanimity, with clarity-and-engagement, from the pathological detachment-with-fear-and-impairment); the diagnostic question is not "is this spiritual?" but "is there distress, impairment, and the fear-of-madness signature?"; the prescription is a practice-hiatus, grounding, and the loop-work, not either "go deeper" (the retreat-escalation error) or "stop all spirituality forever" (the alienating overcorrection). (2) **The psychosis-suspicion door**: the family's terrified casualty run; the one insight-question resolves it ("does she KNOW it is a feeling?"), and the family psychoeducation (the difference between feeling unreal and believing unreal) is the intervention that prevents both the antipsychotic error and the marriage-market concealment that follows stigmatised mislabels. (3) **The cannabis-and-student door**: the metro clinic's most common DPDR population: the post-joint persistence in a high-potency era, presented with the classic sentence "I never fully came back", needing the same explanation-plus-abstinence-plus-loop-work architecture (and the honesty that most recover over months, a minority carry it years). The Indian invalidation-pattern to counsel: the "you look normal to us" family response deepens the loop, one family session teaching the two realities (appearance and experience) converts the household from doubter to ally.

## 11. Clinical cases

**Case 1: the joint that never fully ended.** A 19-year-old Pune student: one high-potency joint at a hostel party, an acute "I left myself" panic, and four months since of "the world behind glass, my hands move like machinery, my marks are fine but nothing lands". The internet spiral (DP forums, schizophrenia pages) deepening the fear; sleep collapsing under the monitoring. The consultation that treats before it prescribes: the mechanism explained (the brake that stuck), the explicit not-madness contrast with the psychosis pages' descriptions, the checking-holiday prescription, attention-training drills with the roommate recruited as practice-partner, cannabis abstinence contracted, sleep repaired, and a review at six weeks, the glass thinner, the fear gone by week two ("the fear leaving was half the glass leaving"), near-full re-entry by month four. Teaching points: the post-cannabis form's favourable course once de-feared; the internet-spiral as modern maintenance-loop; the roommate-as-ally is the Indian practical architecture.

**Case 2: the retreat that thinned the world.** A 32-year-old software engineer post-Vipassana-intensive: the practice's dissolution-stages ("everything arising and passing") curdling after the retreat into persistent two-dimensionality and observer-self; the meditation community's split counsel (half "go deeper; this is progress", half "see a psychiatrist"); her own fear that she had "broken something holy". The boundary consultation: the tradition-honouring frame (equanimity-with-engagement vs detachment-with-distress), the practice-hiatus, grounding-and-absorption work, the sleep-and-cannabis ledger (both had crept), and the return-to-practice conversation months later with the teacher involved, shorter sessions, walking-practice emphasis, the monitoring-ethic retired. Teaching points: the practice-induced tier is real and Indian-volume; the teacher-as-ally (rather than adversary) is the treatment's diplomatic architecture; the clinical line is drawn on distress-and-impairment, not on the experience's content.

## 13. FAQ

- *"Am I going mad, doctor? The world looks false."*: The opposite of mad is the exact truth here: you FEEL the falseness and KNOW it is a feeling; madness would be believing the world is false and never doubting it. This is a known, safe, mis-firing protective mechanism, and your insight is intact, which is the good news this condition offers everyone.
- *"Why did it happen to me?"*: The brake that protects us from unbearable moments stuck ON, yours after (the joint / the panic / the retreat / the exam-and-sleepless months). Sensitive brains, often ones that learned early that feelings were unsafe, are the ones whose brakes engage easiest.
- *"Will it ever go away?"*: Many people's fades over months-to-a-year once the fear-cycle is broken; others carry a thinner version for years and live full lives THROUGH the glass. The honest rule: the suffering is treatable even when the feeling lingers, and fighting the feeling is what makes it deepen.
- *"Everyone says I look completely normal."*: They are watching the outside; you are describing the inside; both reports are true. One session with your family explaining the two realities usually turns the household from doubters into your best team.
- *"Should I take medicine?"*: No tablet removes this directly, but the anxiety and depression riding with it respond well to SSRIs, and the DP often thins as they lift. The treatment that targets the DP itself is the de-fearing and attention work; the tablet is the scaffolding, not the wall.
- *"Should I meditate more, or never again?"*: Neither: a pause for now, then a return with shorter sessions and a teacher who knows the difference between equanimity (peace WITH engagement) and this detachment (distance WITH distress). The tradition itself knows the line; we walk it together.
- *"Can it come back after it goes?"*: Spikes with sleeplessness, fever, cannabis or panic are common, and a spike is not a relapse: the person who knows the mechanism rides out the hour without the fear-cycle, and the brake releases on its own schedule.
- *"Is it like dissociative amnesia or possession, the same family?"*: Related brake-systems, different gears: amnesia-and-possession involve losing memory-or-control; yours is detachment with full memory and full control. Different conditions, different treatments, same lesson about the mind's safety engineering.

## 16. Self-test MCQs

**1. The single interview finding that separates DPDR from a psychotic disorder:**
A. Age below 25
B. Preserved insight: the unreality is experienced as a FEELING, with knowledge that it is not a fact
C. Duration under a month
D. Presence of anxiety
**Answer: B.** The insight axis: feeling-unreal-and-knowing versus believing-unreal-with-certainty, the discriminator every exam and every casualty decision turns on.

**2. The mechanism of DPDR per the dominant model:**
A. Dopamine excess in the mesolimbic pathway
B. Cortical-limbic decoupling: prefrontal inhibition of the emotional signal that should have disengaged after the threat
C. Serotonin depletion
D. Autoimmune demyelination
**Answer: B.** The emergency-brake-that-stuck: the model that also explains the flat colour, the robot-self, and the intact knowing.

A. Organic progression: MRI

C. Medication non-response: switch SSRI
D. Emergent psychosis: antipsychotic
**Answer: B.** The loop is the illness's engine; the war-on-the-feeling is its fuel; the specific psychotherapy tier exists for exactly this.
