# Insomnias (Chronic Insomnia Disorder)

## 1. In one line + overview

**Chronic insomnia = difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking too early, at least three nights a week for three months, with daytime consequences, and the first-line treatment is NOT a sleeping tablet but a structured behavioural retraining of the sleep system (CBT-I) that works better and lasts longer.**

Everyone sleeps badly some nights, before an exam, after bad news, with a fever. Insomnia becomes a DISORDER when it settles in: three nights a week or more, for three months or more, with a daytime bill (fatigue, low mood, poor concentration, irritability) that the person cannot afford. The crucial science: long-term insomnia is rarely maintained by whatever started it. A stressor ignites it; the PERSON'S adaptations to it (going to bed earlier to "catch sleep", lying in bed awake for hours, napping, caffeine stacking, sleep-dread, the bedroom becoming a place of frustrated wakefulness) keep it burning years after the original fire dies. This is why hypnotic tablets, which force sleep chemically without touching the maintaining machinery, produce dependency instead of cure, and why CBT-I (cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia), which retrains the pressure, the cues and the beliefs, delivers superior long-term results and is the guidelines' first-line in every major system. For India, insomnia is also the country's most mismanaged common complaint: a vast over-the-counter economy of sedating antihistamines, benzo-dispensing pharmacies and "nerve tonics" manages the nights while manufacturing the dependency.

## 2. Learning objectives

After this note you can:
- Apply the DSM-5 gates: 3 nights/week × 3 months + daytime impairment, despite adequate opportunity
- Use the 3P model (predisposing → precipitating → perpetuating) to explain why the original cause matters less than the maintenance
- Deliver the full CBT-I package: stimulus control, sleep restriction/consolidation, cognitive work, relaxation, sleep hygiene as adjunct
- Run sleep-restriction therapy safely (and know when not to, mania risk, seizure risk, driving safety)
- Choose medicines correctly when needed: Z-drugs short-term, ramelteon, low-dose doxepin, orexin antagonists, and the benzo caution
- Screen the comorbidities: depression, anxiety, PTSD, pain, menopause, sleep apnoea, restless legs, thyroid, caffeine, alcohol
- Work the Indian realities: OTC sedating antihistamines, pharmacy benzo culture, evening-noise homes, night-shift workers, the tea timeline

## 4. Epidemiology

**Global.** Roughly a third of adults report insomnia symptoms in any year; the full chronic disorder runs at ~6–10%; women about 1.5–2× men; prevalence climbs with age and with lower socioeconomic status. Half to two-thirds of chronic insomnia cases travel with a comorbid psychiatric or medical condition (depression is the classic co-passenger; insomnia both symptom and risk factor: new insomnia roughly DOUBLES later depression risk). The treatment gap is inverted here: too much wrong treatment (chronic hypnotics), too little right treatment (CBT-I access is scarce everywhere).

**India.** NMHS 2015–16 found severe sleep problems reported by a meaningful slice of adults (the insomnia item within its severity module ran in double digits for urban women). The Indian clinical reality has four signature distortions: (1) the pharmacy-first pathway, alprazolam/clonazepam/diphenhydramine dispensed over the counter for years; (2) the OTC "sleep aid" market (sedating antihistamines and herbal blends) consumed nightly by a large unrecorded population; (3) the environmental insomnia layer (shared rooms, late-evening household life, traffic noise, heat and mosquitoes) real sleep enemies that behavioural protocols must accommodate, not lecture away; (4) the night-shift workforce (IT/BPO/healthcare) presenting with insomnia whose root is circadian (see Hypersomnia note) but whose treatment-seeking is for "tablets for sleep".

## 5. Causes & risk factors

**The 3P model (the clinical spine):**
- **Predisposing**: family history, anxious/over-alert temperament ("my mind races"), female sex, age-related architecture thinning; the "sleep-worrier" cognitive style
- **Precipitating**: the igniting event, job loss, exam year, pain illness, new baby, grief, depression onset, menopause; shift changes; hospital admission (the ward insomnia that never self-corrects)
- **Perpetuating (THE TREATABLE LAYER)**: going to bed early and lying awake hours; rising late to "recover"; naps; caffeine escalation; alcohol as a nightcap (REM-suppression + 3 a.m. rebound); clock-watching and sleep-performance anxiety ("I must get 8 hours or tomorrow is ruined"); the bedroom trained into a wakefulness arena; daytime catastrophising about sleep, all persisting long after the igniting event retires

**Medical/psychiatric perpetuators to screen in every case:** depression, anxiety disorders, PTSD, psychosis-spectrum, restless legs syndrome (the "crawling legs at 9 p.m.": see below), obstructive sleep apnoea (the snoring, unrefreshing-sleep pattern, commonly misdiagnosed as pure insomnia in the overweight hypertensive), chronic pain, acid reflux, nocturia/prostate, hyperthyroidism, menopausal hot flushes, steroids/SSRIs/bronchodilators, the 8-cups-of-chai caffeine load, and alcohol used as self-medication.

## 6. What happens in the mind/brain

**Story 1: the Pavlovian bedroom.** The brain is an association machine. Pair "bed" with sleep and the bed itself becomes a sleep-trigger. Pair "bed" with hours of frustrated wakefulness, worrying and clock-checking, and the bed becomes a WAKEFULNESS trigger: the person falls asleep in the sofa-chair but snaps alert the moment the head touches the pillow. This conditioned arousal is the core perpetuator in chronic insomnia, and the direct target of stimulus control (bed = sleep, nothing else; out of bed when awake > 15–20 minutes).

**Story 2: the pressure budget.** Sleep pressure (adenosine's meter: see Sleep Basics note) is a daily budget: spend it on the 3 p.m. nap, and the 11 p.m. account is short. Chronic insomniacs respond to a bad night by "bed earlier + morning lie-in + afternoon nap", a triple spending-cut of exactly the pressure the next night needs, guaranteeing a fresh bad night. Sleep-restriction therapy is the counter-intuitive restore: deliberately compress the sleep WINDOW (e.g., 6 hours) so pressure runs high and sleep consolidates; then titrate the window wider as efficiency returns. The patient's first reaction ("you want me to sleep LESS?") is the treatment's entry ritual.

**Story 3: the performance spiral.** Sleep is the one bodily function that fails when you TRY; trying to sleep is arousal (the paradox every patient knows at 2 a.m.). After months of bad nights, sleep becomes a PERFORMANCE, monitored, scored and feared: the sleeper wakes up "to check the time and start worrying about the next day". Cognitive work dismantles the sleep-performance beliefs ("8 hours or ruin"; "one bad night wrecks tomorrow"; "lying quietly is useless"), because half of chronic insomnia is the fear of insomnia.

## 7. Symptoms

- **Onset insomnia**: lying awake > 20–30 minutes at sleep-start (the racing mind, the conditioned-arousa pattern)
- **Maintenance insomnia**: waking mid-night, returning to sleep slowly (pain, apnoea, alcohol-rebound, menopausal flush, depression's early-morning pattern variants)
- **Terminal insomnia**: waking too early, unable to return (the classic depressive signature, but also the phase-advanced elder)
- **Non-restorative sleep**: hours slept, waking unrefreshed (apnoea and substance-suppressed architecture flags)
- **The daytime bill**: fatigue (not sleepiness, usually, the distinction from hypersomnias), irritability, concentration and memory complaints, tension headaches, low mood, and the anxiety about the coming night itself
- **The behavioural signature to elicit**: early-to-bed, lie-in, naps, caffeine timeline, nightcap alcohol, clock-facing bed, phone-in-bed, and the bed-wakefulness pattern (falls asleep in the chair, alert in the bed)

## 8. Diagnosis

**The logic (DSM-5, paraphrased):** a complaint of dissatisfaction with sleep quantity/quality (difficulty initiating, maintaining, or early waking with inability to return); occurring despite adequate opportunity and circumstances; with daytime consequences; ≥ 3 nights/week; ≥ 3 months; not better explained by another sleep disorder (apnoea, restless legs, circadian misalignment, the screen-list), substance effects, or coexisting conditions doing the whole work (comorbid insomnia is diagnosed ALONGSIDE them, not "secondary", the modern doctrine).

**The assessment craft:**
- **The two-week sleep diary is the examination of this disorder**, bedtime, lights-out, latency, wakes and durations, out-of-bed time, naps, caffeine/alcohol times, perceived quality; no other instrument localises the perpetuating pattern this well
- The three screening anamneses that every insomnia workup owes: snoring/witnessed pauses (apnoea), the 9 p.m. leg-crawl (restless legs), and the mood/PTSD/depression screens
- The caffeine and tea timeline quantified in cups AND clock-times; alcohol quantified honestly ("for sleep" is the phrase to catch)
- Medication inventory including OTC antihistamines and "herbal" products by name
- Instruments: insomnia severity index (ISI) as the tracking measure (named, not reproduced); actigraphy where diary integrity is doubtful; polysomnography NOT routine: reserved for suspected apnoea/parasomnia cases

### Differential table

| Looks like insomnia | The distinguishing feature |
|---|---|
| Sleep deprivation (lifestyle) | Adequate sleep possible when opportunity exists (new parent, gaming nights, two jobs) |
| Delayed sleep phase (circadian) | Normal sleep ONSET AND MAINTENANCE at late times, weekends reveal the pattern |
| Obstructive sleep apnoea | Snoring, witnessed pauses, unrefreshing sleep, weight; screens with STOP-BANG-style questions |
| Restless legs syndrome | The 9 p.m. crawl-urge relieved by movement; sleep-onset failure follows the legs |
| Depression | Early waking, mood, anhedonia, diurnal variation: treated together, not sequentially |
| Anxiety/PTSD | The racing mind and the nightmare pattern respectively |
| Menopausal insomnia | Hot-flush-locked wakes; hormonal context |
| Paradoxical insomnia ("sleep-state misperception") | The patient reports near-zero sleep; partners and actigraphy show hours of it, a real distress entity, treated with the same cognitive architecture |
| Substance/m medication effect | The tea, the steroid, the SSRI, the bronchodilator, the 8 p.m. whisky |

## 9. Management

**Step 1: the assessment-and-reframe consultation.** The diary is collected; the 3P model is drawn for the patient ("your stressor left months ago; these five habits are the squatters now"); the treatment is named honestly: "the first-line treatment worldwide is a therapy, not a tablet, 4–8 sessions, and the results outlast the treatment by years". Patients on years of hypnotics hear the exit plan framed as an upgrade, not a punishment.

**Step 2: CBT-I, the first-line package (4–8 sessions):**
1. **Sleep hygiene: the foundation tier, not the treatment**: consistent rise-time (the circadian anchor, fixed BEFORE bedtime), caffeine cut-off 8 hours before bed, no alcohol as nightcap, dim evening light, cool dark room, the Indian-environment tier (mosquito net, fan timing, the household quiet-hour negotiation)
2. **Stimulus control, the conditioning reversal**: bed only for sleep; out of bed within ~20 minutes of wakefulness, to a dim chair, back only when sleepy; no phone/clock-watching in bed; fixed rise-time regardless of the night's quality
3. **Sleep restriction/consolidation, the pressure rebuild**: set the sleep WINDOW to the person's actual average sleep time (floor 5–6 hours), anchored to the fixed rise-time; expand by 15–20 minutes per week once efficiency passes ~85–90%; safety cautions below
4. **Cognitive restructuring of sleep beliefs**: the 8-hours myth (adults range 6–9), the "lying awake is useless" myth (quiet rest is physiologically valuable), the one-bad-night catastrophe (tested against the actual record), and the performance paradox (sleep arrives by invitation, not pursuit; "let sleep come to you")
5. **Relaxation training**: slow-exhale breathing, progressive muscle relaxation, as arousal-flooring adjuncts, never as "performance-of-relaxation" (the paradox trap again)
6. **Relapse-prevention module**: the drill for the bad-night windows (exam seasons, weddings, illness), the rules to hold (rise-time, no early bed), the rules that may bend (one recovery lie-in capped at +1 hour)
- Safety notes for restriction: caution in mania history (sleep loss is a trigger, psychiatric review during initiation), epilepsy (sleep deprivation lowers thresholds), safety-critical occupations (driving, timing the protocol to leave/vacations), severe bipolar I (restrict WITH mood-stabiliser cover)

**Step 3: pharmacotherapy (the honest tiered position):**
- **Short-term/necessity use** during CBT-I initiation, travel, acute stress windows; prescribe QUANTITY-LIMITED courses with the taper written from day one
- **Z-drugs** (zolpidem 5–10 mg, zopiclone/eszopiclone equivalents): onset-insomnia use; short courses; the complex-sleep-behaviour warnings (sleepwalking/sleep-eating: contraindicated with evening alcohol; see Parasomnias note)
- **Ramelteon** (melatonin-receptor agonist): onset-tier, non-scheduled profile, useful in elder and dependence-risk groups
- **Low-dose doxepin** (3–6 mg): maintenance-flavoured, histamine-blockade mechanism, no dependence signal; a good Indian-friendly choice (low cost as imipramine-class availability varies; the 3–6 mg dosing needs the specific formulation where available, else use pragmatic tricyclic micro-dosing only with informed counsel)
- **Orexin antagonists (DORAs: suvorexant, lemborexant, daridorexant)**: the modern class, hypnotic by wake-promotion blockade, no dependence architecture, next-day-load monitoring; the cost/availability tier in India is private-import/limited; name the class for students, prescribe where accessible
- **Benzodiazepines**: the caution tier, effective acutely, tolerance/dependence/fragmentation/cognition risks (see Benzo Misuse note for the exit protocols); avoid initiating long-term in elders absolutely
- **What NOT to prescribe as nightly treatment**: sedating antihistamines (diphenhydramine, promethazine), anticholinergic load, tolerance, fall-risk in elders, and India's most-consumed OTC sleep trap
- **Melatonin (over-the-counter)**: a chronobiotic, not a sedative, works on TIMING at 0.5–5 mg taken 2–5 hours before desired sleep for phase-shifting (and in jet-lag protocols); the "pop a 10 mg at bedtime" pattern is pharmacologically naive
- **Trazodone** 25–100 mg: the pragmatic Indian antidepressant-hypnotic for the comorbid-depression tier; useful, honest, monitor morning load
- Where psychiatric comorbidity IS the insomnia: treat the driver (SSRI for depression with the sleep plan riding along; the "comorbid, treat-together" doctrine)

*India note (approx 2026): zolpidem 10 mg ≈ ₹60–150/week of use; ramelteon scarce/imported; doxepin formulation-limited (tricyclic micro-dose pragmatism where unavailable); trazodone ≈ ₹100–200/month; melatonin 3 mg ≈ ₹150–400/month (unregulated quality variance, buy reputable); CBT-I private ≈ ₹600–1,500/session; the district/tele tier (Tele-MANAS 14416) delivers basic sleep-protocol counselling. The highest-yield Indian practice upgrade: the pharmacy-insomnia pathway (years of alprazolam/diphenhydramine dispensed without prescription) met by a 10-minute GP protocol (diary, caffeine timeline, stimulus-control leaflet, quantity-limited hypnotic with a written stop-date) converts a dependency mill into a referral funnel.*

## 10. Indian practice

- **The tea timeline is the first intervention.** In much of India, "bedtime chai" is served after dinner; the 5–6 hour caffeine half-life places it squarely in the sleep-onset window. The cut-off prescription (no caffeine after ~3 p.m., the evening chai switched to milk/lemon-grass variants) resolves a meaningful minority of "insomnias" alone, and the family's serving habit must be negotiated, not just the patient's.
- **The joint-family night.** Late-evening household life (TV serials, late dinners, relatives' calls) and shared rooms maintain insomnia environmentally. Prescribe the negotiation: a household quiet-hour, the room-shuffle that gives the poor sleeper the quiet side, the earplugs-and-eye-mask tier, and the dinner-3-hours-before-bed rule where culturally feasible (the 10 p.m. dinner is its own insomnia engine).
- **The pharmacy sedation culture.** The de-facto Indian insomnia treatment is dispensed without a prescription: alprazolam, clonazepam, diphenhydramine, "nerve tonics". Every insomnia consult is an exit-point opportunity: the OTC inventory taken by name, the taper-or-stop plan offered with dignity (see the Benzo note's architecture), and the behavioural treatment positioned as the upgrade. Prescribing quantity-limited hypnotics with written stop-dates interrupts the years-long refill default.
- **The menopausal cohort.** Indian women's menopausal insomnia (flush-locked wakes, the unhoused night-sweat distress, the "I have become irritable" presentation) reaches gynaecologists and faith-healers more than psychiatrists; the honest tier: HRT counsel where appropriate (individualised), CBT-I for the perpetuating layer, and the family briefing that the 2 a.m. wakeful mother is not "difficult" but symptomatic.
- **The night-shift presentation.** The BPO worker asking for "sleeping tablets" has a circadian disorder wearing insomnia's clothes (see Hypersomnia note), hypnotics at 8 a.m. after a shift deepen the derangement; the correct prescriptions are light-blocking sunglasses on the commute home, black-out curtains, a fixed "anchor sleep" window, and strategic caffeine with a hard 2 a.m. cut-off.
- **Faith and the night.** Early-morning religious schedules, night prayers in Ramadan (shifted sleep for a month), and the devout elder's 4 a.m. devotions are rhythm-facts, not disorders, assess the whole pattern before pathologising the clock-time; where distress exists, adapt the devotions' timing rather than abolish them.

## 11. Clinical cases (de-identified)

**Case 1: the woman who went to bed at 9 to catch sleep.** A 52-year-old Kochi homemaker developed insomnia after her son's accident (the precipitant); eighteen months later, despite his full recovery, she lay in bed from 9 p.m. "to make sure", woke at 2, clock-watched, napped at 3 p.m. "to survive", and drank 5 cups of tea including a 9 p.m. cup, plus a nightly brandy "the only thing that works". She took diphenhydramine most nights, groggy by morning. The diary showed: 9.5 hours in bed, 5.2 hours slept, 55% efficiency. Management: the 3P drawing (the precipitant long gone; the squatters named); CBT-I: stimulus control (bed = sleep only, out of bed at 20 minutes of wake, no clock facing the bed), restriction to a 12:00 a.m.–6:00 a.m. window (the shock-and-consent conversation), the tea timeline cut to a 3 p.m. ceiling, the brandy retired with its REM-rebound explained, diphenhydramine stopped (anticholinergic counsel at her age), and the cognitive module on her "6 hours will ruin me" belief, tested against her own diary's functional days. Weeks 1–2: harder nights (expected, told); weeks 3–6: sleep consolidated, window titrated 12:00→11:15→10:45; month 4: 7 hours asleep, 90% efficiency, one relapse-week after a wedding season managed with the drill. Teaching points: (1) the diary's in-bed/asleep arithmetic is the diagnosis in numbers; (2) restriction's paradox is the treatment's entry ritual, consent and warning both scripted; (3) the tea, the brandy, the nap and the diphenhydramine were four separate maintenance engines, each named and retired.

**Case 2: the young shift-leader with "total insomnia".** A 27-year-old Bengaluru BPO team lead, 4 years of night shifts, requested "stronger sleeping tablets"; zolpidem 10 mg had stopped working, and he was taking it with a beer at 8 a.m. to sleep in daylight. Diary: sleep from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m., lying in bed 9 a.m.–4 p.m. "trying", heavy caffeine until 3 a.m., weekend attempts to "sleep like a human" producing Sunday-night collapses. Assessment: circadian-rhythm sleep disorder (shift-work type) with hypnotic tolerance and alcohol co-sedation, insomnia's costume on a clock disorder's body. Management: the reframe (the tablets were fighting the sun, literally); the shift-sleep protocol: black-out curtains and a sleep-anchor of 9 a.m.–5 p.m., sunglasses on the 6:30 a.m. commute home, caffeine ceiling at 1 a.m., the zolpidem tapered and reserved for protocol-break weeks, the weekend compromise (one "human day" with a capped 2-hour shift and a 2 a.m. re-entry, not a full flip); a job-timing conversation (he moved to a rotating schedule with a fixed day-shift month within the year). At 3 months: 6.5 anchored hours, no hypnotic, performance restored; at 6 months the day-shift rotation held the gain. Teaching points: (1) shift-insomnia is light-management, not sedation; (2) hypnotic-plus-alcohol co-sedation in shift workers is the safety conversation; (3) the "weekend flip" strategy is the circadian saboteur, the capped-transition protocol replaces it.

## 13. FAQ: asked the way patients and families ask it

**"Just give me a good sleeping tablet, doctor."** I will when we need one, short-term, quantity-limited, with a stop-date written from day one. But here is the honest medicine: the tablets force sleep chemically and stop working predictably, while the structured therapy retrains the sleep system and keeps working years after it ends. That therapy is the world-guideline first-line, and we start it today.

**"Why can't I sleep even when I am exhausted?"** Because exhaustion and sleep-pressure are different currencies, and because your bed has learned wakefulness; hours of lying awake have TRAINED it. We un-train it with rules that feel odd (out of bed when awake; a shorter sleep window at first) and that work.

**"I go to bed early to catch up. Isn't that sensible?"** It is the most natural mistake in insomnia: the early bed adds empty wakeful hours, trains the bed further, and spends nothing of the pressure the night needs. We reverse it: fixed rise-time, bed later, window matched to what you actually sleep.

**"I have taken alprazolam for six years and it barely works now. Am I addicted?"** The word is tolerance-and-dependence, and the situation is extremely common in India; you are not alone and not at fault. The exit is structured and works: a planned taper, the behavioural treatment taking over the nights, and a review rhythm. People feel like themselves again on the other side of it.

**"My mother takes a cetirizine/diphenhydramine every night to sleep. Is that safe?"** Sedating antihistamines are India's most common hidden sleep habit, and in elders they carry fall-risk, memory-burden, dry-mouth/urinary effects, and lose power within weeks anyway. It is the wrong tool sold as the gentle one. We will show you the safer tiers.

**"Will I need therapy for sleep forever?"** No, CBT-I is deliberately short: typically 4–8 sessions, ending with a relapse drill you own. The skills are like swimming: once trained, they stay.

**"Does melatonin work?"** It works on TIMING, not on force; useful when the body clock is shifted (shift work, jet lag, delayed-phase teens) and taken at the right hours in the right dose. As a nightly knockout tablet, it mostly disappoints; that is not its job.

**"Everyone says try a brandy/wine before bed."** Alcohol sedates you into lighter, broken sleep, it suppresses deep sleep and the dream department, then rebounds you awake at 3 a.m. The nightcap is renting the evening at morning-interest rates.

**"I barely slept at all last night, zero hours."** Genuine near-zero nights are rare; hours of shallow sleep usually feel like zero, and there is a well-described pattern where the sleep meter itself misreads (even partners see you sleeping). The distress is real; the treatment is the same cognitive-and-behavioural package, and the measurement usually surprises us both.

**"If I can't sleep, should I at least rest in bed quietly?"** Quiet rest is genuinely valuable for the body; that part of the belief is true. The part we change is the LOCATION: resting in bed trains the bed-wakefulness link. Rest on the sofa or chair; keep the bed for sleep alone.

**"My father wakes at 4:30 a.m. and is upset about it. Can he be treated?"** If he sleeps soundly 8 p.m.–4:30 and functions by day with an afternoon nap, his clock has simply shifted with age; the treatment is expectation and nap-timing, not sedatives. If he is awake through the night and distressed, that is true insomnia, and it gets the same careful package, gentler titration, and NO long-term benzodiazepine (falls and confusion are the elder's specific dangers).

## 14. Exam & student lens

**Gates to memorise:** ≥ 3 nights/week, ≥ 3 months, daytime consequences, adequate opportunity (the deprivation discriminator); insomnia is diagnosed WITH comorbidities ("comorbid", not "secondary"), the modern doctrine question.

**Mnemonic, stimulus control, the "BOOT-4":** **B**ed for sleep/intimacy only; **O**ut of bed if awake ~20 min; **O**ccur-sleep only when sleepy (not scheduled effort); **T**ime: fixed rise-time regardless of night. Add: no clock-watching.

**High-yield facts:**
- CBT-I = first-line (ACP 2016 guideline is the citable anchor), effects durable post-treatment; hypnotics only short-term
- 3P model: Spielman/Caruso; PERPETUATING layer is the treatment target
- Sleep-restriction safety: mania risk, epilepsy, driving occupations
- Insomnia roughly doubles later-depression risk (the longitudinal marker)
- Z-drug complex sleep behaviours warning; no evening alcohol co-use
- DORAs (orexin antagonists) = the modern class, no-dependence architecture; know the names
- Ramelteon (onset, non-scheduled); low-dose doxepin (maintenance)
- Melatonin = chronobiotic, not hypnotic: timing-and-dose science
- Diphenhydramine nightly = the OTC trap (elder anticholinergic, tolerance)
- Paradoxical insomnia exists (mismatch diagnosis)
- Screen every insomnia for: apnoea, restless legs, depression, the caffeine/alcohol timeline

**Classic traps:**
1. Prescribing long-term hypnotics as "management" of chronic insomnia (guidelines say CBT-I first)
2. Missing sleep apnoea behind "insomnia" in the overweight snorer, treating with zolpidem
3. Forgetting the mania-hazard of sleep-restriction initiation in bipolar patients
4. "Melatonin 10 mg at bedtime" as a knockout, the chronobiotic misuse
5. Missing restless legs (the 9 p.m. crawl) behind onset-insomnia
6. The night-shift worker's circadian disorder treated with morning hypnotics

**Indian exam corner:** "insomnia, diagnostic criteria and management", "CBT-I components" (the evergreen), "pharmacotherapy of insomnia, classes and cautions", "3P model", "sleep hygiene principles". Indian-context marks: the pharmacy-first pathway, the OTC antihistamine trap, the joint-family environment adaptations, shift-work presentations, NMHS sleep-problem data.

**Answer: B.** The ACP 2016 position and all successors, durable superiority over hypnotics.

**4. In stimulus control, a person awake in bed for ~20 minutes should:**
A. Try harder to sleep
B. Take an extra half tablet
C. Leave the bed, do something calm in dim light, return only when sleepy
D. Watch the clock to time the insomnia
**Answer: C.** Bed = sleep only; arousal out of the arena; sleepy return.

**5. Sleep-restriction therapy requires caution in all EXCEPT:**
A. History of mania
B. Epilepsy
C. Safety-critical driving occupation
D. Simple tension-type headache
**Answer: D.** Mania-triggering, seizure-threshold and driving-safety are the three standard cautions.

**6. The correct position of melatonin in insomnia treatment:**
A. A powerful nightly hypnotic at 10 mg
B. A chronobiotic acting on timing at 0.5–5 mg, hours before desired sleep in phase-shifting contexts
C. A morning stimulant
D. An antidepressant
**Answer: B.** Timing-medicine, not force-medicine: the dosage-and-timing science point.

**7. A overweight hypertensive snorer with unrefreshing sleep and "insomnia" complaints most needs:**

**8. The Indian pharmacy-first insomnia pathway most characteristically produces:**
