# Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD)

## 1. In one line + overview

**OCD is a disorder of stuck alarm signals: unwanted intrusive thoughts (obsessions) that the mind appraises as dangerous or meaningful, driving repetitive behaviours or mental rituals (compulsions) that briefly relieve and permanently entrench the whole circuit.**

Nearly everyone has intrusive thoughts, a fleeting image of shouting in a silent hall, a "what if I swerve" while driving, a doubt about whether the stove was really off. In OCD, the machinery that should dismiss these as mental noise BREAKS at the appraisal step: the thought is read as meaningful, dangerous, or revealing of one's true character ("having this thought means I might DO it, or that I AM it"). That catastrophic appraisal demands action (checking, washing, praying, neutralising, confessing) and the action works for minutes. That minute of relief is the trap: it teaches the brain that the ritual saved the day, so the alarm re-arms louder next time. The disorder lives in the loop: obsession → appraisal → compulsion → relief → stronger loop. Two clarifications matter daily in Indian clinics: OCD is NOT "liking orderliness" (that is personality, and the crucial ego-dystonic distinction follows in section 8), and it is eminently treatable, exposure-and-response-prevention therapy plus high-dose SSRIs routinely rescue lives that families had written off as "madness", "possessed", or "just difficult".

## 2. Learning objectives

After this note you can:
- Define obsession and compulsion precisely, with the ego-dystonic core
- Name the four classic content dimensions, including taboo thoughts, and why patients hide the last
- Explain the Salkovskis appraisal model: intrusive thoughts are universal; responsibility-laden appraisal creates the disorder
- Understand thought-action fusion and why reassurance is a compulsion
- Deliver ERP (exposure and response prevention) as a concept patients consent to
- Run SSRI pharmacotherapy at OCD doses (higher, slower) and know clomipramine and augmentation rules
- Apply DSM-5's insight specifiers and the tic-related modifier
- Distinguish OCD from OCPD, psychosis, and GAD worry
- Work Indian realities: religious scrupulosity, purity-contamination frames, family accommodation, tantrik detours

## 4. Epidemiology

**Global.** Lifetime prevalence ~2–3%; the WHO once ranked OCD among the most disabling illnesses by lost-work-years. Onset has two peaks, childhood (boys, often tic-related) and early adulthood (women), with a mean age around 20; onset after 35 should prompt an organic hunt (basal ganglia events, postpartum, medication). The concealment factor: taboo-content patients (sexual, religious, aggressive intrusions) present, on average, YEARS after onset, often with depression or an addiction first, because they fear disclosure more than the disorder. Comorbidity: depression (half), anxiety disorders, tics, body-focused behaviours; suicide risk is real, driven by both misery and the unspoken content.

**India.** NMHS 2015–16 did not isolate OCD (pooled under mental morbidity ~10% overall). Indian clinical experience carries clear patterns: contamination-dimension dominance with purity-pollution framing (bathing rituals after temple visits, "madi" and similar ritual-purity constructs getting fused with the disorder), religious scrupulosity read as devotion by families (prayer compulsions concealed inside piety), and a characteristic treatment lag, the family circuit of tantriks and temples before psychiatry, averaging years in most series. The Indian family's accommodation is intense and well-meaning (separate utensils managed, doors re-locked by others, clothes washed separately), and it is a treatment target as much as the patient's ritual.

## 5. Causes & risk factors

**Biological.**
- Heritability ~40–50%; childhood-onset, tic-related cases carry stronger genetic loading
- Cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical (CSTC) loop dysfunction: the "alarm-gate" circuitry stuck in repetition; the reason basal-ganglia events and streptococcal immune mechanisms are implicated
- Paediatric autoimmune neuropsychiatric syndrome (historically PANDAS): abrupt childhood OCD/tics after infections; the immune trigger concept is real but the specific PANDAS construct remains contested; post-infectious and postpartum onsets deserve an organic timeline review
- Drug-induced: some Parkinson's dopamine-agonist states present with compulsive behaviours (punding, gambling; see Parkinson's note, conceptually adjacent)

**Psychological (the model that drives treatment).**
- Intrusive thoughts are universal (80–90% of people report them)
- The appraisal converts them to disorder: **inflated responsibility** ("if I think it and something happens, I caused it") and **thought-action fusion** (thinking = doing; thinking = wanting)
- Perfectionist, over-conscientious, guilt-prone cognitive styles as soil
- Reassurance-seeking and neutralising are compulsions, even when they look like reasonable questions

**Social / cultural.**
- Religious frameworks with strong purity codes shape the CONTENT (which thoughts and acts get flagged) without causing the disorder; scrupulosity-flavoured OCD concentrates in devout practitioners of every faith
- Childhood strictness around "sinful thoughts" and family models of anxiety
- Indian context: joint families provide a large accommodation workforce (more hands to lock, wash, fetch), and the tantrik pathway absorbs the first years

## 6. What happens in the mind/brain

**Story 1: the faulty metal detector.** Airport security has a detector and a protocol: a beep triggers a wand-wave, then a pat-down, then, finding nothing, a RESET. OCD is a detector whose reset is broken: the pat-down (the checking) never updates the "all clear" register. So the mind pats the thought down again, and again, and again, not because the search finds anything, but because the search cannot file a "cleared" verdict. ERP works by walking through the detector repeatedly WITHOUT the pat-down, until the reset circuit relearns its job: beep, no consequence, stand down.

**Story 2: the internal debate club.** The OCD mind runs a debate club that takes every intrusive thought seriously. "You imagined pushing your baby: DEBATE THIS." Normal minds laugh the thought out of the hall in seconds; the OCD mind assigns a prosecutor (guilt), a defence (reassurance-seeking), and an endless chair (the rumination). The treatment fires the debate club: thoughts are allowed to sit in the audience, un-debated, un-voted, un-tallied. What the patient fears is not the thought but the MEANING assigned to it; the meaning, not the thought, is what therapy re-trains.

**Story 3: the family's love as fuel.** A family member re-locking the door for the patient, providing the "clean" vessel, answering the same question a twentieth time; all of it is love translated into accommodation. But each accommodation is the disorder's assistant: it confirms that the alarm was real and that the ritual's requirements must be met. Reducing accommodation (kindly, gradually, with a therapist's help) is experienced at first as cruelty and later as rescue, the hardest and most family-transforming part of Indian OCD treatment.

## 7. Symptoms

### Obsessions: the four content dimensions
1. **Contamination**: germs, dirt, bodily secretions, "sticky" sensations, pollution, in India, fused with ritual purity frames (untouchable items, "becoming impure" states requiring washes)
2. **Harm/aggressive + checking**: "did I hit someone with the car?", "is the gas really off?", "will I harm my child?": the checking dimension
3. **Symmetry/exactness/just-right**: ordering, arranging, counting until it "feels right", the sensory-motor gut-feel of incompleteness (closest to tic phenomenology)
4. **Taboo intrusive thoughts**: sexual (incest images, unwanted arousal doubts), religious (blasphemous images during prayer, the scrupulosity of every faith), aggressive (the knife-image while holding the baby); THE dimension that hides: patients fear these thoughts mean they are monsters, and clinicians who show alarm confirm the fear

**Mental phenomena to ask about by name**: rumination (mental debating), mental counting, silent praying/neutralising phrases, mental replaying of actions ("did I do it correctly?"), and pathological doubt.

### Compulsions: overt and covert
- Washing/bathing (the Indian marathon bath: hours, specific sequences, family-managed water logistics)
- Checking (locks, gas, re-reading, re-driving the route to check for accident victims)
- Repeating and counting rituals; "evening-up" and symmetry arranging
- Ordering/arranging until "right"
- Hoarding-flavoured collecting (a separate DSM-5 disorder when primary; see differential)
- Mental rituals: neutralising phrases, mental prayers, replay-checking, reassurance-seeking itself
- **Reassurance-seeking as a compulsion**, the disguised form: "just confirm the baby is fine", the hourly phone call, the internet-search loop; family members are the apparatus

### The insight spectrum (DSM-5 specifiers)
- Good/fair insight: "I know it is my OCD, but it grips me"
- Poor insight: "it is probably real"
- **Absent insight / delusional conviction: "the contamination IS real"**: OCD still, treated as OCD (higher-dose, harder engagement), not schizophrenia

### Course
Episodic-worsening; untreated, chronic and gradually consuming (untreated severe OCD is one of psychiatry's most disabling states); a minority remit fully. Postpartum and stress-triggered flares are standard.

## 8. Diagnosis

**The logic (DSM-5, paraphrased):** obsessions (intrusive, unwanted, causing marked distress/anxiety) and/or compulsions (repetitive behaviours or mental acts the person feels driven to perform, per rigid rules, aimed at preventing/reducing distress or some dreaded event, realistically unconnected or clearly excessive); time-consuming (more than an hour a day by common convention) or clinically impairing; the person is not a substance/medical cause; specifiers for insight and tics. ICD-11 houses OCD in its own block, separating it from anxiety disorders, a structural echo of DSM-5's re-chaptering.

**The interview craft:**
- NORMALISE FIRST, then ask: "almost everyone gets odd intrusive thoughts (images of shouting in a quiet place, doubts, 'what if') do any visit you and refuse to leave?" This single framing converts concealed patients into disclosed ones
- Hunt the mental rituals explicitly: "when the thought comes, what do you DO inside your head?": mental neutralising is invisible unless asked
- Quantify time and family accommodation: hours/day of rituals, who else performs them, what the household has reorganised around
- Taboo-content screening done with flat, professional calm; the clinician's non-flinch is the diagnostic instrument
- Timeline: childhood onset vs new late onset (organic review); postpartum flares; infection-triggered abrupt childhood changes

**Instruments by name:** Y-BOCS (Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale, the standard severity measure; named, not reproduced); CY-BOCS for children; DOCS as dimension-focused alternative.

### Differential table

| Looks like OCD | The distinguishing feature |
|---|---|
| OCPD (obsessive-compulsive PERSONALITY) | Ego-SYNTONIC: the perfectionist likes their system, no intrusions, no rituals, no distress about it; the person IS the orderliness; OCD hates its intrusions |
| Psychosis | True delusions and hallucinations un-related to intrusive-thought appraisal; the OCD delusional-insight case still shows ritual structure |
| GAD | Worry is verbal-realistic ("finances"), not image-taboo; no neutralising rituals |
| Health anxiety | Disease themes with checking/reassurance, closer to OCD's checking dimension; treat on the OCD spectrum when rituals dominate |
| Tic disorders | Tics are sensory-motor urges, no cognitive content of threat; the "just-right" dimension overlaps |
| Body dysmorphic disorder | Fixed appearance-defect belief with mirror-checking: a cousin on the spectrum |
| Hoarding disorder | Acquiring/failing to discard regardless of value; distress at discarding, not at intrusions |
| Scrupulous normal religious practice | Devotion without distress, impairment, or unwanted intrusions; OCD's religiosity is agonised, not nourished |
| Paediatric sudden-onset + neurological signs | Inflammatory/autoimmune workup consideration (PANDAS-contested territory) |

## 9. Management

**The architecture: ERP first or ERP-plus-medicine; never medication alone when ERP is available.**

**Step 1: the re-framing consultation.** OCD is presented as a faulty alarm circuit, not a character failing, not madness, and (to religious families) not a spiritual verdict; scrupulosity is OCD wearing the family's own treasured symbols, and the treatment preserves faith while retiring the torment. The patient's relief at hearing "your thoughts are not you" is often the first breath of the entire illness.

**Step 2: Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP), the specific psychotherapy:**
1. Symptom mapping: every obsession, every ritual (overt AND mental), every family accommodation, on paper
2. Hierarchy construction: feared situations ranked by distress (0–100)
3. Graded exposure with PREVENTED rituals: touch the "contaminated" door handle and do NOT wash, for the designated period; drive the route past where "the accident might have been" and do NOT circle back
4. The learning target: "the thought came, I did not neutralise, and NOTHING happened", the alarm's re-education
5. Mental-ritual prevention is as essential as hand-washing prevention: the patient who stops washing but silently prays to neutralise has changed uniforms, not sides
6. Family accommodation reduction module: relatives stop re-locking/re-washing/answering the 20th asking, kindly, on a written schedule, with the therapist's scaffolding
7. Relapse-prevention: the map and the drills travel with the patient for life
- Format: 12–20 sessions typical; intensive formats for severe cases; guided self-help and tele-ERP extend reach

**Step 3: pharmacotherapy (the OCD-specific rules):**
- **SSRIs at HIGHER doses and LONGER trials than depression**: sertraline up to 200 mg, fluoxetine up to 80 mg, fluvoxamine up to 300 mg, escitalopram up to 30 mg(20 in many markets); paroxetine up to 60 mg: target trials 10–12 WEEKS at maximum tolerated dose before declaring failure (the commonest prescribing error: judging an SSRI failed at 8 weeks on a half-dose)
- **Clomipramine** 150–250 mg: the oldest evidence, equal-or-better efficacy; ECG and anticholinergic load considered; still the classic second-line/exam favourite
- **First failure → switch SSRI→clomipramine (or vice versa) → augmentation**: low-dose antipsychotic (risperidone 0.5–2 mg, aripiprazole 5–10 mg; haloperidol historically), most evidence in TIC-RELATED OCD; glutamate agents (memantine, N-acetylcysteine) as research-tier adjuncts
- Maintenance: 1–2 years minimum after response; slow tapers; relapse drills
- Treatment-resistant severe cases: **deep TMS** (FDA-cleared for OCD), and **DBS** (targeting the VC/VS-nucleus accumbens region) in exceptional programmes; the referral ladder to name, not to run in a district hospital

**Step 4, the comorbidities:** depression treated on its own track; tics; the substance layer; and the suicide screen: concealed taboo-content OCD plus depression is a genuine risk constellation.

*India note (approx 2026): fluoxetine 40 mg ≈ ₹60–140/month; sertraline 200 mg ≈ ₹160–320/month; fluvoxamine ≈ ₹200–450/month; clomipramine 150 mg ≈ ₹250–500/month (ECG at a district centre ≈ ₹100–200); risperidone 1 mg augmentation ≈ ₹40–100/month. ERP: private ≈ ₹600–1,500/session; the honest Indian problem is ERP scarcity, not pill scarcity; most Indian cities are rich in SSRIs and poor in trained ERP therapists. The workaround tier: NIMHANS/DMHP-trained psychologists, tele-ERP through medical-college programmes, and structured guided-self-help (the OCD workbooks' hierarchy-and-diary architecture) with monthly therapist review. Family accommodation reduction is FREE and family-delivered; the highest-yield Indian adaptation, because the Indian joint family is either the disorder's largest staff or the treatment's largest workforce.*

## 10. Indian practice

- **The purity-contamination fusion.** Contamination OCD in India frequently fuses with ritual-purity constructs (specific washes after specific exposures, purity states and their loss, items "not to be touched"). Clinical craft: NEVER pathologise the faith frame itself; separate the disorder (the intrusions, the hours, the distress, the impairment) from the practice (the tradition's own norms), and treat to the traditional norm: "your tradition prescribes a defined bath, not a four-hour one; the alarm has hijacked the ritual, and we are repossessing it."
- **Scrupulosity.** The prayer-compulsion patient is often the family's most pious member and proudest exhibit; the family resists "treating the devotion". The alliance sentence: "the treatment is not against God; it is against the anxiety that is using God's name to torment you; devotion is chosen, this is not."
- **The tantrik years.** By the time Indian OCD patients reach psychiatry, most families have done the temple/tantrik circuit (rituals mirrored by rituals, an unfortunately well-matched treatment for the disorder's mechanics). Engage respectfully: the healer's frame ("spirits") and the clinical frame ("a stuck alarm") can co-exist; the measurement frame ("bathing hours, door-checks per night, school-days attended") is what all parties can track.
- **The family as staff.** Map the accommodation workforce early: who re-locks, who washes, who fetches "pure" vessels, who answers the 20th asking. Prescribe the reduction schedule in writing: family members experience it as withdrawal of love until briefed that accommodation is the disorder's oxygen.
- **Concealed taboo content and marriage.** Young adults with sexual/religious intrusive content commonly present via "depression" or "anxiety" while hiding the core; the normalising screening question ("almost everyone gets odd intrusive thoughts...") is the single highest-yield interview skill in Indian OCD practice, it routinely uncovers cases hidden for five years. The marriage-timing question arises often: stabilise and disclose to at least one senior member on the other side, per the framework in the Bipolar note (same principle, smaller magnitude).
- **Childhood OCD in school records.** Morning ritual marathons present as "school refusal"; the school's letter and the counsellor's symptom-map (time lost per morning) often precede any family recognition. CY-BOCS-tracking via school-counsellor partnerships catches the childhood cases that become adult disabilities.
- **The switch-withdrawal and dose-timeframe audit.** For the transfer patient carrying a decade of prescriptions: audit for the two classic errors, SSRI judged "failed" at sub-OCD doses, and clomipramine never tried after two SSRI failures; a large share of "treatment-resistant" Indian OCD is actually under-dosed and under-trialed.

## 11. Clinical cases (de-identified)

**Case 1: the bath before the bus.** A 23-year-old bank clerk in Madurai spent 2.5 hours bathing every morning in a fixed sequence, used separate utensils at home, and changed clothes after any contact with "outside" chairs; his family managed the water logistics and praised him as "particular about cleanliness". His parents brought him after he began taking leave twice a week (the bath plus the bus-avoidance made the 9 a.m. start impossible), framed to the clinic as "weakness and laziness". Assessment: contamination-dimension OCD with major family accommodation; Y-BOCS in the severe range. Management: the alarm-circuit reframe in Tamil; ERP hierarchy (touch the outside chair → sit on it → touch the "pure" vessel → the bus handrail, each with prevented washes, timed, logged); sertraline titrated to 200 mg over 8 weeks (10-week full trial honoured); family module retiring the separate-utensil economy on a written two-week schedule. At month 5: bath 25 minutes, bus-riding daily, leave-usage zero; the father's line at review: "we thought we were helping him all these years; we were helping the illness." Teaching points: (1) accommodation masquerading as family virtue; (2) the ERP hierarchy is local geography (the bus handrail, the "pure" vessel); (3) SSRI at full OCD dose and full OCD duration.

**Case 2: the mother who could not hold her baby.** A 29-year-old first-time mother in Pune presented at 4 months postpartum with "depression", found tearful, avoiding the baby, asking her mother to handle feeds. Only after the normalising screening question did she disclose the intrusions: images of harming the baby with the kitchen knife, and a thought that she "must secretly want it", neutralised by mental prayers and hiding all knives, and never told to anyone (she had begun planning to leave the household "before I do something"). Assessment: postpartum-onset OCD, taboo-aggressive dimension, with concealed content and depression secondary; no psychosis, full horror at the thoughts (ego-dystonicity documented verbatim). Management: the first session's reframe ("these are the mind's smoke-detector beeps, not your wishes; the proof is the horror you feel"); fluoxetine titrated to 40 mg; ERP (photo of the knife block, then holding the rounded knife during supervised sessions, with response prevention of the mental prayers); mother trained as the brief-reassurance partner; breastfeeding counselled with paediatric concurrence. At month 4: holding and bathing the baby alone, knives restored to the kitchen, one relapse-day after a fever week, managed with the written drill. Teaching points: (1) postpartam intrusive-harm OCD is COMMON, concealed, and mislabelled as depression or psychosis; the normalising question is a child-protection instrument; (2) the patient's horror IS the diagnostic sign distinguishing OCD from psychosis; (3) maternal filicide-fear presentations demand direct, calm expertise; the clinician's non-flinch keeps the case safe and in treatment.

## 13. FAQ: asked the way patients and families ask it

**"I have terrible thoughts, images I cannot even say aloud. Am I a dangerous person?"** No, and this is the most important thing this note will ever tell you: intrusive thoughts are universal brain noise. Nearly every parent has had a fleeting "what if I dropped the baby" image; nearly every religious person has had a blasphemous flash. What you have is not the thoughts but an alarm circuit that treats them as crimes. Your very horror at them is the proof: dangerous people do not agonise over these images; people with OCD do.

**"If the thoughts mean nothing, why do they feel so real and so MINE?"** Because the disorder's job is to make them feel meaningful; that is what thought-action fusion does: thinking it feels like doing it, and having it feels like wanting it. The feeling is the symptom, not the evidence. Treatment retrains exactly that mis-signal.

**"Is OCD about being very neat and organised?"** No: that is the popular misuse of the word. Plenty of people with OCD have no neatness theme at all, and plenty of meticulous people have no OCD. The disorder is UNWANTED intrusions and driven rituals that the person would pay to be rid of; the tidy perfectionist who ENJOYS their system has a personality style, not this illness.

**"Why can't I just stop washing? I try every day."** Because willpower is applied at the wrong joint; you are trying not to feel the alarm. The treatment moves the intervention to the ritual: the alarm may ring, and the hands stay still. That specific skill, practised repeatedly, is what re-sets the circuit; self-instruction to "just stop" fails the way telling a stutterer to "just speak" fails.

**"The family re-locks the doors for her. Are we helping?"** You are loving her, and feeding the disorder. Each re-lock teaches her brain that the danger was real and the ritual was necessary. We will help you step back kindly and on a schedule; she will call it cruelty for two weeks and freedom by the third month. Bring the whole household to the session.

**"Is this caused by spirits / our karma? The temple rituals gave some relief."** Temple rituals relieve because they ARE rituals, neutralising acts that briefly quiet the alarm, the same mechanism as washing. The relief's source is the brain, not the curse; that is why it fades and the demand grows. We can respect the faith and treat the circuit at the same time; families here do both successfully.

**"Will medicines be needed lifelong?"** Often for a long season, not always for life: a strong response is held 1–2 years, then tapered slowly with a relapse drill. If ERP is done well and early, many people hold recovery with skills rather than tablets. The worst route is stopping the medicine in month 3 because "the rituals reduced"; the reduction IS the medicine.

**"The doctor gave the same tablet my neighbour takes for depression, but a bigger dose. Why?"** OCD responds to the same medicines at HIGHER doses and SLOWER timelines than depression. It is not that you are depressed; it is that this disorder needs more of the serotonin effect to shift the alarm circuit. And we judge success only at 10–12 weeks at full dose; patience here is a clinical instruction, not a virtue.

**"Our son was fine till the throat infection, then suddenly all these rituals. Is that possible?"** Sudden childhood onset after infections is a recognised pattern (the immune-trigger territory historically called PANDAS; the concept is real, the label is debated). We treat the OCD the same way, promptly, while documenting the timeline; the specialist may consider infection workup alongside.

**"Can he marry? Should we tell the other family?"** People with treated OCD marry and parent like anyone else. Our advice parallels any treatable condition: stabilise first, disclose to at least one senior member of the other family, framed as "a treatable anxiety-circuit condition, like a thyroid problem of the alarm system". Concealment plus a relapse after the wedding is the far crueler path.

## 14. Exam & student lens

**Definitions to write exactly:** obsession = intrusive, unwanted thought/image/urge causing distress, recognized (usually) as one's own; compulsion = repetitive behaviour or mental act driven by rules, aimed at distress reduction, excessive/unrealistic. Ego-DYSTONIC vs OCPD's ego-SYNTONIC: the evergreen one-marker.

**Mnemonic for content dimensions, "CHST": Contamination, Harm/checking, Symmetry/"just-right", Taboo (sexual/religious/aggressive).**

**High-yield facts:**
- DSM-5 gave OCD its own chapter (out of anxiety disorders), with insight specifiers and tic-related modifier; ICD-11 mirrors the separation
- 80–90% of healthy people experience intrusive thoughts (the normality statistic)
- Salkovskis: inflated responsibility appraisal; thought-action fusion as mechanism
- Y-BOCS = the severity scale (named only)
- SSRI rules: HIGHER doses (sertraline 200, fluoxetine 80, fluvoxamine 300), LONGER trials (10–12 weeks), switch classes, then augment (risperidone/aripiprazole, best in tic-related)
- Clomipramine: the classic tricyclic evidence, ECG considerations
- Reassurance-seeking IS a compulsion; family accommodation maintains
- Treatment ladder end: deep TMS, DBS for refractory
- Postpartum onset recognised; sudden paediatric post-infectious onset = PANDAS-contested

**Classic traps:**
1. Calling OCD "severe anxiety" and missing the ritual architecture
2. Judging the SSRI failed at 8 weeks on half dose, the most common real-world error
3. Mistaking absent-insight OCD for schizophrenia (check: ritual structure, theme symmetry, course)
4. Diagnosing OCPD as OCD (enjoyed vs hated)
5. Missing mental neutralising: "washing stopped" while silent prayers continue
6. Missing taboo-content OCD behind "treatment-resistant depression"
7. In postpartum: misreading harm-intrusions as psychosis and separating mother and baby wrongly

**Indian exam corner:** " differentiate OCD from OCPD" (guaranteed somewhere), "OCD (phenomenology and management", "ERP) principles", "pharmacotherapy of OCD, doses and duration", " insight specifiers". Indian-context value-adds: purity-contamination fusion, scrupulosity, family accommodation as treatment target, NMHS data framing.

## 16. Self-test MCQs

**1. The feature that best distinguishes OCD from obsessive-compulsive personality disorder:**
A. Age of onset
B. Ego-dystonic intrusions and rituals vs ego-syntonic trait pattern
C. Response to SSRIs
D. Family history
**Answer: B.** OCD hates its symptoms; OCPD endorses and enjoys them.

**2. A mother avoids her baby after images of harming him. The sign favouring OCD over psychosis:**
A. The images are of harm
B. She is horrified by the thoughts and does not want them
C. She has insomnia
D. She is postpartum
**Answer: B.** Ego-dystonic horror is the OCD signature; psychotic filicide ideation is congruent with the delusion, not fought against.

**3. The correct SSRI approach in OCD compared with depression:**
A. Lower dose, shorter trial
B. Same dose, same duration
C. Higher dose, 10–12 week trials at maximum tolerated dose
D. Any dose, judged at 2 weeks
**Answer: C.** The dose-duration discipline, and the most-violated rule in practice.

**4. In ERP, the response to be prevented is:**
A. The exposure itself
B. The compulsive ritual (overt and mental) after the trigger
C. The anxiety entirely
D. The SSRI
**Answer: B.** Trigger present, ritual absent, the re-learning condition for the alarm circuit.

**5. Which augmentation has the best evidence after two failed SSRI/clomipramine trials, especially in tic-related OCD:**
A. Lamotrigine
B. Low-dose antipsychotic (risperidone/aripiprazole)
C. Lithium
D. Benzodiazepine
**Answer: B.** Antipsychotic augmentation, strongest signal in the tic-related subtype.

**6. A family re-locks doors and manages "clean" utensils for the patient. The treatment-relevant formulation:**
A. Family care, unrelated to the disorder
B. Family accommodation maintaining the disorder: a target for structured reduction
C. Evidence of family OCD
D. Grounds for admission
**Answer: B.** Accommodation is the disorder's oxygen; the reduction module is evidence-based family work.

**7. Intrusive thoughts in the general population occur in approximately:**
A. 2%
B. 15%
C. 80–90%
D. 0%
**Answer: C.** The universality statistic: the foundation of the normalising reframe.

**8. Y-BOCS is best described as:**
A. A diagnostic interview for psychosis
B. The standard OCD severity scale (named, items not reproduced here)
C. An SSRI dosing formula
D. A family-screening tool
**Answer: B.** The severity/tracking instrument of the field.
