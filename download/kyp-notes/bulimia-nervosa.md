# Bulimia Nervosa

## 1. In one line + overview

**Bulimia nervosa is a hidden cycle of secret binge-eating (eating a large amount with a feeling of losing control) followed by panicked compensatory acts (vomiting, laxatives, fasting, exercise) repeated weekly for months, usually in a person of normal weight who feels deeply ashamed and tells no one.**

Where anorexia is visible to the world, bulimia is designed to be invisible: the person often looks healthy, eats normally in company, and performs the binge-purge cycle alone, in bathrooms, in hostels, at 2 a.m. in the kitchen. The engine is a distinctive one-two: restrictive dieting between episodes ("I will be perfect today") that builds a food-debt, then a binge when hunger and stress overwhelm the rules, then panic about the binge's consequences (weight, guilt, exposure) and the compensatory purge, which relieves the panic for minutes and teaches the brain that purging is rescue, guaranteeing the next cycle. The body pays in quiet, dangerous currency: potassium loss from vomiting (the cardiac-risk electrolyte), tooth erosion from stomach acid, oesophagus strain, parotid swelling, and the metabolic chaos of laxative misuse. The shame layer makes bulimia one of psychiatry's most concealed disorders, patients average years before disclosure, often only unmasked by a dentist's finding, a low-potassium lab, or a confession at 3 a.m. to a sister. It is, importantly, one of the most treatable: structured CBT has a strong cure-and-improvement rate, and fluoxetine at specific higher dosing is the one well-established medicine.

## 2. Learning objectives

After this note you can:
- Define binge and compensatory behaviour precisely; apply the once-a-week/3-months DSM-5 gate
- Describe the restriction→binge→purge engine and why "dieting harder" deepens the cycle
- List the physical signs hunt: Russell's sign, dental erosion, parotids, hypokalaemia, alkalosis
- Run CBT-based treatment as the first-line: regular eating, binge-trigger mapping, purge-fading
- Use fluoxetine 60 mg dosing correctly and know the adjunct evidence
- Recognise Indian concealment patterns: hostel binges, "herbal" purgatives, gym-compensation, wedding-diet cycles
- Distinguish bulimia from binge-eating disorder, anorexia binge-purge subtype, and OSFED
- Screen the medical and psychiatric danger lines: hypokalaemia, oesophageal injury, self-harm, suicidality

## 4. Epidemiology

**Global.** Lifetime prevalence ~1–2% in young women, less in men (though under-detected there too); peak onset 16–25, slightly later than anorexia. Comorbidity rule: depression, anxiety, impulsive-spectrum behaviours (self-harm, substance misuse) in a meaningful share; the "multi-impulsive" subgroup that does worse. Mortality is lower than anorexia but real (suicide and medical/electrolyte events); chronicity without treatment is common. A majority of patients never reach specialist care; dentists and primary care hold the detection front line.

**India.** No national figures; clinical-series data suggest bulimia is more common than anorexia in Indian urban populations, and far less often diagnosed, because the normal weight plus the secrecy defeat the referral instinct. Indian pattern notes: binge content is usually carbohydrate-dense household foods (the rice-mithai-roasted-savoury complex) rather than the Western fast-food script; hostel and PG living creates the "alone with the canteen parcel" architecture; purging routes include self-induced vomiting AND the under-recognised "herbal"/Ayurvedic purgative and "detox tea" misuse marketed for weight loss; and wedding-diet cycles ("reduce 8 kg before the December alliance") repeatedly seed the restriction→binge engine in perfectly ordinary young women.

## 5. Causes & risk factors

**Biological.**
- Moderate heritability (~40–60% in twin studies); serotonin-system involvement (binge-purge states alter 5-HT function; SSRI response is the clinical echo)
- Puberty and weight-conscious biological events as onset anchors

**Psychological, the engine in four gears:**
1. **Restraint**: rigid food rules and fasting between episodes ("clean day"), the first gear, and the TREATABLE one
2. **Breach**: hunger + stress + emotion overwhelm the rules → the binge with its dissociated, out-of-control quality ("I watched my hands keep eating")
3. **Panic**: catastrophic appraisal of the binge ("now I am ruined/fat/disgusting")
4. **Compensation**: purging as rescue, which (a) partially undoes the calorie anxiety, (b) relieves the emotional flood, and (c) thereby becomes REINFORCED; the cycle is now self-perpetuating. Laxative truth to teach: they purge WATER and salts mostly, not calories; the "rescue" is almost entirely an illusion sold to the panic

**Social / cultural.**
- Thin-ideal internalisation; appearance-focused occupations and sports
- Weight-related teasing history; sexual-abuse history elevates risk in some studies (screen sensitively, don't assume)
- Indian context: the appearance economy (marriage market, social media), food-abundant festival culture colliding with thin-ideal messaging, and hostel/PG isolation, the perfect binge architecture: alone, stocked, stressed

## 6. What happens in the body/brain

**Story 1: the debt and the breach.** Rigid dieting is a financial austerity programme run by a body that has not agreed to it. Hunger is the accumulating debt; willpower is the interest. At some point (stress-day, loneliness, exhaustion) the debt is called: the binge. The person "fails" their diet not from weakness but from arithmetic; the binge's size and trance-like quality are what a starving-and-forbidden brain does when the dam breaks. The treatment's first move (regular eating, never going hungry) is not kindness; it is removing the arithmetic that powers the engine.

**Story 2: the panic wash.** After the binge, the fear and shame arrive like a flood; the purge is the drain. The relief is real, chemical, immediate, and that immediacy is the disorder's cement: the brain learns purging = salvation, exactly the way it learns any powerful relief. Within weeks the sequence is automatic: full stomach → bathroom. CBT's purge-fading replaces the drain with the truth that a single binge changes nothing on the scale that matters, and that the body's water-weight panic (and the scale's next-morning theatre) passes on its own within days.

**Story 3: the potassium thief.** Vomiting strips hydrogen chloride and potassium; chronic purging runs a body quietly low on K, the electrolyte of the heart's rhythm. Combined with metabolic alkalosis, this is bulimia's invisible cardiac risk, invisible until an ECG or a severe faint reveals it. The stomach acid also re-textures the teeth (the dentist's lingual-surface finding often precedes any psychiatric diagnosis by years) and strains the oesophagus; rare catastrophic events (tears, rupture) are the reason any vomited blood or severe chest pain is an emergency, not embarrassment.

## 7. Symptoms

**The binge (by definition):** eating, in a discrete period, an amount that is definitely larger than most people would eat under similar circumstances, PLUS the sense of loss of control during it (cannot stop, cannot choose). Classic qualities: rapid, solitary, preferentially forbidden/carbohydrate-dense foods, trance-like, followed by shame and concealment (wrappers hidden, evidence disposed).

**The compensatory behaviours:**
- Self-induced vomiting (the majority): with Russell's sign (knuckle calluses from induced gagging), dental erosion, parotid swelling, voice change
- Laxative misuse (the Indian "herbal purgative" and detox-tea market), water/salt loss, dependency constipation (the gut forgets its own rhythm), the no-calories-removed truth
- Diuretics, enemas; fasting the next day ("the clean day"); excessive compensatory exercise
- The dosing of the disorder: DSM-5 frequency gate = once a week for 3 months (moderate: 1–3/week; severe: 4–7; extreme: 14+/week)

**Between episodes:** normal-weight or slightly-above body (commonly); food rules and "good days"; body dissatisfaction concentrated on weight/shape; mood swings tracking the cycle; social-eating performance ("eats properly at the family table"); menstrual irregularities in some.

**The psychological weather:** shame (the disorder's constant companion), secrecy, self-disgust; depression and anxiety comorbid; self-harm and substance use in the impulsive subgroup; suicide risk to screen directly.

## 8. Diagnosis

**The logic (DSM-5, paraphrased):** recurrent binge episodes (large amount + loss of control); recurrent compensatory behaviour to prevent weight gain, both occurring on average once a week for three months; self-evaluation unduly influenced by body shape/weight; the disturbance does not occur exclusively during anorexia nervosa (if weight is significantly low, anorexia binge-purge subtype owns the diagnosis, the weight boundary between them).

**The interview craft:**
- The secrecy-defeating opener: "many people find that when they are alone and stressed, they eat a large amount quickly and feel out of control, does that ever happen to you?" (normalising, behaviour-specific, no weight talk)
- Ask the purge inventory by name (vomiting, "herbal/detox" products, laxatives, the next-day fasting, exercise hours) patients rarely volunteer the last three
- Map the cycle: the rules between, the triggers before (loneliness, conflict, evening, post-results), the location (hostel bathroom, home kitchen at 2 a.m.)
- Weigh and examine: the dental look, the parotids, the knuckles, the ECG when vomiting is frequent
- Screen: depression, self-harm, substance use; suicide directly

**Instruments by name:** the Eating Disorder Examination (EDE/EDE-Q questionnaire version) as the structured gold standard; BULIT-R as the screen; SCOFF shared with anorexia screening.

**Medical workup where indicated:** potassium, magnesium, chloride/bicarbonate (the alkalosis), ECG for frequent purgers (QTc), amylase (parotid marker, non-specific), dental review.

### Differential table

| Looks like | The distinguishing feature |
|---|---|
| Binge-eating disorder (DSM-5's own category) | Binges WITHOUT regular compensatory behaviour: weight usually higher, shame without the rescue acts |
| Anorexia nervosa, binge-purge subtype | Significantly low body weight: anorexia owns the diagnosis at low BMI whatever the purging |
| OSFED/other specified presentations | Sub-threshold frequency/duration; purging disorder (purging after normal-size meals without binges) |
| Recreational overeating/festival eating | Social, planned, no loss-of-control signature, no compensatory panic |
| GERD/bulimia confusion (dental route) | Acid patterns differ; the enquiry about control and compensation clarifies |
| Kleine-Levin/bulimia of neurological origin | Rare; hypersomnia and cognitive change context |
| Substance-use appetite cycles | Locked to use states |

## 9. Management

**The architecture: break the engine at the RESTRAINT gear first.**

**Step 1: the unloading and the safety pass.** The disclosure consultation is itself treatment (the first confession of the secret). Then the medical safety review: potassium/ECG if frequent vomiting; the emergency teachings: vomited blood, severe chest pain, fainting = same-day medical care, not midnight shame.

**Step 2: CBT, the first-line with the strongest evidence (16–20 sessions):**
1. **Regular eating**: three meals plus planned snacks, timed, never skipped; hunger is the binge's invitation; this single prescription cuts binge frequency meaningfully in the first month
2. **Weigh-ins on the clinician's schedule** (weekly, in session), NOT the patient's home scale (which feeds the theatre)
3. **Self-monitoring diary**: time, place, what, triggers, thoughts, behaviour; the map from which every later intervention is drawn
4. **Trigger analysis and problem-solving**: loneliness, conflict, evening structure, exhaustion; the binge's schedule is treated as data
5. **Purge-fading ladder**: delay the purge by 5 → 15 → 30 minutes after meals with support (the anxiety peaks and falls without the rescue, the exposure logic applied to purging); then reduce frequency rungs; replace the bathroom-adjacent hour with a planned activity
6. **Cognitive work on the over-evaluation**: the weight-shape self-worth equation and the "one binge ruins everything" catastrophe; banishing the scale and the body-checking rituals
7. **Relapse-prevention module**: the cycle's personal early warnings (skipping meals returning, evening kitchen-pacing, scale-peeping) with the written drill
- Guided self-help (the CBT workbooks, therapist-supported) is a legitimate first step for milder cases, and the realistic Indian district tier

**Step 3, pharmacotherapy:**
- **Fluoxetine 60 mg/day**: the one medicine with specific bulimia evidence and approval (note the dose: 60, not 20; the dose-response is the exam point); reduces binge/purge frequency as monotherapy and augments CBT; watch activation early
- Other SSRIs if fluoxetine fails (less direct evidence); ondansetron and topiramate exist in the research tier (topiramate's side and teratogenic profile keep it a specialist instrument)
- **No medicine is the treatment**; CBT is; medication is the adjunct or the bridge where therapy is unavailable

**Step 4: the comorbidities and the long arc.** Depression treated (improving with the cycle's break, often); self-harm and substance work in the impulsive subgroup; family sessions converting the household from surveillance-and-shame to meal-structure-and-support. The course is good: with treatment, most improve substantially and many remit fully; without treatment, chronic oscillation for years.

*India note (approx 2026): fluoxetine 60 mg ≈ ₹80–180/month; CBT-based treatment private ≈ ₹600–1,500/session, 16–20 sessions; guided self-help via district psychologists/college counsellors and Tele-MANAS (14416) at nominal cost. The Indian-specific clinical warnings: (1) the "herbal/detox" purgative market; ask about it by name in every suspected case, patients do not count it as "laxatives"; (2) potassium/ECG access is excellent even at district level (₹100–200/ECG); use it for every frequent vomiter; (3) hostel/PG architecture: treatment plans must build the alone-with-food structure (the tiffin-service meal plan, the roommate alliance, the evening activity block) rather than assume a family kitchen; (4) dentists are the front line; the lingual-erosion referral conversation is worth seeding in dental continuing-education locally.*

## 10. Indian practice

- **The secrecy stack.** Indian bulimia hides three layers deep: the behaviour is secret; the normal weight deflects suspicion; and the cultural read ("she eats so little at meals; such control") actively covers it (the family-table performance IS the symptom's camouflage). The detection questions belong in gynaecology (menstrual irregularity), dermatology/dentistry (erosion), and general practice (unexplained fainting, electrolyte noise), the "alone and out-of-control eating" question, asked in the normalised phrasing, is the two-line detector.
- **The wedding-diet cycle.** The pre-alliance crash diet is the seedbed: 6–8 weeks of restriction, the binge on the celebratory feasts that follow, the panic-purge, and the engine is built. Counsel families against crash programmes (see Anorexia note); for the already-cycling bride-to-be, the treatment entry is the regular-eating prescription, framed harmlessly as "the dietician's stabilisation plan".
- **"Detox" as the Indian purgative door.** The wellness industry sells purging as cleansing: the herbal tea, the virechana-retreat misuse, the salt-water flush. Patients self-report "I just do detox, doctor"; count it as compensatory behaviour in the inventory, and address it as part of the cycle, not as a separate health habit.
- **The hostel kitchen at 2 a.m.** Structure prescriptions must be local: the meal-plan subscription replacing the fridge-raiding; the canteen-parcel timing; the roommate confided-in as the meal-support ally; the evening activity block (gym-as-exercise-only-if-not-compensatory, dance, library hours) occupying the binge's prime hours. Plans written for family kitchens fail in hostels; write for the architecture the patient lives in.
- **Family sessions.** The Indian family's discovery-response is typically either surveillance (checking the bathroom, counting mithai) or shame-silence, both feed the engine. The module converts the household to: regular family meals (the best anti-binge architecture), no scale at home, no weight commentary (the "you have reduced!" compliment is a trigger in both directions), and one ally-parent, not a police-parent.
- **The disclosure and marriage problem.** As with other disorders (see the Bipolar note's framework): stabilise, then disclose to at least one senior member of the prospective family, framed as a treatable eating-anxiety cycle. The concealment-into-marriage route imports the cycle into the new household's surveillance economy, usually discovered at crisis.

## 11. Clinical cases (de-identified)

**Case 1: the dentist found it first.** A 24-year-old interior designer in Kochi was referred by her dentist after a cleaning revealed lingual-surface enamel loss across the upper teeth ("do you have reflux?"). In the normalised interview she disclosed a 4-year cycle: strict 1,200-kcal "clean days", evening binges on leftovers alone (past midnight, 2,000+ kcal), self-induced vomiting 3–4 nights/week, and a bathroom ritual timed after her parents slept. Weight normal; menses irregular; potassium 3.2 mmol/L, chloride low, bicarbonate high, the classic alkalotic signature. Management: the unloading session ("the first time I have said any of this aloud"); CBT over 18 sessions: regular-eating prescription first (binges fell from 4 to 1/week by week 6 on this alone), diary-based trigger work (the 11 p.m. kitchen-pacing identified as the launch-pad, re-structured), purge-delay ladder with the 5-15-30 minute rungs, fluoxetine titrated to 60 mg at week 4 for the residual evening urges, family module (mother as meal-ally, the home scale ceremonially donated). At 6 months: one mild lapse month (placement season), managed with the drill; potassium normal; dental maintenance with fluoride varnish. Teaching points: (1) the dentist as first detector, seed that referral channel; (2) regular eating alone cuts binge frequency dramatically (the restraint-gear principle); (3) the specific fluoxetine dose point; (4) the family's surveillance instincts had to be converted, not suppressed.

**Case 2: the hosteller and the detox teas.** A 19-year-old engineering hosteller in Manipal, slightly above "target weight" by her own arithmetic, was brought after her roommate found the stash: parcels, wrappers, and three brands of "slimming detox tea". The cycle: restrictive canteen days ("just a dosa"), Thursday-night binges on delivery apps ordered for one ("the app knows my address by heart now"), the teas as the panic-purge, plus two hours of gym the morning after. She had never induced vomiting; the "detox" route was her entire compensation layer. Management: the inventory conversation that named the teas as purgatives ("it removes water and fear, not calories; the fat arithmetic you fear is untouched; what it removes is potassium"); CBT structure adapted to hostel architecture (three-meal tiffin subscription to eliminate the hunger-debt, the roommate as meal-ally after a mediated disclosure, Thursday-evening dance class occupying the launch window, gym prescribed on a written 45-minute schedule decoupled from mornings-after); no medication needed beyond the first month's fluoxetine 40 mg bridge. At 4 months: binges rare, teas abandoned ("I read the electrolyte leaflet you gave, that scared me straight"), weight stable and deprioritised. Teaching points: (1) the Indian detox-tea compensatory channel is invisible unless asked by name; (2) laxative truth-telling is itself an intervention (the rescue is an illusion); (3) hostel-architecture prescriptions beat family-kitchen templates; (4) the roommate alliance is the hosteller's family-equivalent.

## 13. FAQ: asked the way patients and families ask it

**"She is a normal weight. How can she have an eating disorder?"** Eating disorders are defined by the mind's relationship with food and the body, not by thinness. Bulimia's cycle (the secret binges, the purging, the shame) runs mostly in normal-weight people. The weight looks fine; the potassium, the teeth, and the misery do not.

**"She eats so sensibly at the table. Are you sure about the binges?"** The family-table performance is part of the disorder's camouflage. The binges happen alone, usually at night, usually on whatever is stocked. The person you see at dinner and the person in the 2 a.m. kitchen are both real; the illness requires both performances.

**"Is this just a diet that went too far?"** It began as one, but the engine has its own gears now: restriction builds hunger-debt, the debt breaks into a binge, panic triggers the purge, and the purge's relief books the next cycle. Diets end; this cycle does not, without specific treatment.

**"The doctor says vomiting is ruining her potassium. What does that mean?"** Potassium is the heart's rhythm-keeper; purging washes it out quietly. Low-K plus the body's acid-base shift is the reason we check bloods and ECGs in frequent purgers; the risk is invisible until it is not. That is also why vomited blood, chest pain, or fainting means the emergency department the same day, not the bathroom again.

**"Are these 'detox teas' actually harmful? They are herbal."** Herbal does not mean harmless: they purge water and salts (including the potassium the heart needs), and they do not remove the calories from the binge. The rescue is an illusion sold to your panic. We will show you the numbers plainly; the cycle is what needs the treatment.

**"Will she need medicines?"** One medicine has specific evidence here, fluoxetine, at a higher dose than the usual (60 mg), which reduces the binge-purge frequency. But the treatment with the real cure-rates is the structured talking therapy: 16–20 sessions that dismantle the engine at its gears. Medicine is the support act, not the show.

**"We checked the bathroom; we count the sweets. Is that helping?"** It is surveillance, and surveillance feeds the shame that powers the cycle. What helps is the opposite architecture: regular family meals, no scale in the house, no weight commentary, and one parent as the ally she can call at midnight instead of the bathroom.

**"Can she stay in the hostel?"** Usually yes, with a plan written for hostel life: scheduled meals she actually eats (not the one-dosa day), one roommate who knows, the evening hours deliberately occupied, the delivery-app friction raised. Removing her from studies punishes; structuring the hostel treats.

**"Will this come back?"** It can, in the known windows, exam seasons, placements, pre-wedding dieting, breakups. That is why the therapy ends with a written drill: skipped meals returning, evening pacing, the scale creeping back. Most people who relapse once, catch it with the drill and stay recovered.

**"Is she at risk of the anorexia-type dangers?"** Lower than anorexia's, but real: the heart-rhythm risk from purging, oesophageal injury, and the depression and self-harm that travel with the shame. This is why we take a "normal-weight" cycle as seriously as we do thinness.

## 14. Exam & student lens

**Gates to memorise:** binge (large amount + loss of control) + compensatory behaviour, once weekly × 3 months; NOT exclusively during anorexia (the weight boundary). Severity bands: mild 1–3/week; moderate 4–7; severe 8–13; extreme 14+.

**Physical signs mnemonic, "R-D-P-K-A":** Russell's sign, Dental erosion, Parotid enlargement, HypoKalaemia, metabolic Alkalosis.

**High-yield facts:**
- DSM-5 relaxed the frequency gate from twice-weekly (DSM-IV) to once-weekly, a favourite "what changed" question
- Fluoxetine 60 mg = the dose-specific evidence/approved medicine
- CBT (16–20 sessions) = first-line with the strongest evidence; guided self-help as the low-intensity tier
- Laxatives purge water/salts, not calories: teach it in the exam AND the ward
- Regular eating as the first intervention (the restraint-gear cut)
- Binge-eating disorder = binges WITHOUT compensation; purging disorder = purging without binges (OSFED)
- Mortality lower than anorexia but real; impulsive-subgroup comorbidity (self-harm, substances)
- The dentist as first-line detector (lingual erosion)

**Classic traps:**
1. Missing the diagnosis in a normal-weight patient with "disciplined" table-eating
2. Not asking about laxatives/"detox" products by name
3. Prescribing fluoxetine 20 mg and calling the evidence done; the dose IS the point
4. Treating the binge with stricter dieting advice, pouring fuel on the restraint gear
5. Confusing bulimia with anorexia binge-purge subtype at low BMI (anorexia owns the diagnosis)
6. Missing self-harm/suicide screening in the impulsive subgroup

**Indian exam corner:** "bulimia nervosa, criteria, complications, management", " differentiate anorexia from bulimia" (guaranteed), "fluoxetine in bulimia, the dose", "hypokalaemia and alkalosis in self-induced vomiting". Indian-context value-adds: the detox-tea channel, hostel architecture, the wedding-diet cycle, dentist-detection.

## 16. Self-test MCQs

**1. The DSM-5 frequency gate for bulimia nervosa:**
A. Binge-purge once a month for 3 months
B. Once a week for 3 months
C. Daily for 6 months
D. Twice a week for 2 months
**Answer: B.** Relaxed from DSM-IV's twice-weekly: the "what changed" fact.

**2. The laboratory signature of habitual self-induced vomiting:**
A. Hyperkalaemia with acidosis
B. Hypokalaemia with metabolic alkalosis
C. Hypernatraemia with alkalosis
D. Hypercalcaemia
**Answer: B.** Chloride and potassium lost with acid, the classic pattern, with the cardiac-rhythm risk attached.

**3. The single medicine with specific, dose-relevant evidence in bulimia:**
A. Fluoxetine 60 mg/day
B. Sertraline 50 mg
C. Olanzapine 10 mg
D. Lithium
**Answer: A.** The dose-response is the exam point, 60 mg, not the usual antidepressant dosing.

**4. The first gear of the cycle that treatment attacks, and why:**
A. Purging, because it is dangerous
B. Restriction, because hunger-debt powers the binge
C. Weight, because it is the goal
D. Family, because they cause it
**Answer: B.** Regular eating removes the arithmetic that drives the breach; binges fall before any "willpower" work.

**5. Laxative misuse as a compensatory behaviour:**
A. Removes most binge calories
B. Removes mainly water and electrolytes, not calories
C. Is harmless
D. Rebuilds potassium
**Answer: B.** The rescue is almost entirely illusion, a two-sentence patient teaching and a standard exam line.

**6. A patient of BMI 21 binges and purges four times weekly. A patient of BMI 15.5 binges and purges four times weekly. Diagnoses:**
A. Both bulimia nervosa
B. Bulimia nervosa; anorexia nervosa binge-purge subtype
C. Both anorexia
D. Binge-eating disorder; bulimia
**Answer: B.** Weight owns the boundary: significantly low weight hands the diagnosis to anorexia whatever the purging.

**7. The most common Indian concealment error in detection:**
A. Patients over-report binges
B. Normal weight + family-table performance + "detox" products not counted as purging
C. Dentists over-diagnose
D. Families under-feed
**Answer: B.** The three-layer camouflage: look well, eat well in public, purge via the wellness aisle.

**8. In CBT for bulimia, home weighing is:**
A. Encouraged daily for accountability
B. Stopped; weighing happens weekly in-session only
C. Done hourly
D. Irrelevant
**Answer: B.** The home scale feeds the panic theatre; scheduled in-session weighing removes the trigger while the work proceeds.
