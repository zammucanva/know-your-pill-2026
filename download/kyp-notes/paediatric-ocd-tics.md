# OCD & Tic Disorders in Children and Adolescents

## 1. In one line + overview

**OCD in the young is a sticky-thought machine (contamination fears, doubting, magical rituals and taboo intrusions that the child KNOWS are silly but cannot drop) treated with the same exposure-and-response-prevention logic as adults but with parents as co-therapists (they must stop feeding the rituals), while tic disorders (including Tourette's) are the body's urge-tension-release loops (waxing and waning, suppressible, largely self-fading) treated with habit-reversal training first and medicines only when the tic costs.**

Two conditions travel together often enough to share a note: childhood OCD (sharing its engine with the adult disorder; see the OCD note for the full cognitive model and drug dosing) and the tic disorders. The child differences matter: young OCD patients have less insight (the rituals can feel utterly sensible), their family is frequently woven INTO the illness (parents driven to provide reassurance, wash clothes, check homework, the accommodation that feeds the machine), and the themes wear child costumes; magical contagion ("if I see red cars someone dies"), exactness, asking-forever. Tics arrive instead through the body: eye-blinks, sniffs, throat-clears, grunts, head-jerks, transient in a large share of ordinary children (a brief tic era passes in months), persistent in a minority; Tourette's when multiple motor tics and vocal tics have ridden a year. The twin signature most people never learn: tics are PRECEDED by a building urge and relieved by doing them; they are not "habits" to be punished and not "naughtiness"; they fade with calm, spike with stress and excitement, mostly improve by late teens. Meanwhile, the combined OCD+tic child is common (and the treatment program addresses both). Both conditions are genuinely treatable without lifelong medication: exposure-and-response-prevention (child edition) and habit-reversal training are among the most effective therapies in child mental health.

## 2. Learning objectives

After this note you can:
- Recognise childhood OCD through its child-themes and the "silly but stuck" quality, with the reduced-insight qualifier
- Run the family-accommodation audit and coach parents out of the reassurance-ritual loop, the make-or-break of paediatric OCD treatment
- Deliver child-adapted ERP: externalisation, ladders, the reward economy, 8–14 session packages (the POTS-tier evidence)
- Use sertraline/fluoxetine correctly in paediatric OCD (the higher-slower dose logic carried from the adult note) and know when combined treatment beats either
- State the honest position on PANDAS/PANS; what is real, what is contested, what to actually do with a sudden-onset case
- Diagnose across the tic spectrum: transient, chronic motor/vocal, Tourette's (the 1-year gate, multiple motor + vocal), and recognise the premonitory-urge-suppression cycle
- Treat tics in the evidence order: psychoeducation + watchful waiting → CBIT/habit-reversal → medication (alpha-2 agonists first-line, dopamine blockers with weight-and-monitoring caution)
- Handle Indian realities: the punished tic ("stop making that noise"), the OCD-read-as-religious-problem family, the scarce CBIT professional, and the teacher-briefing that changes school life

## 4. Epidemiology

**Global.** Paediatric OCD: roughly 0.5–2% of children/adolescents (about half of all OCD begins in childhood/adolescence; the mean adult-onset story is wrong; paediatric-onset is a large share of the lifetime picture). Boys predominate in early-onset (pre-pubertal), often with tics in tow; the sex ratio evens by adolescence. Insight is poorer in children (the DSM specifier runs from good to absent, and "absent/delusional" is more common in young ones). Tics: transient tics are almost ordinary; up to a quarter of children show a brief tic era at some point; persistent tic disorders ~1–2%; Tourette's itself roughly 0.3–1%, boys 3–4×. Tic peak severity sits at 10–12 years with substantial improvement by late teens in a majority (roughly a third resolve fully, most of the remainder meaningfully improve). The comorbidity lattice of Tourette's: ADHD in over half, OCD in a third to a half, anxiety, and rage-attacks as a common secondary layer.

**India.** No dedicated national tic/OCD child survey; clinic experience maps the global patterns with the Indian costume: tics brought as "this sound he makes; we have told him a hundred times" (the punishment presentation), OCD arriving through temples (the family has already spent years interpreting the washing as shuddhi-gone-wrong) and through school (the erasing-until-paper-tears presentation; the teacher sends for "careless handwriting"), and paediatric OPD reflux from the throat-clear's ENT carousel. CBIT-trained professionals are scarce; paediatric OCDERP is delivered mostly in metro child-guidance clinics; parents default to either punishing rituals (as stubbornness) or performing them (as obedience); the clinical task is to convert both into the co-therapist role.

## 5. Causes & risk factors

**Genetic / biological**
- OCD: heritability ~40–50% in paediatric-onset (higher than adult-onset); tic-OCD linkage (the same cortico-basal ganglia circuit family; paediatric OCD with tics shows the stronger familial loading)
- Tourette's: strongly familial (the tic-diathesis inherited broadly, one family's "nervous coughs" across three generations)
- PANDAS/PANS layer: abrupt dramatic symptom explosion (OCD + tics + urinary frequency/eating change + regression) temporally linked to streptococcal infection, the basal-ganglia autoimmune hypothesis; genuinely observed in clinics, genuinely contested as a distinct entity (the honest position in section 9)

**Neurodevelopmental**
- The cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical loops: the habit-learning circuitry's over-tight error signals (OCD's "not-just-right" feeling) and the tic circuit's urge-loop both live in this neighbourhood, the reason the two co-travel

**Psychological / family**
- The accommodation engine (the child-specific MAINTAINER): parents' reassurance, ritual-assistance and trigger-removal feel like love, work like oxygen on the fire
- Anxiety temperament (behavioural inhibition) as the fertile soil; attachment to rules and exactness (the "just right" temperament profile)
- Anxious-modelling: the family's own checking/washing culture amplified in a susceptible child

**Indian context**
- Religious-family amplifier: rituals interpreted through the spiritual frame, sometimes protective (acceptance), sometimes catastrophic (exorcism route, the faith-healer circuit)
- Punitive interpretation of tics and rituals as defiance; the "stop it" reflex at home and school
- High-academic exactness culture: the erasing-until-perfect child praised for the first two years ("so meticulous!") then sent for the third (the 3-hour homework)
- Gender: adolescent girls' scrupulosity/purity-contamination presentations ride the local modesty-purity matrix; handle with cultural care, treat the OCD engine

## 6. What happens in the brain

**Story 1: the sticky-thought engine (child translation).** The adult OCD note's model in child terms: thoughts arrive to everyone; a child's mind is actually a PROLIFIC thought-generator (magical, catastrophic, wild). The OCD engine is not the generation but the STICKINESS: one class of thoughts gets tagged "unacceptable-dangerous-must-fix", and the fix (wash, check, ask, count, pray) removes the discomfort for a minute, which teaches the brain the fix worked, which glues the thought firmer. The child's insight is thinner ("of course I must wash; otherwise I'll get sick"), and the parents become the fix's outsource department ("Amma, tell me again it's clean"); each parental answer is one more training repetition. The treatment logic follows exactly: cut the reassurance, face the feared-not-washed, let the discomfort fall on its own; the un-fixing is the learning.

**Story 2: the urge-loop (the tic engine).** A tic is not a random movement: it is the RELEASE of a building internal itch, the premonitory urge (a tickle in the throat, tension at the neck, pressure behind the eyes). Doing the tic scratches it: seconds of relief, then the rebuild. The loop is exquisitely suppressible (the child can hold it, at assembly, at the exam) but suppression COSTS attention and builds tension; the after-school tic-storm that mystifies parents ("he was fine all day at school!") is the held-back tide releasing. It waxes and wanes over weeks (the stressful-term explosion, the holiday calm), migrates (old tics retire, new ones debut), and, the prognosis's engine, the loop itself tends to loosen with maturation through the teens.

**Story 3: the circuit's double-booking.** OCD's "not-just-right" signal and the tic's urge-loop run through overlapping habit circuitry (cortico-basal ganglia loops), the reason tics and rituals travel in families and in the same child. The practical translation: the child with tics PLUS contamination rituals PLUS a hyperactivity history is one integrated neurodevelopmental picture, not three diagnoses to be stunned by, and the treatment program is braided, not sequential.

**Story 4: the strep question (the PANDAS window).** The honest version: a small subset of children show explosive onset (overnight OCD, tics, urinary frequency, eating changes, separation panic, academic regression) in the weeks after streptococcal infection, with a relapsing-remitting course tied (in research settings) to new infections; the hypothesis is immune-mediated basal-ganglia inflammation (sibling of Sydenham's chorea's mechanism). What is contested: whether it is a distinct syndrome (guidelines differ); what is NOT contested: such presentations are real, merit evaluation (streptococcal testing, thorough differential), standard OCD/tic treatment, and avoidance of both extremes, dismissing the family's observation, or launching unproven immune therapies (the prophylactic-antibiotic and plasmapheresis/IVIG evidence is limited-to-discouraging outside research contexts).

## 7. Symptoms

**Childhood OCD, the child costumes:**
- **Contamination/washing** (the commonest child theme): fear of "dirt", germs, sticky things, public toilets, specific colours or numbers designated "dirty"; elaborate hand-washing, avoiding doorknobs, school-toilet refusal; skin raw from washing
- **Magical/superstitious thinking** rituals: stepping patterns, lucky actions, "must touch the door twice or something bad happens to Papa", the thought-action fusion era of the illness
- **Checking/doubting**: homework checking-forever, schoolbag re-packing loops, the erasing-until-perfect handwriting (the paper tears), asking-for-reassurance on loop ("is the gas off? is the door locked? will you die?")
- **Exactness/symmetry/order**: shoes aligned, pencils by size, distress at crooked or mixed things, the "just right" family
- **Religious/moral themes (scrupulosity)**: prayer loops, confession repetition, purity/washing fears; the Indian presentation that gets mis-routed to the religious frame
- **Taboo intrusions** (the adolescent secret): violent/sexual intrusive images, feared homosexuality/identity intrusions, the most shameful, most hidden, most asking-worthy layer
- **The functional cost**: 3-hour homework, school refusal from toilet-fear, raw hands, family hostage to the ritual rules, the child crying "I know it's silly but I can't stop"

**Family accommodation (the hidden symptom set):** parents providing reassurance hourly, washing the child's "contaminated" items, buying the special soap, answering the same question 40 times, restructuring the house around trigger-avoidance, accommodation in the severe majority, and the treatment's first-order target.

**Tic disorders, the repertoire:**
- **Motor tics**: eye-blinking, facial grimace, nose-wrinkle, head-jerk/jerk-shoulder, sniff, throat-clear, grunt, bark, word-fragments; complex ones: jumping, touching, twirling, echopraxia
- **Vocal tics**: sounds (sniff, hiss, click, animal noises), words; coprolalia (swear words), the cinematic stereotype, actually occurs in a small minority (roughly 10–15%); state it, kill the myth
- **The premonitory urge**: the child can usually localise it ("here, in my throat, before it comes"), the diagnostic gold and the treatment's lever
- **The rhythm of the condition**: waxing/waning over weeks, worse with stress/excitement/fatigue/illness, better in absorbed activity and sleep, after-school release storms, migration of tics over time
- **Spectrum gates**: transient (tics <1 year, the ordinary-child era), persistent motor OR vocal (1+ year), Tourette's (multiple motor + at least one vocal, 1+ year, typical onset 4–6)
- **The comorbids that matter**: ADHD (>50% of Tourette's, the school-failure engine), OCD (a third to a half, the rituals layer), anxiety, the rage-attacks/sensory-modulation layer (the explosive-frustration presentation that parents fear is "personality")

## 8. Diagnosis

**The logic (DSM-5/ICD-11 paraphrased):** OCD, obsessions (intrusive, unwanted, generative of distress) and/or compulsions (repetitive rule-driven acts the child feels driven to perform), time-consuming (>1 hour/day is the guideline benchmark) or functionally impairing; DSM-5 adds the insight specifier (good/fair/poor/absent, frequently poorer in young children) and drops the earlier "recognises irrational" requirement (a child-friendly correction). Tic disorders: the duration gates (transient <1 year; persistent/Tourette's 1+ year) and Tourette's combination (multiple motor + 1+ vocal, not necessarily concurrent); onset typically 4–6 years, prepubertal; the waxing-wane pattern is diagnostic texture, not a criterion.

**The assessment structure:**
1. **The secret-themes interview**: alone with the child, named normalised first ("everybody's brain makes weird pictures; mine does too; tell me about yours"): taboo intrusions surface ONLY under permission
2. **The ritual inventory + severity mapping**: which compulsions, how long daily, what triggers, what the family does for each (the accommodation grid)
3. **The tic examination**: observe (waiting-room video from the parent's phone is gold), elicit the premonitory urge ("show me where you feel it before the sniff comes"), map waxing-waning history, the after-school pattern
4. **The comorbidity sweep**: ADHD (the school picture), anxiety, depression, the rage-attack layer, learning disorders (the erasing child gets a writing-speed check)
5. **The strep question (PANDAS/PANS audit)** where onset was explosive: symptom timeline vs infection timeline, urinary/eating/regression flags, throat culture/ASO titre where acute; with the honest framing of what the tests can and cannot settle
6. **The mimics screen**: rheumatic/Sydenham's chorea in the sudden movement-disorder child (the paediatric referral's differential, India's RF-positive reality: cardiac examination, ESR/ASO, the paediatric cardiologist linkage), substance (the adolescent), medication-induced movement (stimulants/antipsychotics unwisely started), myoclonus and other neurological signs (the neurological exam, tics are suggestible, suppressible, preceded by urge; none of the mimics are all three)
7. **Rating scales (named only)**: CY-BOCS for OCD severity; YGTSS for tics: the tracking pair

### Differential table

| Looks like paediatric OCD/tics | The distinguishing feature |
|---|---|
| Normal childhood magical rituals (games, bedtime scripts) | Enjoyed, flexible, not distressing, doesn't balloon: disorder = suffering + time + spread |
| Anxiety's reassurance-seeking (separation/GAD) | The worry content is feared events, not felt-wrongness; no ritual grammar; the alarm's engine (see Child Anxiety note) |
| Autism's rigid routines and sensory behaviours | Social-reciprocity history, sensory profile, the routine is PREFERRED not feared-wrong; insight layer differs |
| Transient ordinary tics | Duration <1 year: a quarter of children pass through a tic era; reassurance and watchful waiting suffice |
| Sydenham's chorea | Post-streptococcal, flowing unpredictable movements WITHOUT premonitory urge/suppressibility; cardiac examination mandatory (the Indian reality check) |
| Myoclonus/seizure-adjacent movement | No urge, no suppressibility, no suggestibility; EEG/neurology referral |
| Medication-induced movement (stimulant/antipsychotic era) | The prescription timeline precedes; re-emerges on re-challenge |
| Skin-picking/hair-pulling (body-focused behaviours) | Body-focused repetitive behaviours: related family, their own handling |
| ADHD fidgets | Restlessness without the urge-release loop or the waxing architecture |
| Psychotic rituals (schizophrenia-spectrum) | Fixed delusional logic, no "silly" admittance, other psychotic phenomena; rare in children, but the insight-absent OCD can mimic; time and treatment response clarify |

## 9. Management

**OCD in the young, the evidence ladder:**

**1: Psychoeducation + family-accommodation reduction (the step-zero that doubles all downstream effect):** name the OCD ("the Worry Witch", "Mr Bossy", the child externalises and fights a character), explain the sticky-loop with the child's own example, and retrain the parents: reassurance weaned (the scripted answer: "that's the OCD asking, I won't answer it, but I'll sit with you while it yells"), ritual-assistance withdrawn gradually and kindly, triggers not engineered away. The CBT evidence itself shows accommodation reduction is a predictor of treatment success.

**2: ERP, child edition (the first-line for all severities):** the ladder built with the child (contamination: touch the doorknob with one finger, hold, no wash, till the wave falls → the full palm → the 30-minute no-wash rule); ritual-blocking as the ladder's partner (the "homework with no erasing" contract); the reward economy for exposure miles (brave-points charts, the child-version of exposure's motivational engine); 8–14 sessions with parent modules; the POTS-tier evidence: CBT alone suffices for milder cases and rivals medication; the combined CBT+sertraline for moderate-severe outruns either alone. The Indian delivery adaptation: metro child-guidance clinics carry therapist-led ERP; the manualised parent-led variants (the evidence-dose honestly thinner but real) plus fortnightly professional supervision are the district-town compromise.

**3, SSRIs (the moderate-severe tier):** sertraline (the POTS member) and fluoxetine carry the paediatric OCD evidence; the adult note's dosing logic transfers: OCD needs HIGHER-slower titration than depression (often 6–10 weeks to effect; the doses run higher), fluvoxamine as an alternative; start low, review weekly for activation, expect the slow clock and warn the family about it (the week-3 abandonment is the Indian pattern); duration post-response 6–12 months with ERP throughout; clomipramine as the experienced second-line (ECG, the anticholinergic load), full pharmacology in the OCD note.

**4, The PANDAS/PANS honest position:** sudden-onset cases get the strep evaluation, the differential closed (Sydenham's excluded with the cardiac examination), and STANDARD OCD/tic treatment begun immediately, not delayed by the immune debate; tonsillectomy/antibiotic-prophylaxis/immune-therapies: research-tier decisions, not clinic routines (guidelines caution against them outside protocols); the family's strep-observation validated without the autoimmune-treatment rabbit hole.

**Tics, the evidence ladder:**

**1: Psychoeducation + watchful waiting (the genuine first-line for most):** the myth-killing package (not naughtiness, not caused by parenting, coprolalia is a minority phenomenon, most improve by late teens), the school-briefing letter (ignore-not-scold the tic, no "stop it" demands, suppression demands cost classroom attention), the stress-architecture audit (the waxing-wane map), and scheduled review. For mild tics this IS the treatment and it completes.

**2: CBIT / habit-reversal training (the active-therapy tier):** awareness training (the child learns to catch the premonitory urge's exact signature, the lever), competing-response training (a 1-minute incompatible action deployed at the urge: slow mouth-breathing against the sniff; eyes-open-soft against the blink), relaxation and functional analysis of high-risk settings; 8–10 sessions with parent-and-teacher modules; evidence strong, adults' and children's alike. The Indian scarcity problem: CBIT-trained therapists are rare; the teachable core (awareness + competing response) can be taught to parent-teacher triads in clinic with review, imperfectly but honestly, and still outperforms scolding and sedation.

**3: Medication (when the tic costs, or comorbidity stacks):**
- Alpha-2 agonists (clonidine, guanfacine, availability varies in India): the first-line choice ESPECIALLY when ADHD co-travels (a double-duty effect); the sedation/hypotension titration cautions; weeks-to-effect
- Dopamine blockers (risperidone, aripiprazole, haloperidol, pimozide-the-classic): the most effective tic-suppressants, reserved for severe, function-destroying or socially-burnishing tics, lowest-dose, time-limited with review, because the side-effect ledger in growing children is real (weight, sedation, prolactin, drug-induced movement risk, the paradox of treating a movement disorder with a movement-causing drug class)
- Botulinum toxin for single severe vocal/focal tics; DBS: adult-severe-refractory territory, essentially never paediatric routine
- The tic-ADHD trap: stimulats do NOT generally worsen tics at the population level (the old dogma is dead; the modern evidence and consensus allow stimulant treatment in the ADHD+tics child with monitoring), a practice-changing point to state explicitly

**4, The Tourette's-comorbidity braiding:** the ADHD gets its full treatment (the school trajectory depends on it more than on the tics), the OCD gets ERP+/-SSRI, the rage-attacks get the behavioural-regulation module (the escalation-pattern work), and the family gets the long-horizon framing (the condition's arc is improvement; the treatment's job is to protect the school years and the self-esteem on the way through).

**5, School liaison (the quiet half of the whole note):** the teacher's letter with three instructions, ignore the tics, seat where least disruptive, exam-time awareness (suppression is fatiguing: extra time or reduced written load where the writing tics cost); plus the anti-bullying stance (the mimicking-classmate problem is the tic child's school-life's cruellest feature and the intervention is a class-discipline matter, not a therapy matter).

*India note (approx 2026): metro child-guidance/child-psychology CBT-CBIT ₹800–2,500/session; sertraline/fluoxetine ₹50–150/month; clonidine cheap; aripiprazole ₹150–600/month; the neurology referral for the atypical-movement suspicion (Sydenham's-reality India) before committing to the tic label; the faith-healer circuit: redirect families with respect ("the rituals you noticed are real, the mechanism is this, let us treat it the medical way and keep your faith practices for faith purposes").*

## 10. Indian practice

- **The "stop it" household.** The commonest Indian tic-management technique is suppression-demand, and it is exactly wrong: it spends the child's attention (the marks fall), raises the tension (the after-school storm), and teaches shame (the self-image scars). The clinic's single highest-yield intervention is converting the household to the ignore-protocol, one family meeting, one letter for the school.
- **The religious-frame family in OCD.** Scrupulosity and washing presentations arrive pre-interpreted through the spiritual lens, sometimes after years of temple-remedies. The clinical handle: full respect for the faith, precise separation of the mechanism ("your prayers are devotion; THIS loop is an illness that uses prayer as its grammar; we treat the loop, not the devotion"); the frame that keeps the family engaged rather than offended.
- **The erasing-child referral.** The Std 2–4 child sent for "sloppy handwriting and crying over notebooks" is a classic paediatric-OCD door; the paper that tears from repeated erasing is the exam-finding. Check the ritual inventory before the handwriting curriculum.
- **The ENT carousel (the throat-clear).** Persistent vocal tics tour ENTs for months (allergies, "tonsils") before anyone recognises the pattern; the premonitory-urge question ("does he feel something in the throat BEFORE the sound?") ends the carousel in one visit.
- **The family co-therapist economics.** Indian paediatric OCD outcomes live and die on the parent module: the family that weans reassurance and stops carrying the "contaminated" schoolbag is a treatment multiplier; the family that keeps answering on loop has hired the best therapist to lose. Budget the family sessions FIRST in the plan; they are the cheapest and highest-leverage hours.
- **The Sydenham's discipline.** In India, the sudden-onset movement child earns the cardiac examination and streptococcal workup before the tic-disorder label; rheumatic chorea is not rare here, and the diagnostic cost of missing it is the heart. One paediatric-cardiology linkage in your referral habit covers it.
- **The marriage-market forecast for Tourette's.** Parents of an 8-year-old ask about marriage; the honest answer is the prognosis's good news delivered without sugar-coating: most improve substantially by the late teens, the tics that remain are often mild, and the predictor of adult social outcome is not the tic count but the self-esteem kept intact through school, which is what the ignore-protocol and the anti-bullying stance are FOR.

## 11. Clinical cases (de-identified)

**Case 1: the girl whose family washed for her.** An 11-year-old Nagpur girl was brought for "hand-eczema and school refusal"; the dermatologist's biopsy was normal; the mother added, unprompted, "and she asks me fifty times a day whether the gas is off". The inventory (with the child alone, after the "everyone's brain makes weird thoughts" normalisation): contamination fears centred on "school dirt" (toilets, a particular classmate's desk), a magical rule ("if my shoes touch the bathroom floor, Amma must wash them or the house becomes dirty and Papa will have an accident"), checking loops on her school bag (re-packing 6–8 times), and a secret taboo layer she only disclosed under permission: intrusive images of hurting her baby cousin ("I must be evil; I sit far from her"): the most shameful thread and the reason she had stopped eating lunch at school (sitting near others "spreads the evil"). Family accommodation grid: the mother washed the shoes DAILY, answered the gas question each time, and had taken over the schoolbag-packing; the father drove to school to deliver the "correct" lunchbox twice weekly; the grandmother had identified the illness as a dosha and arranged two temple visits (no change, and the family's trust in that route was fading, the entry point for engagement). CY-BOCS-scored severe. Assessment: OCD, poor insight subtype in the magical layer, good insight in the contamination layer, with heavy family accommodation and secondary depression beginning. Management: the family module first (the accommodation-withdrawal ladder: the mother's shoe-washing moved from daily to "the OCD's trick we are starving; shoes stay", scripted non-answers to the gas question with sitting-near; the father's delivery route retired), the externalisation ("the Dirt-Witch") and the child's exposure ladder (the bathroom door handle with the full palm, the 30-minute no-wash rule, sitting one desk closer to the "contaminated" classmate per rung, and the cousin-proximity ladder with the psychoeducational frame that evil people do not fear their thoughts; the thought-fear was the symptom), brave-points economy with the cricket-star-charts her father ran (the retrained rescuer now the coach), ERP over 13 sessions with the school letter (toilet-time and lunch-seat accommodations during the early rungs, withdrawn as she climbed). Sertraline added at week 4 (the moderate-severe tier; slow titration to the OCD dose-band; the family warned about the 6–8-week clock and held the course through week 3's "nothing is happening"). Six months: CY-BOCS halved; lunch at school; the cousin-picture on her phone screen ("proof I sat beside her"). Teaching points: (1) the accommodation grid preceded the ladder; the family's retirement from the fix-outsource business was the treatment's front half; (2) the taboo layer surfaced ONLY under the normalising permission ("evil people do not fear their thoughts" is the sentence she reported remembering); (3) the sertraline clock was warned about BEFORE it was needed; (4) the dermatology route had cost a year; the fifty-times-a-day question was the diagnosis hiding in the mother's sentence.

**Case 2: the boy who was told to stop sniffing.** A 10-year-old Kolhapur boy arrived with his school's ultimatum ("the sound disturbs the class, final warning") and a father's log of punishments: the sniffing had been "corrected" with a ruler to the knuckles for two years, producing an elaborate concealment apparatus; he would leave the classroom to "drink water" 8 times a day, hold the sniff through assembly till tears stood in his eyes, and release the whole storm in the 4 p.m. school bus ("the driver says he goes mad on the way home"). The history assembled: onset age 6 (eye-blinks, dismissed as "TV eyes"), the migratory repertoire (blinks → throat-clear → sniff → a shoulder-jerk that arrived last term), the premonitory urge easily localised ("here, a tickle, like before a sneeze, and if I hold it, it screams"), waxing with exam terms (the teacher's chart showed the pattern), calming in absorbed cricket. No coprolalia (the family's feared question, "he will start swearing?": answered with the minority statistic). Comorbid screen: ADHD-inattentive positive (the marks decline, the daydreaming reports, the knuckle-punishments that read as "carelessness"), and an early erasing-exactness thread (the mild OCD cousin). Assessment: Tourette's syndrome (multiple motor + vocal tics, 4-year course, waxing-waning), with ADHD (inattentive) and subclinical OCD traits, the integrated circuit-family picture; no strep-era explosive onset (the PANDAS frame closed by timeline). Management: the psychoeducation package delivered to ALL THREE parties (the family: not naughtiness, the punishments retired with the ruler handed to the boy ceremonially, his idea, the therapist's blessing; the school: the ignore-protocol letter with the water-bottle permission formalised (the concealment cost retired), no suppression demands, the exam-time fatigue awareness; the boy himself: the loop explained and the prognosis honestly; "it gets better for most; your job is school and self-esteem, not silence"), CBIT-approximated in clinic (the awareness training, he learned to catch the tickle at 2/10 intensity; the competing response, slow double-breath through the nose against the sniff, practiced in escalating settings), the ADHD treated (atomoxetine, the family's stimulant-fear respected and the tics-not-worsened evidence shared; the marks recovering within a term), the father converted to the tic-mapping partner (the weekly chart that replaced the punishment log). Eight months: classroom sniffing reduced to occasional with the competing response deployed; the school ultimatum retired (the principal's letter, on file, apologising, the family framed it); the bus-storm down to short bursts. Teaching points: (1) the punishment apparatus had manufactured the concealment costs (the water-runs, the assembly tears) that read as the "disturbance" being treated; (2) the premonitory-urge question made the diagnosis in the waiting room; (3) treating the ADHD helped the SCHOOL trajectory more than any tic intervention; the comorbidity is the marks; (4) the stimulant-worsens-tics dogma is dead at the evidence level; state it and monitor; (5) the father's log was converted, not defeated, the same vigilance re-deployed as the waxing-wane map.

## 13. FAQ: asked the way parents ask it

**"He makes that sound on purpose to irritate us. He can control it; he stops when we glare."** He can SUPPRESS it, which is different from controlling it: holding a tic costs attention and builds a tension that bursts out later (the school bus). That is why the glare seems to work and the evening explodes. Suppression is a favour he does you at a price, and the price shows up at homework, marks and meltdowns. Ignoring is the counter-intuitive treatment that works.

**"Will he start swearing in public?"** Probably never. The swearing tic (coprolalia) is the movie version; it affects roughly one in ten at most. The real-life condition is blinks, sniffs, and head-jerks, invisible to strangers mostly, and improving by the late teens for the large majority.

**"Is it because we scolded her / the ghost / the dosha?"** None of these. It is a neurodevelopmental wiring pattern with a strong family fingerprint; ask the elder generation how many "nervous coughs" run in the family; the answer usually opens a wiser conversation. No ghost, no parenting fault, no curse, and punishing it works like punishing a stammer: it adds shame to neurology.

**"The doctor said OCD. But these are just her prayers, are you against religion?"** No, and the distinction matters: her DEVOTION is chosen, flexible, and peaceful; this LOOP is intrusive, rigid, distressing and ballooning. It uses prayer as its grammar the way another child's OCD uses hand-washing. We treat the loop; the faith stays exactly as your family holds it. (And where the loop attacks purity itself, treating it RESTORES peaceful worship, families of faith usually see this clearly once framed.)

**"How long will the tics last?"** Honest arc: worst around 10–12, then substantial improvement through the teens; roughly a third of Tourette's resolves fully and most of the rest becomes mild. What we protect during the years it runs: his marks (treat the ADHD if present), his hands (ignore the tic), and his self-esteem (stop the class-mimicking). The tics are the visible part; those three are the actual prognosis.

**"Are medicines needed for the tics?"** Usually not. Most children need understanding, a school letter, and time. Medicine enters when the tic itself costs (socially severe, physically painful, or school-destroying) and then we start with the gentler class (the blood-pressure-family drug, clonidine) and reserve the stronger ones for truly severe cases, at the lowest dose with reviews. The strongest "medicine" in most Indian homes is retiring the ruler.

**"Will the OCD go away on its own?"** Rarely fully, and untreated it tends to grow its architecture, the rituals recruit the family, the family feeds the machine. The good news is the opposite of waiting: this is one of child psychiatry's most treatable conditions; the exposure therapy works, the medicines work, the combination works best, and treatment early costs months while treatment late costs years.

**"We answer her questions to keep the peace. Is that wrong?"** It is loving, and it is the illness's supply line. Each answer teaches the question to return. The replacement is scripted kindness: "I can see the OCD is asking you again. I won't answer it, because that feeds it, but I will sit here with you while it shouts." Two weeks of that is harder and better than two years of answering.

**"His tics got worse after the fever, is that the PANDAS thing we read?"** Possibly related, flares with infections are well documented, and worth an evaluation when the onset was explosive (overnight symptoms, plus things like urinary frequency or eating changes after a sore throat). But most fever-flares are ordinary waxing. The strep question gets tested and discussed; the treatment either way is the standard one, and the exotic immune treatments are research-tier, not clinic-routine.

**"The school is threatening the child over the sounds. What do we do?"** The letter with three instructions: ignore (no "stop it", it costs him marks and buys you evening storms), seat (where it is least disruptive), and exam-awareness (suppression is tiring, some accommodation where the tics hit writing). Schools respond well to written medical instructions; the mimicking classmates are a discipline matter for the class, not a therapy matter for your child, and the school's protection there is part of the treatment.

## 14. Exam & student lens

**The criteria skeleton for viva:** OCD (obsessions/compulsions, time/impairment gate, insight specifier, frequently poor in children); tic gates (transient <1 year vs persistent 1+ year; Tourette's = multiple motor + 1+ vocal, 1+ year, onset in childhood); the comorbidity braid (Tourette's: ADHD >50%, OCD 30–50%).

**Mnemonics:** the tic identity, "USS" (Urge, Suppressible, Suggestible; the three findings that exclude mimics); the OCD child themes, "C-M-C-S-E-T" (Contamination, Magical, Checking, Symmetry/exactness, Scrupulosity, Taboo), or the Indian classroom pair: "the washer and the eraser".

**High-yield facts:**
- Transient tics: up to ~25% of children pass through one, the reassurance diagnosis
- Tourette's prognosis: peak 10–12, substantial teen improvement, ~a third full resolution; coprolalia minority (~10–15%)
- Premonitory urge: present in most, THE CBIT lever, the one-question diagnosis ("do you feel something before it comes?")
- POTS-tier: CBT+sertraline combination for moderate-severe paediatric OCD; ERP first-line all severities
- OCD SSRI dosing: higher and slower than depression; weeks 6–10; the week-3 abandonment trap
- Accommodation reduction = the family module that doubles outcomes
- Stimulants do NOT typically worsen tics (the dead dogma, modern consensus with monitoring)
- Alpha-2 agonists first-line for tics-especially-with-ADHD; dopamine blockers reserved-severe with the growth-era monitoring
- PANDAS: real-contested-honest-position; standard treatment; immune therapies are research-tier
- Sydenham's chorea: the Indian sudden-movement differential, cardiac examination before the tic label

**Classic traps:**
1. The scolding-suppression cycle (treating the tic as defiance, creates the concealment costs)
2. The ENT carousel for vocal tics (the premonitory-urge question ends it)
3. Missing the taboo layer (no permission-normalisation = no disclosure = no treatment of the most shameful symptoms)
4. The reassuring family (answering forever: the supply line never cut)
5. Missing ADHD under the tics (the marks fall and the tic gets blamed)
6. The week-3 SSRI abandonment (the clock never warned)
7. The erasing child sent for handwriting remediation (the OCD door missed)
8. Sydenham's mislabelled as tics in the post-strep movement child (the cardiac cost)

**Indian exam corner:** expected patterns, "a 10-year-old with hand-washing rituals and repeated questioning: diagnosis and management" (the family module + ERP ladder); "Tourette's syndrome: diagnostic criteria and treatment"; "PANDAS: the concept and current status"; "tic disorder vs chorea differentiation". Indian-context marks: the punish-the-tic household, the scrupulosity-religious frame handling, the Sydenham's discipline, the ENT-carousel ending, the accommodation-grid tool.

## 16. Self-test MCQs

**1. Tourette's syndrome requires:**
A. One motor tic and swearing
B. Multiple motor tics + at least one vocal tic, 1+ year, onset in childhood
C. Single vocal tic for 6 months
D. Coprolalia mandatory
**Answer: B.** The combination with the year gate; coprolalia stays a minority (~10–15%) cinematic myth.

**2. The finding that most identifies a movement as a tic rather than a mimic:**
A. It occurs in sleep
B. Premonitory urge + suppressibility + suggestibility
C. It worsens with relaxation
D. It is rhythmic and constant
**Answer: B.** The USS triad, and the urge is CBIT's lever.

**3. The first-line treatment for most children with mild tics:**
A. Risperidone
B. Psychoeducation + ignore-protocol + watchful review
C. Haloperidol
D. Deep brain stimulation
**Answer: B.** The reassurance-and-ignore package; medicine enters when the tic itself costs.

**4. In paediatric OCD, the family behaviour that most maintains the illness and is a first-order treatment target:**
A. School enrolment
B. Accommodation: reassurance, ritual assistance, trigger-removal
C. Prayer at home
D. Vitamin supplementation
**Answer: B.** The supply line; its weaning doubles downstream treatment effect.

**5. The POTS-tier evidence for moderate-severe paediatric OCD supports:**
A. Watchful waiting
B. CBT alone only
C. CBT + sertraline combination
D. Antipsychotic monotherapy
**Answer: C.** The combined ladder: ERP first-line all severities; combination for the moderate-severe tier.

**6. A child's tics exploded overnight after a sore throat, with urinary frequency and eating changes. The appropriate position:**
A. Dismiss the strep link entirely
B. Evaluate (strep testing, Sydenham's exclusion) + standard OCD/tic treatment; immune therapies remain research-tier
C. Start plasmapheresis
D. Long-term antibiotic prophylaxis routinely
**Answer: B.** The honest PANDAS position: neither dismissal nor the immune-therapy rabbit hole.

**7. Regarding stimulants in the child with tics + ADHD:**
A. Absolutely contraindicated
B. Modern evidence allows stimulant treatment with monitoring; the "stimulants worsen tics" dogma is dead
C. Stimulants cure tics
D. Tics always resolve with stimulants
**Answer: B.** The practice-changing consensus point: untreated ADHD costs the school trajectory more than the tics do.

**8. The CBIT core technique pair:**
A. Awareness training + competing response
B. Free association + dream analysis
C. Punishment + reinforcement
D. Hypnosis + sedation
**Answer: A.** Catch the urge early, deploy the incompatible action for a minute, the loop's off-switch.

**9. SSRI dosing in paediatric OCD compared to depression:**
A. Lower and faster
B. Higher and slower: weeks 6–10 to effect
C. Single fixed dose
D. Identical always
**Answer: B.** The OCD clock: warn families at the start or they abandon at week 3.
