# Nicotine dependence and tobacco cessation

## 1. In one line + overview

**Tagline:** Tobacco is India's **single largest preventable cause of death** (killing over a million Indians a year through the quiet routes of heart, stroke, cancer and lung) and it is also the addiction with the **cheapest, best-evidenced, most under-used treatment**: two questions, a quit date, and a few weeks of medicines that cost less than the cigarettes did.

**Overview, in plain words:** Nicotine reaches the brain in seconds and teaches the reward system one lesson with unusual efficiency: *that puff mattered*. Dependence builds within weeks to months, faster than most substances, and the withdrawal (irritability, craving, poor concentration, hunger) arrives within hours of the last dose, which is why the average unaided quit attempt fails within a week. India's tobacco is a **double epidemic**: smoked (bidis, cigarettes) and **smokeless** (gutka, khaini, paan with tobacco, zarda: India's distinct and enormous contribution to the world's tobacco problem), with over a quarter of adults using one or the other. Psychiatry's role is twofold: first, **tobacco is the invisible co-addiction inside every other addiction** (alcohol, opioid, cannabis and psychiatric patients smoke at double-to-triple general rates; treating them together is the rule); second, nicotine is also the **most quit-able** of the addictions with proper treatment: behavioural support doubles quit rates, **nicotine replacement therapy (NRT) doubles them again**, varenicline adds more, and the whole package in Indian generics costs less than the habit itself. The prescription that changes Indian health most costs five minutes: **Ask, Advise with the personal number, Agree a quit date, Assist with medicines and a plan, Arrange follow-up**; the structure every MBBS graduate can run.

## 2. Learning objectives

1. Quantify India's tobacco burden (smoked + smokeless) and the death attribution.
2. Describe nicotine's dependence profile: fast brain entry, rapid withdrawal onset, cue density (puffs per day).
3. Assess dependence in one minute (the two questions + first-morning cigarette) and severity guides (FTND-style, named).
4. Run the 5-A structure (Ask–Advise–Agree–Assist–Arrange) in a real OPD consultation.
5. Prescribe NRT (patch, gum, lozenge), **bupropion**, and **varenicline** with mechanisms, cautions and Indian costs; know cytisine.
6. Adapt cessation to the **smokeless tobacco user** (India's main problem) with the same architecture.
7. Integrate tobacco treatment into psychiatric care (the co-addiction rule; varenicline safety update; smoking and medicine interactions: the clozapine/olanzapine dose trap).
8. Prevent relapse with the dated-plateau counselling and weight-management script.

## 4. Epidemiology

### Global
- Over a **billion smokers** worldwide; tobacco kills **more than 8 million people a year** (including second-hand smoke attribution); the WHO-FCTC treaty architecture governs the policy world (taxation, pictorial warnings, plain packaging, cessation support).

### India
- The Global Adult Tobacco Survey (GATS) lineage: about **28% of adults use tobacco in some form**, roughly a fifth smoke (bidis + cigarettes, bidis dominating the poor and rural) and above a fifth use **smokeless** forms (gutka, khaini, paan-tobacco, zarda); hundreds of millions of users, with the world's largest smokeless-tobacco population.
- Deaths: **over 1 million Indians a year** attributable to tobacco; the largest single preventable cause; the disease map runs through oral cancer (the smokeless signature; India has the world's highest oral-cavity cancer burden), heart disease, stroke, COPD and tuberculosis worsening.
- Psychiatric comorbidity gradient: persons with schizophrenia, depression, other substance disorders smoke at 2–3× the general rate with heavier consumption and shorter lives; tobacco is a leading cause of the 10–20-year mortality gap in schizophrenia (see that note).

## 5. Causes & risk factors

- **Pharmacological speed:** arterial brain entry within seconds; the association between act and reward is instant and repeated **hundreds of times daily**: no other substance trains so densely.
- **Form factors:** bidi's high tar/nicotine delivery; smokeless products' ever-present availability (no smoke breaks needed, the office chew); new-generation products (vapes, heated tobacco, nicotine pouches) reaching youth with candy-flavoured on-ramps.
- **Psychological:** relief-function for stress, focus and mood (real short-term, borrowed); the ritual slotting (tea, toilet, travel, after meals, the cue architecture); weight-control motive, especially in young women.
- **Social:** household and peer modelling; occupational breaks (drivers, factory shifts); paan-shop ubiquity; celebratory and hospitality rituals; the misconceived "bidis are safer" belief.
- **Psychiatric comorbidity:** self-medication hypotheses (attention, mood, negative-symptom relief in schizophrenia) plus the industry's targeted history; treat the use as part of psychiatric care, not a parallel vice.

## 6. What happens in the body/brain

**Story 1: the two-second teacher.** Nicotine reaches the brain in about **ten seconds** of a puff, faster than intravenous for practical purposes, and flips the nicotinic receptors to release dopamine. The brain's learning rule is *simultaneity*: whatever fires with reward becomes wanted. With a smoker, that lesson repeats **200+ times a day**: every tea, every break, every phone call, every stress spike. No wonder the cue-web is denser than any other addiction's, and no wonder each individual cue is weak but the web is iron.

**Story 2: the thermostat in hours.** The receptors adapt fast (up-regulation), so the brain runs *through* the day's dose: within hours of the last bidi, withdrawal appears, irritability, craving, restless concentration, hunger. The tragedy of the unaided quitter: the worst week arrives immediately, while the health benefits are invisible, an awful incentive structure that pharmacotherapy (NRT/varenicline) simply deletes from the equation.

**Story 3: the medicine interaction nobody expects.** Tobacco **smoke** (not nicotine itself) induces the liver enzyme CYP1A2, which clears clozapine, olanzapine and theophylline-type drugs. A psychiatric patient who quits smoking can see **clozapine/olanzapine levels rise substantially** (sometimes 50%+ clinically meaningful rises), sedation, seizures (clozapine). The clinical rule: quitting is still right; **medicine levels get reviewed in the first weeks of abstinence**, one of the highest-yield safety pearls in psychiatric cessation.

## 7. Symptoms

**Dependence markers:** the first cigarette/chew within **30 minutes of waking** (the single best severity question); difficulty not using in restricted settings; withdrawal symptoms within hours; failed quits; use despite illness (the post-MI patient lighting up outside the CCU; the oral-cancer patient still chewing).

**Withdrawal (hours to weeks):** irritability, frustration, craving, poor concentration, insomnia-dream disturbances, increased appetite and **weight gain (~4–5 kg average over months, script this in advance)**, and the cue-driven craving waves that continue for months in weakening intensity.

**Harm inventory (India-weighted):** oral submucous fibrosis (the gutka signature: burning mouth, reduced mouth opening, **a pre-malignant lesion visible in any OPD**), leukoplakia, oral cancer; COPD and lung cancer; coronary disease and stroke; peripheral vascular disease; tuberculosis outcomes worsened; pregnancy harms (low birth weight, prematurity); gastric ulcers; diabetes control worsened.

## 8. Diagnosis

- **The two-question minute:** "Do you use tobacco?" (smoke/chew) and "How soon after waking?", within 30 minutes = high dependence.
- Severity instruments (named, not reproduced): the FTND (Fagerström-type test) and its smokeless adaptations; exhaled CO monitoring where available.
- **The comorbidity sweep:** every psychiatric and substance patient's tobacco status recorded at intake (the co-addiction rule).
- **Readiness assessment:** the three postures, precontemplation ("no problem"), contemplation ("later, doctor"), action ("I want to"), the 5-A structure routes each differently.
- **The quit-history:** previous attempts, what broke them (the cue-map), medicines tried, weight fear, the household's use environment.
- **Medical baseline where relevant:** mouth examination for every chewer (submucous fibrosis screening, early referral saves lives), cough/COPD screen, cardiovascular risk framing for the Advise step.

## 9. Management

### The consultation structure: the 5-As (run it in five minutes)
1. **Ask**, every patient, every contact, both forms.
2. **Advise** (personal, specific, brief: *"Your mouth fibrosis/your chest/your heart and this tobacco) quitting is the single best thing for it."* (Not "smoking is bad", the *their-body* version.)
3. **Agree**: a quit date within 2 weeks (a real date, chosen by the patient), and the household's role (paan-shop route change, tobacco-free home declaration).
4. **Assist**: medicines + behavioural plan (below).
5. **Arrange**: follow-up within the first week (the withdrawal peak), again at 4 weeks, then monthly; a named relapse plan ("call when the craving wins a day").

### Pharmacotherapy (double or triple the odds)
- **NRT, the backbone:** patch (steady background) + gum/lozenge (breakthrough cravings, the act-replacement); dosing by dependence (heavier users: higher patch strength); the "no smoking while patch" teaching; the chew technique (park-and-chew, not continuous chewing, the practical failure point); Indian generic NRT is affordable (approx ₹150–500/week for gum, similar for patches, 2026, variable by brand and supply, often cheaper than the habit it replaces).
- **Varenicline (the strongest single agent:** partial agonist at the α4β2 receptor) reduces craving and **blocks the reward of any lapse**; start a week before quit date; the nausea/insomnia counselling; the psychiatric-history conversation (the old black-box-era warning has been softened by subsequent trial evidence in psychiatric populations, quote the current evidence honestly, monitor mood anyway).
- **Bupropion:** noradrenergic-dopaminergic antidepressant with cessation evidence; **contraindications: seizure risk (epilepsy, eating disorders, abrupt alcohol/benzo withdrawal), and caution with hypertension**; useful where depression co-travels.
- **Cytisine** (the plant-derived varenicline cousin, ultra-cheap, used in Eastern Europe and increasingly studied elsewhere) an exam name and a hopeful Indian generic path.
- Combining patch + gum/lozenge outperforms single-form NRT; varenicline + patch combinations are used in resistant cases.
- **No e-cigarettes as prescription** in Indian practice (regulated out; effectiveness debated): the harm-reduction conversation, if it arises, is a specialised, honest one.

### The behavioural architecture (as in every KYP substance note)
- **Cue-mapping:** tea, toilet, travel, phone, after-meals, the paan-shop corner, the office break; each gets a written substitute (water bottle, walk loop, gum, breath drill).
- **The first week plan:** the withdrawal peak, remove tobacco from the house (all forms, including the guest paan box), change the morning sequence (shower-first reorders the first tobacco of the day), a week of high-density structure and support calls.
- **Relapse framing:** a lapse is a mapped event, not a failed identity; the dated-plateau script (craving waves weaken over weeks; weight stabilises by 6–12 months with activity).
- **The weight script, in advance:** gain ~4–5 kg is average and health-positive trade vs the tobacco; walk 30 minutes daily; the chew-replacement with sugar-free gum/makhana-type snacks, not sweets.

### The smokeless-tobacco adaptation (India's main event)
- Same architecture, different cues: the ever-present packet (pocket-environment change: water bottle, cardamom or fennel substitute), the after-meal slot, the workplace chew breaks; oral-examination follow-up for fibrosis/leukoplakia (ENT/dental referral for any lesion; early oral-cancer catch is the highest-stakes follow-up in Indian medicine).
- NRT gum works for chewers too (replaces both nicotine and the oral act).

### Psychiatric integration
- Treat tobacco **inside** the psychiatric plan: quit attempts succeed in psychiatric patients with the same tools, staged with clinical stability (not during an acute psychosis, for instance).
- **The CYP1A2 alert:** quitting smoking on clozapine/olanzapine → levels rise → sedation, seizure risk (clozapine) → **monitor and adjust in the first weeks**, say it in the file, not in hindsight.
- In other addictions: treat tobacco in parallel, not sequentially; the old "one thing at a time" folklore loses lives to the decade of tobacco deaths the other recovery preserves.

## 10. Indian practice

- **The policy frame:** COTPA (ban on smoking in public places, pictorial warnings, advertising ban), state-level gutka bans (patchy, replayed in courts, supply finds its way), GATS surveillance, the National Tobacco Control Programme (district-level cessation cells, patchy but the referral address), and the mCessation/NRT-scheduling realities.
- **The bidi problem:** the poorest smoke the most harmful product cheapest; cessation counselling and NRT affordability are equity work; cost-comparison counselling (₹/month habit vs ₹/month treatment) is a genuine motivator.
- **The paan culture door:** paan with tobacco is hospitality in much of India; the "tobacco-free home and function" declaration is the family-level intervention that works with the culture rather than against it.
- **The dental-OPD junction:** every dental/ENT visit is a cessation teachable moment for chewers; oral-lesion screening (submucous fibrosis: reduced mouth opening, burning) is a life-saving examination taught in five minutes.
- **Primary care role:** the 5-As, NRT prescribing, and the first-week phone follow-up; all district-doctor work; the mCessation helpline and NTCP cells are the referral layer.

## 11. Clinical cases

**Case 1: the driver with two habits and one plan.** A 48-year-old lorry driver: 20 bidis/day for 30 years + khaini 10×/day, presenting with morning cough and mouth-burning with reduced mouth opening. Mouth examination: early submucous fibrosis; ENT referral confirmed pre-malignant stage. The consultation: personal Advise (the mouth, the chest), quit date in 10 days, patch + gum NRT, cue-map (tea, after-meals, the highway paan-stops, packed substitute box, co-driver enlisted), wife's tobacco-free-home declaration, first-week phone calls. Lapse in week 3 (a breakdown night, mapped, survived, plan adjusted). At 6 months: tobacco-free, mouth-opening improved, weight +4 kg (scripted in advance, accepted), cough resolved.

*Teaching points: dual-form tobacco is the Indian norm; the oral lesion made the Advise personal; the cheapest treatment (generics) beat the habit's price; lapses mapped, not punished.*

**Case 2: the clozapine patient who quit.** A 32-year-old with schizophrenia, stable on clozapine 400 mg/day, quit smoking (20 cigarettes/day) after a chest infection, motivated by his brother's support; unbeknownst to his psychiatrist at the next visit, he was sedated and had one witnessed seizure; clozapine level had roughly doubled with the CYP1A2 loss. Re-titration under level-monitoring restored stability; the tobacco quit was retained with the prevention framing ("the quit was the right call, the medicine needed to learn about it").

*Teaching points: the smoking-cessation pharmacokinetic pearl; medicine-level review belongs in every psychiatric quit plan; tobacco is a live clinical variable in psychiatric prescribing, not background noise.*

## 13. FAQ

**"Are bidis safer than cigarettes?"**
No: bidis deliver comparable-or-worse tar and nicotine with worse outcomes in Indian cohorts; the smaller stick is not a smaller risk.

**"Is gutka/khaini really as bad as smoking?"**
For the mouth, it is worse: India's oral-cancer burden is the world's largest, and the mouth-burning/reduced-opening changes are visible years before cancer. For the heart and lungs, smokeless tobacco also raises risks. Different routes, same destination.

**"Can I just cut down instead of quitting?"**
Cutting down helps a little but usually slides back, and each remaining dose re-trains the cue-web. The health payoff is steeply at the *full* quit; the plan's job is making that survivable with medicines.

**"Nicotine in the patch, is it not the same poison?"**
Nicotine is the *dependence* chemical, but the tar, carbon monoxide and carcinogens are the killers. The patch gives steadier, lower nicotine with none of the smoke; it is the methadone logic done honestly, for weeks, then tapered.

**"Will I gain weight?"**
On average 4–5 kg over months, and the trade is strongly health-positive; with a 30-minute daily walk and prepared substitutes, most of it stabilises. Script this in advance: the unscripted kilo becomes the relapse excuse.

**"He has schizophrenia, is quitting too much for him to handle?"**
Not only manageable, but the single biggest life-extender available to him: tobacco is a leading cause of the early-death gap. The quit is planned around clinical stability, with the same medicines, and his clozapine/olanzapine dose reviewed as smoking stops.

**"What about vapes to quit?"**
E-cigarettes are not licensed cessation medicines in India (regulated out of sale); the evidence for quit-effectiveness is genuinely contested. The proven path is the patch/gum/varenicline package, cheaper and legal here.

**"The craving still comes months later. Is something wrong?"**
Nothing is wrong, cue-waves weaken over months but never fully vanish for some; each survived wave is weaker than the last. The plan's arithmetic is patience, not perfection.

## 14. Exam & student lens

- **Mnemonics:** the consultation as **5-As** (Ask–Advise–Agree–Assist–Arrange); dependence severity as **"30-minute question"**; medicines as **"Patch-Gum block it, Varenicline blocks + calms, Bupropion warns seizures"**.
- **Highest-yield facts:** >1 million Indian deaths/year; GATS ~28% adult use; the smokeless/oral-cancer signature; puff-count conditioning (200+/day); varenicline as the strongest single agent; bupropion seizure contraindications; the clozapine-CYP1A2 quit interaction; FTND named; GATS as the survey lineage.
- **Classic traps:** (1) advising "smoking is bad" generically instead of the personal version; (2) forgetting smokeless in "do you smoke?": ask both forms; (3) NRT dosed too low for bidi/heavy users; (4) the unreviewed clozapine level after a psychiatric quit; (5) treating tobacco as the trivial afterthought in other addiction care.
- **Viva favourites:** varenicline partial-agonist logic; combination NRT outperforming single; bupropion mechanisms and cautions; CYP1A2 induction pharmacology; oral submucous fibrosis as a visible pre-malignant lesion; COTPA provisions; psychiatric smoking-rate gradient.
- **Indian exam corner:** GATS numbers; bidi burden and equity; state gutka bans' patchiness; National Tobacco Control Programme cells; mCessation; the tobacco-free-home declaration as a family-level intervention; oral-cancer screening in every chewer's mouth examination.
- **Rehearse one line:** "Tobacco treatment is five minutes of structure plus a few weeks of medicines that cost less than the habit, the cheapest life-saving prescription in Indian medicine."

## 16. Self-test MCQs

**1. The best single severity question for nicotine dependence is:**
A. "Do you smoke indoors?"
B. "How soon after waking do you first use tobacco?"
C. "Which brand do you prefer?"
D. "How many friends smoke?"

**Answer: B.** Within 30 minutes of waking = high dependence (the FTND's most informative item's logic).

**2. The strongest single pharmacotherapy for cessation is:**
A. NRT gum alone
B. Varenicline
C. Benzodiazepine
D. SSRIs

**Answer: B.** Partial α4β2 agonism: craving reduction plus blockade of lapse-reward; NRT is the backbone, combinations are stronger than single forms.

**3. A stable clozapine patient stops smoking. The mandatory action is:**
A. Double the clozapine immediately
B. Anticipate rising clozapine levels: monitor and adjust in the first weeks
C. Ignore; nicotine is irrelevant to clozapine
D. Stop clozapine

**Answer: B.** Loss of CYP1A2 induction raises levels (sedation, seizure risk), the highest-yield safety pearl in psychiatric cessation.

**4. For a gutka user, the specific physical-examination priority is:**
A. Knee reflexes
B. Oral examination for submucous fibrosis/leukoplakia with ENT referral of lesions
C. Fundoscopy
D. Spine mobility

**Answer: B.** The visible pre-malignant signs (burning, reduced mouth opening, patches) make early referral life-saving, the chewer's follow-up centerpiece.

**5. Bupropion's classic contraindication set is:**
A. Asthma
B. Seizure risk (epilepsy, eating disorders, abrupt sedative withdrawal)
C. Migraine
D. Vitamin deficiency

**Answer: B.** The dose-dependent seizure threshold-lowering defines its prescribing caution profile.

**6. Average weight gain after cessation, to script in advance:**
A. 15–20 kg
B. About 4–5 kg over months: trade still strongly health-positive
C. No gain
D. 20 kg immediately

**Answer: B.** Pre-scripted counselling ("walk daily, substitutes ready") prevents the kilo from becoming the relapse excuse.
