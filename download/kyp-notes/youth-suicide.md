# Suicide & Self-Harm in Children and Adolescents

> **If you are reading this in worry about a young person right now:** call **Tele-MANAS 14416** (24×7, Government of India, free, 20 languages) or **CHILDLINE 1098** (for anyone under 18). If you are the young person yourself: you are reading a page written by people who have studied this pain for a century, and the single most repeated finding is that the urge passes; it is intense, and it is temporary. Talk to one adult today. That is the whole of step one.

## 1. In one line + overview

**Youth suicide is not a mystery of sad children (it is most often an impulsive act of a distressed, sometimes mentally ill, sometimes momentarily overwhelmed young brain with easy access to a lethal method, which is why the medicine that actually saves lives is unglamorous: asking directly, removing means, building the safety net around one named person, and treating the condition underneath) while self-harm (cutting, burning) is mostly a different thing, a private way of surviving unbearable feelings, and contempt for it is the clinician's error.**

India loses a student to suicide roughly every 40 minutes: NCRB's ADSI counts exceeded 13,000 student suicides in 2022 and have climbed each recent year; suicide ranks among the leading causes of death in adolescents and young adults nationwide. Yet the popular picture is wrong in both directions. Wrongly MYSTERIOUS: "no one could have known"; most young people telegraph distress (falling marks, sleep inversion, giving things away, dark talk online, a sudden calm after a storm) to someone who wasn't asking. Wrongly SIMPLE: "it's exam stress"; the exam is the trigger, not the gunpowder; beneath most attempts sit months of depression, abuse, humiliation, isolation, hopelessness, loaded chambers waiting for the moment. The adolescent brain adds its own physics: impulse control is the LAST circuit to mature (mid-20s), so youth suicidal acts are disproportionately STATE-dependent, minutes-to-hours of overwhelming urge rather than settled plans, which is exactly why MEANS MATTER: the pesticide in the shed, the rope, the unlocked medicine strip convert a passing storm into a death, and their absence lets the storm pass. The clinical craft: screen directly (asking does NOT plant the idea, the dead myth), build a written safety plan with the young person (named people, named red-flag signs, named actions), counsel families on means-restriction concretely, treat the underlying depression/anxiety/substance, and for self-harm, understand its job (affect regulation) and treat the pain, not punish the skin. India's law has caught up with compassion: attempting suicide is no longer a crime (Mental Healthcare Act 2017, presumed severe stress; care not custody), and the state now runs a national helpline spine. What remains missing is the will to ask.

## 4. Epidemiology

**Global.** Suicide is among the top three causes of death in 15–29-year-olds worldwide; rates vary enormously by country and means-availability (the world's sharpest natural experiments are means-based: the Sri Lankan pesticide story in the next section). Adolescent attempt-to-death ratios are high (many attempts per death, the young survive their storms more often than adults, which is precisely why means and the 20-minute window matter). Risk is not evenly distributed: LGBTQ+ adolescents carry several-fold elevated rates; Indigenous and marginalised youth likewise; abuse survivors and prior attempters are the two highest-risk clinical groups. Self-harm (NSSI) is far more prevalent than attempts; roughly 15–20% of adolescents report some self-injury history, girls' presentations dominating clinic routes.

**India.** The NCRB's Accidental Deaths and Suicides in India reports: student suicides above 13,000 in 2022 (a year-on-year climb across the recent decade, now roughly one student death every 40 minutes), with exam failure recorded among the leading stated causes (the stated cause is always the trigger-layer, not the load-layer). Suicide is a leading cause of death in Indian adolescents and young adults; the young share of India's suicide burden has grown as national rates rise past 1.7 lakh annually. The Indian method distribution, hanging commonest, poisoning second (pesticides historically prominent in rural youth), dictates the means-restriction counselling content. The pressure structures are ours alone: the board-result season (May–June's national weather), the coaching-city complex (isolation + weekly rankings + the "wasted year" fear), and the family investment-debt frame ("we sold the land for your fees"). And the protections are growing: the Mental Healthcare Act 2017 decriminalised attempt (presumption of severe stress), Tele-MANAS (14416) runs the national crisis spine, CHILDLINE (1098) covers minors, and gatekeeper programmes are being seeded in schools: the infrastructure exists; the asking is what must still spread.

## 5. Causes & risk factors

**Distal (the load, what fills the chamber):**
- Mental illness: depression (present in the large majority of attempters, often undiagnosed), anxiety, substance use (the disinhibition engine), emerging bipolar/psychosis (smaller but high-lethality share)
- Prior suicide attempt: the single strongest predictor of eventual death
- Chronic abuse (physical, sexual, emotional), neglect, family violence (POCSO surfaces in exactly these assessments, the reporting duty binds)
- Bullying and cyber-humiliation; chronic loneliness; marginalisation (caste, poverty, disability, identity: sexual/gender minority youth carry several-fold risk)
- Family history of suicide (the heritability is real and loaded); family emotional climate: high criticism, low warmth, achievement-conditional love
- Hopelessness (the cognitive variable that outranks sadness as a predictor); the perfectionist marks-identity fusion
- Prior NSSI (the repetition history), self-harm discovered-and-punished (adds shame to pain)

**Proximal (the spark, the moment's events):**
- Exam results (the Indian spike-season trigger), public humiliation, bullying incidents, relationship ruptures, online shaming
- Intoxication (the great disinhibitor: a large share of youth attempts occur under substance influence)
- Contagion: a peer's, celebrity's or online figure's death; media romanticisation; challenge-era online spirals
- Disclosure catastrophes: forced outing, abuse disclosure met with blame, the "what will people say" attack
- Access: the available method at the available hour, the physics of the moment

**The developmental physics (why youth acts differ):**
- Prefrontal maturation completes in the mid-20s; the adolescent engine (reward, emotion) runs ahead of its brakes, impulse, state-dependence, the minutes-scale storm
- Time horizon shrinkage: the adolescent present feels permanent ("it will ALWAYS be like this"); hopelessness is developmentally amplified
- Sleep loss and circadian chaos (the phone-inverted adolescent) degrade regulation further, the vortex from the Paediatric Mood note
- Romantic intensity + peer-identity stakes: the "social death" that adults discount is not discountable to the adolescent brain

**Indian context (the pressure architecture):**
- The marks-contract: love and worth experienced as conditional on performance
- The coaching-city isolation: distance from attachment figures at the exact age the brakes are thinnest
- The stigma-silence complex: distress not speakable at home ("we don't have such problems in our family")
- The access reality: agricultural pesticides in rural households, high terraces and ceiling fans, unlocked medicine strips; the audit list is short and literal

## 6. What happens in the body/brain (the mechanism stories)

**Story 1: the storm's 20 minutes.** The overwhelming majority of adolescent suicidal crises are minutes-to-hours long, not weeks. The brain's alarm (amygdala-driven) fires at full volume; the control circuitry that would say "wait, this is permanent" is under construction; the pain system (psychache: the felt unbearable-ness) crowds out everything else; and thinking narrows into the tunnel where only two options exist. In the tunnel, the method at hand decides the outcome; the person who must FIND a method is usually saved by the finding-time; the person with a method at arm's reach is not. This single mechanism is why three boring interventions carry life-saving evidence: means-restriction (make the finding slow), the safety plan (pre-install the tunnel-exits before the tunnel), and the connected person (a voice that penetrates the tunnel). It is also why "she was fine an hour later" is not evidence of drama; it is the storm's natural weather.

**Story 2: the load and the spark.** Think of a chamber loaded over months (depression's neurochemistry, abuse's sensitisation, hopelessness's learned narrowing) and a spark that would not fire in an unloaded chamber (the result, the message, the humiliation). Families afterwards fixate on the spark ("it was just 62%") because the spark is seeable; the clinician's job is to map the load, because the treatment treats the LOAD, and the next spark is never predictable. The exam is not the cause; it is the calendar.

**Story 3: the skin that speaks (NSSI's engine).** Cutting and burning, in the great majority of cases, are not dying-attempts but regulation-attempts: physical pain that is CONTROLLABLE and VISIBLE displacing emotional pain that is neither; the body's endorphin response supplies a real chemical relief; the marks are a private language when speech has been punished or shamed. The behaviour repeats precisely because the relief is real and fast, the same reinforcement curve as any compulsive relief-loop. The risk layers: accident-lethality, escalation, and the longitudinal elevation of later suicide risk (an outcome marker, not an intent marker). The treatment follows the mechanism: build better regulation (DBT-A's skill modules), remove the punishment-of-disclosure, treat the underlying pain, and never treat the skin with contempt ("she's doing it for attention"; for affect-REGULATION, actually, and attention is not a sin to a child who needs it).

**Story 4: the echo chamber (contagion).** Suicide behaviour is socially transmissible in the young more than in any other age group: a classmate's death, a celebrity's romanticised coverage, an online challenge spiral. The mechanism is identification + permission + script-provision; the vulnerability pool is the already-loaded (the distressed, the isolated, the prior-attempters). The counter-mechanisms are equally real: reporting discipline (no method, no location-detail, no romanticisation, help-line numbers in every story), postvention protocol in schools (identify and support the vulnerable, memorialise WITHOUT ritualising the method, no glorifying assemblies), and the frank classroom conversation that mourns the person while telling the truth about the act's physics (the storm passes; the dead don't get to see it pass).

## 7. Symptoms: what actually shows before the storm

**Speech and language (the most missed signs):** direct ("I want to die", "I won't be a problem much longer") is commoner than folklore suggests, the young TELL, to peers and online, before they tell adults; indirect: "what's the point", "you'd all be relieved", "I want to sleep and not wake", the dark-emoji/post era signals, goodbye-flavoured messages, will-flavoured statements ("my cycle goes to X").

**Behaviour:** giving away possessions; settling/sorting (the room cleaned, the phone wiped, the "final" posts); searching history later found (method-seeking online); sudden calm after a stormy period (the decision's peace, the sinister quiet); self-harm marks (concealed; the long sleeves in summer is the Indian clinic's tell); substance escalation; school refusal/marks collapse; withdrawal from the peer-friend; appetite and sleep inversion.

**The situation flags:** post-result weeks; post-humiliation; the returning-from-coaching-city withdrawal; the abuse-disclosure window; the anniversary cluster; the classmate's death (contagion's high-risk week).

**The clinical presentation layer:** adolescents arrive in three costumes, the somatic route (body pain, weakness, weight loss, the paediatric carousel again), the behavioural route (the "attitude problem", the school conflict), and the disciplinary route (runaway, self-harm discovered). All three deserve the screen.

**The screen itself (the questions, verbatim-worth):** In the past two weeks, have you felt life isn't worth living? Have you thought about ending your life? Have you thought about HOW? Do you have what you'd need? Have you done anything toward it? Have you ever tried before?; then the two that matter most for the plan: what stops you / who would you tell; and the means audit with the family. Asking does NOT plant: the randomised and survey evidence is consistent; screening reveals, does not create; the myth's persistence costs lives.

## 8. Diagnosis (the assessment architecture)

**The risk formulation (structured, not gestalt):** the C-SSRS (Columbia scale, named, the screen-to-depth ladder) or equivalent structured interview; the formulation braids: current ideation intensity/frequency/plan/means/intent; prior attempts (number, lethality, recency: the strongest single predictor); NSSI history; distal load (depression/anxiety/substance/abuse/bullying/identity stress); family history; protective factors (connected adults, treatment engagement, reasons-for-living, beliefs, including religious, and future-orientation); access audit; intoxication state now; and the developmental-physics judgement (the 14-year-old's state-dependence vs the 17-year-old's settled plan carry different weights).

**The tiering logic:**
- **High acuity**: intent + plan + means (or recent attempt, or attempt-discovery) → do not leave alone; means removed now; hospitalisation/admission decision with family; daily-to-alternate-day contact through the crisis weeks
- **Moderate**: ideation with plan-ness but no means/uncertain intent, or heavy load with recent crisis → safety plan + means-restriction + treating-the-condition + weekly review + the named-adult contract
- **Lower**: passive ideation, load present → the condition treated, the plan seeded, the follow-up held; the schedule is the safety net
- **NSSI without suicidal intent**: treat as its own condition (the DBT-A tier) while screening for the overlap at every review

**The mimics-and-comorbid screen:** depression (the majority; see the Paediatric Mood note's full ladder), intoxication states, emerging psychosis (the command-hallucination layer, high lethality, treat as high acuity), impulsivity disorders (ADHD/ODD), and the abuse screen MANDATORY in every case (POCSO surfaces here, the mandatory-reporting duty applies to the disclosed abuse of a minor; the survivor's confidentiality needs professional handling within that legal frame).

### Differential/adjacent table

| Presentation | What it is / what to do |
|---|---|
| Suicidal ideation, passive ("wish not to wake") | Real risk-layer, often depression's floor: treat the illness, seed the plan, follow |
| Suicidal ideation, active with plan/means | High acuity: the full architecture above |
| NSSI (cutting/burning for regulation) | Dying-intent absent: DBT-A-tier treatment, risk-screening maintained |
| Suicidal gesture / interrupted discovery | An attempt is an attempt, full assessment; "just a threat" is the deadliest sentence in the field |
| Self-harm secondary to abuse disclosure | Both layers treated: the abuse's protection-and-legal pathway, the pain's therapy |
| Accidental overdose in the impulsive teen | Treat as attempt-equivalent until assessed; intent can be undetermined, lethality doesn't care |
| Contagion-cluster presentations (the school's bad week) | Postvention protocol + the vulnerable-pool screening (below) |
| The "calm after the storm" presentation | The decision's quiet: the sinister remission; assess, never reassure-dismiss |

## 9. Management

**The frame: safety is built from four boring pillars, asked, means-removed, people-named, condition-treated. Heroics are for the movies; protocols are for the clinic.**

**1: The safety plan (the evidence-based core).** A written card, built WITH the young person, in their words: (a) my warning signs (the storm's first clouds; sleep inversion, the dark playlist, the marks falling); (b) what I do myself (the coping list; the run, the shower, the music, the breathing); (c) who I call, in order (the friend first for the young, then the adult; NAME them, NUMBER them); (d) what makes the home safe (the means list; see below); (e) the professional contacts (the clinic, Tele-MANAS 14416, 1098 for minors); (f) the reasons (their own words: the dog, the cousin, the unfinished thing). The plan is rehearsed in session (the tunnel-exit only works if pre-installed); the family holds a copy; it is reviewed at every contact. This structure (the Stanley-Brown Safety Planning Intervention lineage) has outcome evidence the no-suicide contract (the "promise you won't", zero protective evidence, false reassurance, banned from modern practice) never had. The one line to retire from Indian clinic culture: "sign here that you will not do anything".

**2: Means-restriction counselling, Indian edition (the mortality lever).** The family conference walks the house: agricultural pesticides (the shed's lock; the "buy small, store locked" rule; the Sri Lankan lesson, the national bans and reformulation cut suicide rates dramatically, the strongest means-evidence in world psychiatry), medicines (the week-of-quantities rule: no strip bottles, one week per family member, the grandmother's cardiac medicines are the commonest household pharmacy), ropes and ligature-access points in the depressive crisis weeks (the honest hard topic: the terrace, the bathroom door, supervision-level decisions during high-acuity weeks, not architecture forever), the alcohol-and-disinhibition audit, and the ONLINE means (the search-history conversation; the app-level filtering at home during crisis weeks). The framing sentence for families: "we are not treating your child as a criminal; we are adding friction at the tunnel's entrance because storms pass and doors must be slow to open."

**3, Treating the load (the condition-tier):**
- Depression: the Paediatric Mood note's ladder (safety-first, CBT/IPT-A ± fluoxetine-class with the monitoring discipline); treated depression is protective at population scale
- Anxiety, substance (the relevant notes), ADHD-impulsivity, emerging psychosis, each treated on its own evidence
- Abuse: protection (placement decisions), the legal pathway (POCSO reporting for minors, the mandatory duty), trauma therapy (TF-CBT tier)
- DBT-A (dialectical behaviour therapy, adolescent multigamily edition): the strongest evidence for the self-harming, emotion-impulsive adolescent (skills modules: distress tolerance, emotion regulation, walking-the-middle-path; the family in the room learning the same skills); the standard of care where the presentation is repetition-heavy NSSI + dysregulation; Indian availability is metro-concentrated; the honest approximation: a CBT-skills-plus-family programme with the DBT structure and the same targets

**4: The connectedness prescriptions (the unglamorous outcomes layer):** one named adult contracted for the crisis weeks (the "who does this child call at 11 p.m." question answered in writing); the school re-entry that avoids both spotlight and isolation; the peer-reconnection engineering (the one friendship that gets scheduled); the family communication rebuild (the validation-before-boundary script from the mood note; the "what will people say" reflex retired; the marks-contract renegotiated in a family session); brief contact interventions (the scheduled texts, the caring letters lineage, the Tele-MANAS callbacks), cheap and measurably protective in the post-discharge window (the highest-risk weeks in all of suicidology).

**5: The school programme (the public-health tier):** gatekeeper training for teachers (the 2-hour package: the signs, the ask-script, the refer-pathway); the results-season protocol (the counselling room staffed, the helpline cards distributed, the re-attempt-and-NIOS scripts in hand before the day); the anti-bullying enforcement (the single strongest modifiable school-side risk factor); postvention (after a student's death): the scripted whole-staff response (identify the vulnerable pool (close friends, prior attempters, the identified-with) and support them; the memorial without method-mention; the media discipline; the refusal of the "homage assembly" format that romanticises); WHO's guidance on responsible media (no method, no location, no front-page, no "everything was going well", always the helpline numbers); the Indian newsroom's violations are a standing public-health failure every result season.

**6: The law and the system (the Indian spine):** MHA 2017 s.115; attempting suicide is decriminalised; the person is presumed to be under severe stress and deserves care, not prosecution (the police-training reality is imperfect; the family's rights-script helps); admission to mental-health establishments under the Act's rights-protective structure (for minors: the nominated representative, the least-restrictive principle); Tele-MANAS 14416 (the national tier; the child-adjacent routing), CHILDLINE 1098 (the minors' spine), iCall and other crisis lines (the urban tier); POCSO's mandatory reporting when abuse is disclosed, the rare but binding conflict between a teenager's begged confidentiality and the law, best handled by telling the young person honestly what the duty is BEFORE the disclosure window and navigating protection WITH them.

**7, What does NOT work (the honest list):** no-suicide contracts (no evidence, retire them); "scaring straight" programmes and fear-assembly moralising (can INCREASE risk); punitive boarding-school transfers (the isolation amplifier); the "24-hour watch" as a substitute for a plan (vigilance decays by day 3, plans outlast eyeballs); and the moral hazard myth: "hospitalising her will make her think attention works": attention is the treatment for a child whose load is isolation; nobody withholds antibiotics because the infection "will learn".

*India note (approx 2026): the crisis consult (private) ₹800–2,500; child-guidance and medical-college psychiatry at nominal rates; Tele-MANAS and CHILDLINE free; DBT-A programmes metro-concentrated (the long-course cost honest-conversation: ₹30,000–1,00,000+ for full programmes, the skill-module approximation far cheaper); the SSRI tier ₹50–150/month. The recurring Indian clinic failure is not resource scarcity; it is the screen nobody ran at the paediatric or "attitude problem" visit.*

## 10. Indian practice

- **Results season is a national risk weather (May–June and March).** Clinics, counsellors and schools should enter it like a monsoon forecast: the pre-result letter home (the helpline card, the "whatever the result, we have a plan" script), the results-week staffing, the post-result week's vigilance (the delayed presentations, the girl who "coped" through the day and breaks on day 9), and the re-attempt architecture (supplementary exams, NIOS, the gap-year normalised as strategy, "the second attempt is a tradition, not a defeat").
- **The coaching-city contract (the Kota consultation).** The family's sunk-cost panic ("we sold the plot") collides with the child's isolation. The clinical session that saves: the family conference converting the contract ("the fees are recoverable, the child is not; an engineer who took a year exists; a dead child does not re-attempt"), the hostel-warden briefed as a monitor (the weekly well-check list), the parent-call protocol (frequency fixed, content non-performance; the "how did you sleep, what did you eat, who did you talk to" triad replacing "how was the test"), and the return-normalisation planned in advance (the trigger of shame removed by the pre-decided exit).
- **The "she is just threatening" trap.** Indian families bring the overdose or the cut with the appraisal "she is doing this to manipulate us". The clinical response that reframes a decade: an attempt is an attempt; threats that are dismissed escalate in lethality; and the manipulation-frame, even when partly true, is still a child using the only lever left; the response is to address the NEED, not the lever. There is no "call the bluff" in suicidology; bluffs are discovered at funerals.
- **The joint family's surveillance myth.** "There are eight adults at home, how could we miss it?" Because eight adults watching marks do not equal one adult asking feelings; surveillance is not the asking. The safety plan's named-adult contract cuts through: ONE person, named, with the script.
- **The faith and shame complex.** "Our family does not have such problems" and the religious-suicide taboo both delay disclosure; the clinic's stance: the taboo can stay (most faiths hold life sacred; ally with it), the shame must go ("this is a pain-condition, not a character-condition; it arrives in every kind of family, including families exactly like ours").
- **The pesticide conversation is the single highest-yield rural intervention.** The locked-shed-and-buy-small script, delivered at every rural visit involving a distressed adolescent, is the Indian version of the strongest means-evidence in world psychiatry. Teach it to every rural MBBS graduate and every agricultural-extension officer you can.
- **The media discipline is clinical work too.** The doctor quoted after a student's death shapes the next cluster: say the physics (storms pass; the load was treatable; help exists, 14416), never the method, never the "model student, everything going well" elegy. A two-line press discipline in every hospital's protocol.

## 11. Clinical cases (de-identified)

**Case 1: the 62% that didn't become a headline.** A 16-year-old Ahmedabad girl was brought semi-comatose from an evening medicine overdose, found by her mother an hour after the board result showed 62% ("the family's WhatsApp had already announced her as the topper-track"). She survived on supportive care. The assessment on day 4 (post-medical-clearance, with both parents): her load was two years deep, an undiagnosed depression (sleep inverted, the "wet rag" fatigue, the cricket dropped, the 20-mark fall read as "carelessness"), a marks-conditional family climate (the father's chart on the fridge tracking her percentages against the cousin's), a breakup she had been unable to speak of at home ("they would have stopped college"), the school's coaching-culture isolation, and the spark: the result, the WhatsApp, the evening alone in a home with a unlocked medicine cabinet holding the grandmother's full cardiac stock. Screened directly, she answered everything: two weeks of active ideation before the result, no specific plan ("it was always the result that would decide"), one prior episode of cutting at 14 (found, scolded, never assessed, the punished disclosure). Formulation: severe load, state-dependent act, means by opportunity, no plan-preparation (the storm's physics), protective factors: one cousin, one teacher, the dog. Management: the safety plan built with her, her words, on one card (warning signs: sleep inversion, the dark playlist, the "what's the point" thoughts; coping: the run, the shower, the cousin's number first; the means audit: the medicine cabinet re-keyed and re-dosed to week-quantities; Tele-MANAS and the clinic's evening number); the family conferences (three sessions): the marks-contract renegotiated, the fridge-chart ceremonially retired, the father's scripted first sentence converted ("the result is a number; you are not"), and the breakup-communication channel opened (not approval, acceptance of disclosure-ability); depression treated (CBT + fluoxetine with the weekly review discipline, the activation-watch taught); the school re-entry letter (the supplementary-exam route + NIOS as Plan B in writing, the counsellor contracted for the term); the cousin and teacher named and briefed with her consent; brief-contact layer: the scheduled Sunday texts, the day-9 and day-30 reviews (the delayed-collapse window). Six months: remitted depression, the supplementary exam taken "with a normal heartbeat", the cutting never repeated. The card stayed in her pencil box ("like a fire drill card; I have never needed the extinguisher"). Teaching points: (1) the 62% was the spark; the two-year load was the illness, and the load was treatable; (2) the medicine cabinet audit is the Indian household's highest-yield single act; (3) the punished cutting at 14 had been the field's first warning, missed; (4) the safety plan's named-people (cousin first, adult second) follows the adolescent's real trust-map; (5) the fridge-chart and the WhatsApp-announcement are the exam-culture's load-loaders; the family sessions treat them, not just the girl.

**Case 2: the school's worst fortnight (postvention done right).** A Std 12 boy in a Hyderabad school died by suicide on a Sunday night (method and details withheld by discipline here, as they should be everywhere). By Wednesday the school had three crisis presentations: two close friends not eating or sleeping, one girl who had exchanged "we should all go" messages with him a month earlier (the contagion pool), and a class-wide dread. The school's first instinct (assembled homage, photo-wall, the principal's grief-speech praising him as "the brightest star") was interrupted by the counsellor who had been gatekeeper-trained, and a postvention protocol ran instead: the vulnerable pool identified within 48 hours (friends, the message-exchanger, two prior self-harm cases known to the counsellor) and individually assessed; the girl with the exchanged messages assessed at the high-acuity tier (safety plan, family conference, treatment referral; her load had its own depression architecture); the memorial converted to service-format (a tree planted, the exam-stress counselling room named after no one, a help-seeking assembly that taught the physics honestly: "storms pass; the dead don't get to see it pass; 14416 exists"); the staff briefed on the reporting rules (no method, no note-romanticising, no "he had everything" narrative); a letter to all parents (the signs-list, the ask-script, the helpline numbers, the invitation to a briefing); the boys' hostel wardens (boarding school) briefed on the weekly well-check for the term; and the local press given the two-line physics statement instead of the story they wanted. No cluster occurred. The message-exchanger finished her year under treatment; one of the friends asked the counsellor the question that defines postvention done right: "if I ever feel like that, can I just come here?": the answer was yes, and that sentence is the programme's outcome measure. Teaching points: (1) the homage-assembly is the contagion multiplier, the service-and-honesty format memorialises without script-provision; (2) the vulnerable pool is identifiable in 48 hours if anyone has trained eyes; (3) the message-exchanger was the highest-risk student in the school and would have been missed without the protocol; (4) the parents' letter is prevention for the next school down the road; (5) the media discipline held because the school issued its two lines BEFORE the press wrote its own twenty.

## 13. FAQ: asked the way parents and teachers ask it

**"Won't asking about suicide put the idea in her head?"** No. This has been studied directly; screened young people show no increase in suicidal thoughts, and disclosing groups show RELIEF. The idea is almost always already there; the question only decides whether it travels to you or stays in the tunnel. The thing that plants danger is NOT asking.

**"He's just threatening to manipulate us. If we react, he'll learn it works."** An attempt is an attempt, and a threat is a warning; the deadliest sentence in this field is "just threatening". Even when there is manipulation in it, a child using the suicide-lever is a child who has run out of every other lever. Address the need underneath, always take it seriously, and let professionals judge the risk-layering; that is not indulgence, that is triage.

**"She cut herself, does that mean she wants to die?"** Usually not; most self-harm is a way of HANDLING unbearable feelings, not ending life: physical pain that is controllable replaces emotional pain that isn't. But it is never "just drama": it signals real distress, it repeats because it works, and over years it raises later suicide risk. It needs treatment (skills, therapy, the underlying pain addressed), not punishment of the skin or the "doing it for attention" dismissal.

**"What do we actually change in the house?"** Five things, this week: lock the pesticide shed and buy agricultural chemicals in small locked quantities; re-dose the household medicines to one-week strips in a locked box (the grandmother's cardiac stock is the usual find); remove or secure ropes and ligature items during the risk period; hold the alcohol audit (disinhibition is the spark's fuel); and agree who is the ONE adult the child calls at 11 p.m.: named, with the number saved in her phone. None of this treats the cause; all of it keeps the storm survivable.

**"Will she try again?"** The honest math: a prior attempt is the strongest predictor we have of a future one, which is precisely why the follow-up exists, the plan exists, the treating continues. Risk is highest in the first weeks-to-months after an attempt, then falls with time and treatment. The question that protects better than prediction is preparation: the plan card, the named person, the treated depression, the removed means.

**"The school is scared after what happened. What should they do?"** The sequence: identify and support the vulnerable pool (close friends, anyone with a history of self-harm or exchanged messages); memorialise through service, not homage, no assemblies romanticising him, no photo-walls, no "he had everything" speeches; tell the classes the honest physics (the storm passes, help exists, 14416) without method details; letter the parents with signs and scripts; and refuse the press the method-story. Handled this way, schools interrupt clusters; handled the other way, they create them.

**"We are a strong, good family. How did this happen here?"** It happens in every kind of family. Depression, abuse, pressure and pain do not check addresses or values, and the "our family doesn't have such problems" belief is itself a risk factor: it makes the young person's pain unspeakable at the one place it should be safest. The strongest families are the ones where the sentence "I'm not okay" is sayable at the dinner table.

**"Is it a crime in India to attempt suicide?"** No, since the Mental Healthcare Act 2017, attempting suicide is decriminalised; the law presumes the person was under severe stress and entitles them to care, not punishment. (If a police officer treats it otherwise, the family can invoke this; the Act's text is on their side.)

**"Won't hospitalising her make it worse / stigmatise her?"** Sometimes admission is the safest days of the whole treatment (active intent, means at home, the family unable to hold the plan), and modern Indian practice under MHA 2017 is rights-protective, least-restrictive and brief. Sometimes the better course is the intensive home package with daily contact. The decision is clinical, made with the family, and revisited; the error is not which option you choose, it is choosing neither and hoping.

**"What do we say to the relatives and neighbours?"** The smallest safe sentence: "she was unwell, she is in treatment, we are grateful for your support." The method, the drama and the details belong to the clinic, not the compound. Protect her return; the community's memory is short when the family's framing is calm.

## 14. Exam & student lens

**The architecture skeleton for viva:** the load-spark formulation (distal diathesis + proximal trigger + the impulsive-state physics of the adolescent brain); the screen ladder (ideation → plan → means → intent → history, asked directly); the intervention quartet (safety planning, means restriction, connectedness, treating the condition); NSSI as regulation-behaviour with risk-elevation; postvention and media discipline; MHA 2017 s.115.

**Mnemonic, "SAM-C":** Screen (ask directly), Access (means restriction), Map (the safety plan's people and signs), Condition (treat the load). Or the four boring pillars: "asked, means-removed, people-named, condition-treated".

**High-yield facts:**
- India: >13,000 student suicides (NCRB 2022), rising yearly; leading cause-of-death rank in young Indians
- Prior attempt = the single strongest predictor (every review, every exam)
- Asking does NOT plant ideation (the screened evidence, the viva trap)
- No-suicide contracts: zero protective evidence, retired, replaced by the Stanley-Brown-tier safety plan
- Means restriction: the Sri Lankan pesticide-ban story = the strongest single intervention evidence in suicide research
- NSSI prevalence in adolescents ~15–20%; function = affect regulation; DBT-A = the treatment tier
- The post-discharge/brief-contact window (weeks 1–12) = the highest-risk weeks, scheduled contacts measurably protect
- Contagion: youth > adults; media discipline (no method/location/romance; helpline numbers always)
- Adolescent acts: state-dependent, minutes-scale, means-at-hand; the physics that makes friction life-saving
- MHA 2017: decriminalisation + presumption of severe stress; Tele-MANAS 14416; CHILDLINE 1098

**Classic traps:**
1. "Just threatening": the dismissal that precedes funerals
2. The no-suicide contract as "the intervention" (medieval paperwork, modern theatre)
3. Screen skipped at the paediatric/"attitude problem" visit (the load found only at the attempt)
4. Means audit skipped ("there are adults at home"; surveillance is not friction)
5. NSSI treated as attention-drama (regulation mistreated as manipulation)
6. The homage-assembly postvention (the cluster's opening ceremony)
7. Media statement with method details from the treating team itself
8. The "calm after the storm" read as recovery (the decision's quiet)
9. The marks-contract left intact in the family sessions (the load's loading)
